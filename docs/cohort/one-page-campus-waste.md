# Campus Waste Last-Mile — one page

## Purpose → outcome

**Purpose:** make segregation actually count. Our campus already segregates — orgs like MEA sort plates, spoons, and food waste at every event — yet the sorted material likely gets recombined downstream because nobody owns what happens after the bin. We also plan events blind: three cans for two thousand people, cleaned up only after everyone leaves.

**Outcome:** by showcase day, an event organizer can open one site, get a waste estimate for their event, request deployable colored-bag stations, and see exactly which partner takes each stream afterward. The school keeps using it after YCLAP ends — that is the whole point.

## The idea

One system, three moving parts on top of Gargar's proven engine:

1. **Calculator** — tell us your attendance, sponsors, and venue; we estimate kilos per stream, bags, bins, and haul trips before the event happens.
2. **Colored-bag kit** — hangable bag stations on poles/signs where people actually eat and gather; easy to deploy, easy to restock.
3. **Partner map** — after segregation, where next? Each stream (PET, paper, food, packaging) routed to a real downstream taker, visible on the map.

Underneath it all, one study: what percentage of segregated campus waste survives to recycling today? That number is our baseline and our proof.

## What changes

| | Today | After |
|---|---|---|
| Event planning | guess, then clean up | estimate → deploy kits → log actuals |
| Segregated waste | recombined, landfilled | routed to partners, tracked in Gargar's ledger |
| Our project frame | SOM forest species loss (narrow, few users) | forest evidence stays as the story; product serves everyone who eats on campus |
| Gargar site | scrap-price tool for Pasig pilot | same engine, new front doors: calculator · kit · partner map |

**We ask the group to decide:** commit to this framing? Which parts first? Who owns AIS/CFMO coordination and partner hunting? And do we rewrite Gargar in place (recommended) rather than fork?

*Honest limits: open-field concerts may stay a mentality problem; no partners means no system; calculator starts rough until real audit data lands.*
