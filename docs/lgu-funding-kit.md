## 1. The map — three hops from project to money

A school climate project does not get funded directly. It becomes a **line item in an LGU plan**, that line item gets **tagged into the annual budget**, and only then can a **fund window** pay for it. Skip a hop and the money never moves.

| Hop | What lands where | Gate to pass | Source |
|-----|------------------|--------------|--------|
| **Hop 1** — project → LCCAP entry | Your project becomes a program-activity-project (PAP) inside the LGU's Local Climate Change Action Plan, with risk rationale, costing, responsible office, timeline | Climate rationale must trace to local risk data (CDRA); PAP must be costed with an office named; mainstreaming matrix entry into CLUP / CDP / AIP | `R17…md:41`–`:49`, `:65` |
| **Hop 2** — LCCAP entry → budget line | The PAP gets tagged via Climate Change Expenditure Tagging (CCET) into the Annual Investment Program, then appropriated by ordinance in the local budget | CCET tag + appropriation ordinance — *without* tagging and ordinance appropriation, the plan stays a shelf document | `R17…md:16`, `:23`, `:66`–`:67` |
| **Hop 3** — budget line → fund source | The costed, owned, tagged PAP is matched to a fund window: PSF, PDG, LGU own budget, or an external window R33 supports | Window-specific documents + call timing + review flow | `R16…md:72`–`:78`; `R33…md:82`, `:163` |

### What each hop actually requires

**Hop 1 requires the plan to be real, not compliance-grade.** An eLCCAP carries a local climate and disaster risk profile (CDRA preferred), sectoral vulnerability analysis, costed PAPs with timelines and responsible offices, a mainstreaming matrix into CLUP/CDP/LDRRMP/AIP, a financing strategy naming local budget and PSF among source options, and M&E indicators (`R17…md:43`–`:49`). The documented failure mode: plans written as one-shot compliance products with uncosted PAPs that never enter the AIP (`R17…md:55`–`:57`). Your job at this hop is to hand the planning office a **PAP-shaped package** so their entry is not another thin one.

**Hop 2 requires tagging plus ordinance.** Priority climate PAPs should enter the AIP via CCET under DBM–CCC–DILG JMC 2015-01 (`R17…md:23`, `:66`). Reality check: LGU-level expenditure-tagging compliance averaged ~18% (2016–2020) and slid further through 2023, and traced expenditure from tag to ordinance to Statement of Expenditure is weak nationally (`R17…md:36`, `:67`). So at this hop you ask the planner one concrete question: *which AIP year and which tag code does our line carry?*

**Hop 3 depends on which window.**

| Fund window | What it pays for | Who can knock | Hard requirement set | Source |
|-------------|------------------|---------------|----------------------|--------|
| **People's Survival Fund (PSF)** | Full adaptation projects (water/land management, infrastructure, ecosystem protection, early warning, capacity — statutory list `R16…md:16`); PHP 1B floor, suppletory to regular appropriations (`R16…md:14`–`:15`) | Each LGU or CCC-accredited local/community organization (`R16…md:13`) | 4 documents only: Letter of Intent · project proposal + Work and Financial Plan · adaptation reference (any of CRVA, CCA-DRR-enhanced CLUP/CDP, **LCCAP**) · Annual Investment Plan (`R16…md:74`); board approval → financing agreement → disbursement via DBP/BTr under COA rules (`R16…md:17`, `:78`) | R16 |
| **Project Development Grant (PDG)** | Proposal preparation only, cap PHP 2M per proponent | Municipalities of class 2 and below **only** — provinces, cities, barangays excluded (`R16…md:27`, `:96`) | PDG guidelines + same adaptation-reference logic | R16 |
| **LCE-led local budget** | Anything the LGU prioritizes — the pilot tranche, counterpart funds, staff time | The LGU itself | CCET tag + sanggunian appropriation ordinance (Hop 2 done properly) (`R17…md:66`) | R17 |
| **World Bank Pagkilos sub-grant** | Community-led resilience planning + subproject grants (flood/drought, NbS) across 500 municipalities in 49 provinces, USD 700M | Participating municipalities via DSWD community structures | LGU inside the covered municipality set; CDD process | `R33…md:82` |
| **GCF readiness / concept note** | Pipeline development first, projects years later (~USD 199M country total; RPSP3 readiness USD 6.78M) | DOF as NDA + accredited entity (Landbank/DBP are the direct-access entities) | Multi-year cycle — expect ~5 years concept-to-approval class friction (`R33…md:19`, `:43`, `:48`, `:162`) | R33 |
| **Bilateral TA (UNDP/Australia SHIELD caravan, GIZ-type)** | Capacity, proposal packaging, pilots — not big checks | Any LGU the partner programs reach | Partner-program windows | `R33…md:110`, `:118`; `R16…md:37`, `:82` |

## 2. Worked example — Gargar scrap value-recovery through every hop

**The project as it stands today:** Gargar is a Pasig pilot — reference ₱/kg scrap rates, calculator, field-verified collector directory, diversion log counting kilograms (`docs/campaign-canvas.md:43`; SMART goal ≥30 users / ≥100 kg by Sep 12, `docs/campaign-canvas.md:35`). Its honest climate stack: waste diversion as mitigation co-benefit, drainage protection as adaptation co-benefit, price transparency as justice for informal recovery workers (`docs/campaign-canvas.md:18`–`:19`; `docs/pitch-3min.md:33`).

**Step 0 — reframe as an adaptation line item.** Name it something an LGU planner can defend: *"Barangay-scale recyclable value-recovery network keeping recoverable waste out of the flood pathway."* The hazard linkage is measured, not vibes: 70% of NCR internal drainage silted with basura and other matter (DPWH 2024), MMDA hauls ~12,000 t/year from waterways and pump intakes, and post-Carina clearing pulled 1,513 t out of the network (`docs/research/wave3-agent/R09-basura-baha-evidence.md:120`–`:122`, safe-use table `:126`–`:130`). Claim exactly this: measurable kg diverted from the flood pathway — never "solves Metro floods" (`R09…md:137`).

**Hop 1 — land it in the Pasig LCCAP.** Pasig already has an adopted LCCAP on a 2016–2026 horizon plus an ICLEI-built community GHG inventory (~1.48 MtCO₂e, ~83% electricity) and the 422-action Promise of Pasig (`R17…md:83`–`:85`). The target document is the **LCCAP update/enhancement**, with mirror entries pushed through the mainstreaming matrix into the CDP and AIP (`R17…md:47`). Which office: City Planning and Development Office owns the plan/AIP mechanics; CENRO/SWMO owns solid waste; the LDRRMO owns the flood-risk framing; the sustainability/climate unit (Pasig has ICLEI/EcoPledge heritage, `R17…md:86`) is the natural champion. What attaches as evidence:

- Diversion-log export (kg diverted, dated) from the campus/barangay pilot (`docs/campaign-canvas.md:43`)
- Field-verified collector records — real shops, real rates, `is_verified` earned by contact (`docs/campaign-canvas.md:70`)
- Barangay drain-clog incident notes tied to haul dates (the CDRA-consistent story)
- Informal-recovery-worker count and rate-transparency benefit statement — the just-transition beat (`docs/research/wave3-agent/R05-informal-waste-worker.md:109`, `:117`)

This mirrors R16's own recommendation: student teams produce **barangay-to-municipal evidence packs** under-resourced planning offices can attach (`R16…md:135`), and R17's product wedge: tooling that outputs PSF-ready concept-note seeds from LCCAP PAPs (`R17…md:120`, `:175`).

**Hop 2 — tag and appropriate.** Ask the City Planning officer to carry the PAP through CCET into next AIP cycle (`R17…md:23`), sized honestly: collector coordination honoraria, drop-off logistics, log maintenance, monitoring kg — a low-millions-peso line. Then request the sanggunian appropriation for tranche 1. Traceability matters more than size: tag → ordinance → Statement of Expenditure is exactly the chain national reviewers find broken (`R17…md:67`, `:133`).

**Hop 3 — pick the fund window, honestly.**

- **Near-term, realistic (this is the answer for Pasig):** the LCE-led local budget line from Hop 2, optionally with SK/barangay counterpart. R16 is blunt: PSF awards cluster in highly exposed provincial LGUs and Metro Manila is a **secondary** PSF path — local funds are the realistic near-term route for NCR pilots (`R16…md:138`).
- **PSF full application — the long game, better aimed at a partner vulnerable municipality outside NCR:** assemble exactly the four documents (`R16…md:74`) — Letter of Intent signed by the LCE; project proposal + Work and Financial Plan (costed, phased, with the diversion-kg and drain-protection metrics); the LCCAP itself serves as adaptation reference (that is Hop 1 paying off); and the AIP showing the line exists (Hop 2 paying off). What the PSF Board actually does with it (`R16…md:78`): Secretariat screening → technical review and field appraisal (Secretariat, DBP, CCC, interagency) → CCC concurrence → **Board final approval** (DOF chair, `R16…md:17`) → financing agreement → disbursement via DBP/BTr under COA audit rules. What kills proposals at review: activities lacking clear adaptation linkage — the climate-rationale filter — and incomplete documents, the top rejection cause (`R16…md:90`); what lifts them: multi-hazard risk relevance, poverty incidence, community participation in design, cost-effectiveness, co-benefits, gender-responsive design, existence of local climate plans (`R16…md:80`) — note your evidence pack hits participation, co-benefits, and plan-existence simultaneously.
- **PDG:** skip for Pasig — cities are excluded; the window exists for municipalities of class 2 and below (`R16…md:96`). Flag it if the team ever partners with a low-income municipality.
- **External parallel tracks:** check whether the partner municipality sits inside the WB Pagkilos 500-municipality set for community sub-grants (`R33…md:82`); treat GCF and bilateral TA as multi-year packaging routes channeled via DOF/Landbank/partners, not near-term cash (`R33…md:162`, `:165`).

## 3. Student checklist — runnable without expert help

Plain language. Every box is a thing you can do in an afternoon or less.

**Find out (week 1)**

- [ ] Get the LGU's current plan stack: LCCAP, CLUP, CDP, latest AIP — request from City/Municipal Planning Office or download from the LGU site
- [ ] Search each document for your problem: is clogged-drainage/flood/waste already a stated priority?
- [ ] Find the name of one live champion: planning officer, CENRO/SWMO staff, LDRRMO staff, or climate-office staff

**Build the evidence pack (week 1–2)**

- [ ] Export your diversion log (kg + dates)
- [ ] Photograph every collector handoff; keep rate receipts from field-verified shops
- [ ] Write one page: hazard (clogged drains) → exposure (flood-prone puroks) → what our activity changes (kg kept out of waterways)
- [ ] Count beneficiaries (households, students, collectors reached)

**Enter the plan (week 2–4)**

- [ ] Draft the PAP slip: name, objective, activities, cost estimate, timeline, responsible office, indicator
- [ ] Hand it to the champion office; ask which plan update cycle it can ride (LCCAP enhancement, CDP, AIP)
- [ ] Ask the planner: which AIP year and which CCET tag code?
- [ ] Request a sanggunian resolution or appropriation line for tranche 1

**Match the fund window (with the planner)**

- [ ] Small pilot now → local budget line (this is the default win)
- [ ] Municipality class ≤2 → also file PDG (PHP 2M prep grant, `R16…md:27`)
- [ ] Full project later → PSF four-document set (`R16…md:74`): LOI · proposal + WFP · LCCAP/CRVA as adaptation reference · AIP
- [ ] Check call windows before writing anything long — PSF accepts mainly during opened calls, not rolling intake (`R16…md:91`)
- [ ] Partner municipality in the Pagkilos set → ask DSWD about community sub-grants (`R33…md:82`)

**Track (ongoing)**

- [ ] Log every submission date; approval ≠ cash — expect disbursement lag even after board approval (`R16…md:39`)

## 4. Honest limits — what this kit cannot promise

1. **No guaranteed funding anywhere in this kit.** The PSF floor is PHP 1B/year against historical aggregate asks above PHP 14B (`R16…md:18`), roughly ~1.2% of all LGUs had received awards by mid-2025 (`R16…md:35`), and there was a five-year approval drought 2019–2022 (`R16…md:28`). Even perfect paperwork competes in a needle's eye.
2. **Students cannot apply to PSF directly.** Eligible proponents are LGUs and CCC-accredited community organizations — and zero accredited community organization had received direct PSF project funding through mid-2024 (`R16…md:68`, `:95`). Every path here runs **through** an LGU office; without an office champion, nothing moves.
3. **Approval ≠ cash.** Disbursement lags approvals due to financing-agreement templates, ECC/CNC prerequisites, work-plan revisions, and document readiness (`R16…md:39`, `:92`). Budget the calendar for this, not just the pitch.
4. **Metro Manila is not the PSF heartland.** Awards historically favor exposed provincial LGUs; NCR pilots should lead with local budget and treat PSF as secondary (`R16…md:138`).
5. **Plan entry ≠ spending.** LGU expenditure-tagging compliance ran ~18% and slid (`R17…md:36`); there is no mature national scorecard of plan quality or delivery (`R17…md:35`), and election-cycle resets reshuffle offices and priorities (`R17…md:58`; `R16…md:93`). A line item can still stall.
6. **Do not overclaim in pitches.** Say *"we reduce the documentation friction that keeps LGUs out of the pipeline"* — never *"we will unlock the PSF"* (`R16…md:142`). The honest deliverable is the evidence pack, the PAP draft, and the champion relationship.
