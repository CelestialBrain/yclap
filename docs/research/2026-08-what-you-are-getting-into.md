## 1. Short answer

**Youth CLAP is a short CCC university accelerator, not a semester course and not a funded startup program.**

You will:

1. Sit modules on climate science, PH governance, project design, and mobilization  
2. Build a **Campaign Canvas** + a **demoable pilot slice**  
3. Pitch in about **3 minutes** at an **Innovation Showcase** (Sep 12, 2026)  
4. Optionally stay in a **Young Climate Innovators Circle** afterlife  

There is **no public archive of named Youth CLAP team projects from a prior finished cohort.** The clearest design document is the CCC **Terms of Reference** for the consultancy that runs the program. For “what good looks like,” use (a) that TOR, (b) CCC’s own youth recognition winners (Philippine Resilience Awards), and (c) PH / SEA climate showcase patterns.

## 2. What Youth CLAP is (and is not)

| Is | Is not |
|----|--------|
| CCC-owned program under ACCELERATE / Outcome 1.2.2 (youth accelerator) | An Ateneo-owned national program |
| ~5 weeks, multi-university Metro Manila | A full-year fellowship with stipend |
| Learning → design → pilot → pitch | VC diligence or Series A climate-tech |

| Afterlife: Young Climate Innovators Circle (when launched) | Automatic funding for your project |

**2025 design (TOR):** ~10 universities, ≥3 groups per school, ~4 students per group, Metro Manila focus; three F2F Saturdays + async + masterclass + showcase; ABC ~₱1.7M for the consulting package (program delivery cost, not prize money for teams).

**2026 Ateneo path (this cohort):** Aug 15 LEARN → Aug 22 BUILD → Aug 29 DEEPEN → Sep 5 PREPARE (async) → Sep 12 SHOWCASE. Mapúa Intramuros for several F2F sessions.

## 5. Comparable PH / SEA climate showcase patterns (what tends to win)

From SEA climate-tech showcases (CIIC, ClimateLaunchpad) and PH social-enterprise stages (Villgro Climate Action Showcase and peers), **winning themes** cluster as:

1. **Circular economy with a real diversion or offtake loop** (waste → value, kg, partners)  
2. **Nature / regenerative + livelihood** (ecology + income)  
3. **Energy transition with a pilot path** (harder for a 5-week student team unless scoped tiny)  
4. **Agri / food systems** that cut waste or emissions **and** help people eat or earn  
5. **Hyper-local adaptation hardware or protocol** (rain-catchers, early warning, heat routes, flood last-mile)  

**Villgro Climate Action Showcase 2026** (Makati, public coverage) featured enterprises such as regenerative cacao, mushroom livelihoods for displaced communities, gravity rain-catchers for floating communities, waste-to-materials, and energy-saving products. Same signal: **real product or install, named place, dual climate + people story.**

**Judge pillars (ClimateLaunchpad-style, maps well to student showcases):**

1. Innovation (mechanism, not slogan)  
2. Feasibility (can this team run it now?)  
3. Scalability (honest next step, not planetary fantasy)  
4. Climate impact (causal chain, not badge)  
5. **Clarity + proof under time pressure** (the student-showcase fifth pillar)

## 6. What you will actually be asked to produce

**Source of truth:** CCC *Youth CLAP Onboarding for Students (Batch 2)* slide, “Output / Description” table (2026).

| Output | Description (official) |
|--------|------------------------|
| **Project Prototype** | A concrete project plan addressing a climate problem in your school or community |
| **Project Pitch** | A 3-minute presentation delivered at the Innovation Showcase |
| **Project Summary** | A 1-page summary (problem, solution, activities, M&E plan) |
| **Resource Map** | A map of what you have vs. what you need |
| **M&E Plan** | A simple monitoring and evaluation plan with indicators and tools |

**On the project itself (cohort chat, not a theme constraint):** form is open. Examples given: AutoCAD if architecture, device, youth campaign if comms, website also fine. Groupings still TBD (intercampus or not).

Desk materials (Campaign Canvas, Gargar demo, etc.) are **how we fill those five boxes**, not a sixth official CCC output name.

## 7. Practical “what am I getting into” checklist

**Logistics (from 2026-08-08 Ateneo orientation):** early assembly at Areté, Mapúa registration, PE attire day one, school-activity rules, day-of contacts (Jack Rivera primary, Shen backup).

**Workload:** Saturdays are dense; midweek async exists; Sep 5 is build week; Sep 12 is fixed.

**Team model that works:** multi-lane (build / science / mobilize / story). One person cannot own demo + science QA + partners + pitch alone.

**Risk that kills showcases:** slideware with no pilot; fake kg; vague “platform for all youth”; credit products without methodology.

**What success looks like for this desk:** a mentor-safe story, a clickable or field-logged artifact, one named partner or place, and a team that still wants to run the pilot after Sep 12.
