# R06 — Metro Manila Scrap / Recyclable Material Price Dynamics (2024–2026)

**Scope:** PET, HDPE (sibak), aluminum cans / scrap aluminum, copper (tanso), cardboard (karton), steel (bakal / yero)  
**Geography:** Metro Manila primary; adjacent NCR–Rizal–Cavite–Batangas junkshop quotes used where MM-specific live lists are scarce  
**Window:** 2024–mid-2026 (with historical NSWMC / EMB reference for baseline)  
**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Agent:** Wave 3 research (R06)

---

## Key findings

### 1. There is no official, daily Metro Manila scrap rate board

- The only widely cited **government-adjacent** schedule remains the DENR–EMB / NSWMC *Price of Recyclables* table (historically based on EMB Central Office MRF sales). It is still linked from `nswmc.emb.gov.ph` and reprinted on secondary sites, but it is **not a live 2024–2026 index** and should be treated as a **baseline / educational** schedule, not current NCR street prices.
- **Live prices are informal:** individual junkshops, consolidators (“rekta” buyers), and scrap vloggers post lists on **Facebook groups, shop pages, TikTok, and YouTube**. Lists typically say *“subject to change without notice”* and are often morning snapshots.
- Implication for product design: any “rate” UI must be labeled **reference / last checked / shop-specific**, not as a guaranteed national price.

### 2. Sample rates (₱/kg) — synthesized ranges

Rates below are **indicative buy prices paid by junkshops / consolidators to walk-in sellers or collectors**, unless marked as factory/sell side. Cleanliness, color, caps/labels, volume, and location move prices sharply.

| Material | Street / household name | Indicative junkshop **buy** (recent window) | Notes / higher-tier |
|----------|-------------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------------------|
| **PET bottles** | Bote, PET mineral | **~₱8–16** clean clear; **~₱7–11** dirty/assorted; colored often lower (blue/green ~₱7–8 in some lists) | Clean white mineral historically NSWMC **₱16** junk / **₱20** factory; one documented junkshop **buy ₱8–11 → sell ₱28** to pelletizers (large spread) |
| **HDPE / hard plastic** | Sibak, sibakin (vinegar/soy bottles, basins, etc.) | **~₱10–15** | NSWMC baseline HDPE ~₱10 junk / ₱13 factory; secondary write-ups still quote ~₱15 |
| **Aluminum cans (UBC)** | Soft-drink cans; often confused with **lata** (tin) | **Highly variable; historically NSWMC ₱50 junk / ₱60 factory**; 2025–26 scrap-aluminum quotes for related grades often **~₱80–145** depending on form | **Do not use “lata ₱5–8” as aluminum-can price** — that is typically **tinplate / mixed cans**. Vlog/rekta quotes for aluminum **can/panta** and **spam/cast** grades have appeared near **~₱87–107**; structural aluminum (jalousie, heavy/lite) in Caloocan lists **₱105–145** (Mar–Jun 2026) |
| **Copper** | Tanso (Class A–D) | **~₱550–800 Class A** through H1 2026 (shop-dependent); B/C lower; D / coated much lower | Caloocan list: Mar 2026 **A ₱650 / B ₱610 / C ₱580**; Jun 2026 **A ₱740 / B ₱720 / C ₱680**. TikTok junkshop updates May–Jun 2026 show Class A roughly **₱711–793**, with multi-day moves of **tens of pesos**. Yellow copper (tanso dilaw / brass) **~₱340–440** in same lists |
| **Cardboard** | Karton, corrugated | **~₱2.50–5** (often ~₱3–4 household/junkshop) | NSWMC cartons ~₱2.50 junk / ₱3 factory; secondary still ~₱4. Thin volume; sensitive to paper mill / export OCC cycles |
| **Steel / iron** | Bakal solid, assorted, BI/tapalodo, yero | **Solid ~₱12–17; assorted ~₱11–12; BI ~₱9–11; yero ~₱5–8** (2026 MM consolidator lists near top of band) | Caloocan Mar→Jun 2026: solid **16→17**, assorted **11.5→12**, yero **7→8**. Older buy→sell example: bakal **₱12 buy → ₱15 sell**; yero **₱5 → ₱11–12** |

**Takeaway:** Between March and June 2026 alone, **copper Class A rose ~₱90/kg (~14%)** and **scrap aluminum grades ~₱25/kg (~20–24%)** at one NCR shop — while ferrous moved only **~₱0.5–1**. Metals track global markets; plastics/paper move more with resin mills, oil, and local offtake.

### 3. Junkshop vs collector split (value chain economics)

Typical Metro Manila recovery chain (informal + semi-formal):

```
Household / street residual
    → Waste picker / mambobote / itinerant buyer (kariton, pedicab, tricycle)
        → Small junkshop (cash buy, sort, store)
            → Larger consolidator / “rekta” / Chinese-owned big shop
                → Factory / pelletizer / smelter / exporter
```

| Layer | Role | Price position | Margin driver |
|-------|------|----------------|---------------|
| **Collector / picker** | Labor + first recovery; often no scale, no storage | **Lowest ₱/kg** received | Volume/day, mix quality, transport cost; income volatility = price × kilos |
| **Small junkshop** | Cash liquidity, grading, baling/sorting, neighborhood access | Buys low, sells mid | Spread often **~15–40%+** depending on material; absorbs price-drop risk while inventory sits |
| **Consolidator / factory gate** | Bulk offtake, trucking, quality specs | **Highest ₱/kg** paid to upstream | Specs (clean PET, Class A copper), locked volume, export LME |

**Documented spreads (illustrative, not universal):**

- Junkshop operator (Balita / Erwin Tambong, buy→resale to recyclers):  
  - PET mineral **buy ₱8–11 → sell ~₱28** (very large plastic processing spread)  
  - Lata **₱5 → ₱11–12**  
  - Bakal **₱12 → ₱15**  
  - Yero **₱5 → ₱11–12**
- NSWMC dual-column table (junk vs factory) shows systematic factory premium: e.g. clean PET **₱16 vs ₱20**, aluminum cans **₱50 vs ₱60**, copper Class A **₱300 vs ₱350** (historical levels; absolute pesos outdated, **structure** still valid).
- Academic / sector literature (2024 junkshop studies; informal-sector reviews) frames junkshops as **critical recovery actors** between pickers and industry; many remain semi-formal; monthly shop incomes vary widely with working capital.

**Justice angle:** Pickers capture labor value but not commodity upside; when copper spikes, consolidators and shops capture most of the mark-up unless pickers can **sort grade** and **time sales**. Opacity of daily rates is itself a bargaining asymmetry.

### 4. Sources of price volatility

| Driver | Materials most affected | Mechanism |
|--------|-------------------------|-----------|
| **LME / global copper & aluminum** | Copper, aluminum scrap/cans | PH scrap prices are local translations of world non-ferrous markets + importer bids |
| **China / Asia scrap demand & policy** | Copper, aluminum, some paper | Import enforcement, fraud crackdowns, mill downtime shift demand between scrap and refined metal |
| **PHP/USD and freight** | All exportable grades | Weaker peso can lift peso scrap quotes even if USD LME flat |
| **Oil & virgin resin prices** | PET, HDPE, mixed plastics | When virgin resin is cheap, recyclers bid less for scrap; when oil/resin tight, rPET/rHDPE demand firms (World Bank plastics circularity work flags this linkage) |
| **Local offtake capacity** | Plastics, cardboard | Few pelletizers/mills; plant maintenance, holidays, or working-capital freezes collapse walk-in prices overnight |
| **Quality grade** | All | Caps/labels on PET; Class A–D copper; clean vs dirty aluminum; wet cardboard — same “material” can differ **2×** |
| **Volume & logistics** | All | Free pickup only above thresholds (e.g. aluminum buyers advertising free pickup ≥100 kg); walk-in kilos get lower bids |
| **Weather / disasters** | Collection volumes more than unit price | Typhoons disrupt collection routes and warehouse drying; temporary supply gluts or shortages |
| **EPR / brand programs (emerging)** | PET, flexible packaging (select) | Can create parallel premium channels; risk of **bypassing** informal collectors if poorly designed |

**Empirical volatility signal (2026):** Copper Class A publicly quoted in the **high ₱600s to high ₱700s** within weeks (shop lists + TikTok “tanso price update” culture). Ferrous is stickier; cardboard/plastics less publicly high-frequency-quoted than metals.

### 6. Material-specific notes for MM product design

- **PET:** Highest household volume for campus pilots; price is **grade-sensitive** (clear mineral >> colored/dirty). Caps/labels and residual liquid cut kilos and ₱.
- **HDPE:** Often bulked as “sibak”; less glamorous than PET but stable industrial demand.
- **Aluminum cans:** Energy story is strong (recycling saves large fraction of primary energy); **nomenclature risk** — UI must separate **aluminum UBC** from **tin lata**.
- **Copper:** Highest ₱/kg; theft and wiring crime context exists; pilot should avoid encouraging strip theft — stick to **household appliance / campus e-waste** pathways with partners.
- **Cardboard:** Low ₱/kg but high campus volume (delivery boxes); good for **kg diversion** metrics even if ₱ story is small.
- **Steel:** Steady, low drama; useful ballast for mixed loads and junkshop relationships.

## YCLAP–Gargar implication

1. **Price transparency is the product wedge.** There is **no trusted public NCR rate list**. Gargar’s “your trash has a price” line is credible: households and students cannot price PET/Al/carton without calling shops or trusting opaque walk-in quotes.
2. **Never ship a single national ₱ as truth.** Show **range + as-of date + shop/source + grade notes** (clean PET vs dirty; Al can vs lata; copper class). Align with pilot patch: *spot-check 2 Pasig shops for PET/Al buy price*.
3. **Collector path > calculator alone.** Value accrues upstream; diversion fails if users cannot reach a **junkshop/MRF** that pays cash that day. Directory + verified hours matter as much as rates.
4. **Justice framing for mentors:** EcoWaste-aligned rates + collector path = **climate justice for informal recovery workers**, not just campus convenience. EPR conversations (stretch) should **include**, not replace, junkshops/pickers.
5. **Metric design:**  
   - **kg diverted** (PET + carton dominate volume)  
   - **₱ estimated** (use conservative mid of range; disclose volatility)  
   - Optional **avoided residual / basura→baha** narrative (not a carbon credit claim)
6. **Material priority for 5-week pilot:** PET + aluminum cans + cardboard (household-safe, high recognition). De-emphasize copper as core student KPI (value high, risk/optics higher).
7. **Data ops:** Treat FB/TikTok scrap lists as **sensors**, not sources of record — scrape/manual log with shop ID, timestamp, grade; refresh weekly for demo honesty.
8. **Do not** pitch plastic/carbon credits as the monetization layer (see carbon-markets research); scrap **cash markets already exist**.
