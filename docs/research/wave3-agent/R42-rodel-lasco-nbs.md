## Key findings

### 1. Who he is (and why it matters for mentors)

- **Dr. Rodel D. Lasco, PhD** — multi-decade Philippine scientist in natural resources, climate change, land degradation, and tropical forests. Author of **80+** technical publications; **IPCC** report author (shared in the 2007 Nobel Peace Prize recognition of the IPCC); member of the **National Academy of Science and Technology (NAST)** Philippines; affiliate professor at **UPLB**.
- **Institutional dual identity that defines his “science-to-action” brand:**
  - **Executive Director, Oscar M. Lopez (OML) Center** — private foundation founded 2012 (vision: harness science to produce knowledge that **leads to action** on climate adaptation and disaster risk management). Tagline language: *“Science for Climate Resilient Communities.”*
  - **ICRAF / World Agroforestry** — long tenure as Philippines Country Coordinator (~2004–2019) and senior scientist; core agenda **“trees on farms”** (agroforestry as adaptation *and* mitigation).
- He **pioneered Philippine research** on climate adaptation in the natural-resources sector and on the role of tropical forests in climate change; also led the Philippines sub-global component of the **Millennium Ecosystem Assessment**.
- For youth mentoring: he is a **credible local-global bridge** — IPCC-scale rigor + Philippine landscape reality + private-foundation knowledge products (PhilCCA, CDDJ, State of the Philippine Climate, Klima Film Festival, documentaries). Use him as a **model of evidence → communication → co-production**, not as a celebrity endorsement.

### 2. Core science-to-action thesis (the mentor spine)

Lasco’s repeated public thesis (WCRP SEA Forum 2021 presentation *“From Science to Action: Bridging Researchers and Practitioners”*; OML Center strategy; NbS talks):

1. **Climate research findings do not automatically reach users.** The gap is structural (format, language, incentives, timing), not just “more data.”
2. **Co-production and engagement with partners** (farmers, LGUs, private sector, youth, media) help bridge that gap.
3. **Openness to collaborate is essential** — science products must be designed *with* decision contexts, not only published *at* them.
4. **Actionable knowledge** means: decision-relevant, place-based, multi-hazard, and usable by non-academics — not only peer-reviewed novelty.

OML Center operationalizes this as a **knowledge-to-action pathway**: co-production framework + communications + partnerships so research products “lead to action.” Flagship products mentors can point youth toward:

| Product / pathway | Science-to-action role | Youth-project hook |
|-------------------|------------------------|--------------------|
| **PhilCCA** (Philippine Climate Change Assessment; IPCC-style national assessment with CCC/NPTE) | Localizes IPCC for PH; WG reports on physical science, impacts/VA, mitigation | Cite PhilCCA for problem framing; fill research gaps Lasco names (species, EbA, monitoring) |
| **State of the Philippine Climate** (with PAGASA) | Annual essential climate variables + extremes | Local baseline / “what is changing here” |
| **Climate Knowledge Portal / WebCCIM** | Provincial projections; water-resource impact tool | Site selection, water stress pitches |
| **CDDJ** (*Climate, Disaster and Development Journal*) | Open peer-reviewed venue for applied resilience research | Student papers; forestry/climate theses |
| **Mga Kwento ng Klima** + **Klima Film Festival** | Narrative + youth film as science communication | Storytelling, not just slides; youth as co-producers |
| **Resilience Grants** lineage | Fund applied tools (e.g. water impact models) | Grant-ready design: tool + user + decision |

**Closing line mentors can reuse (Lasco 2021):** co-production and collaboration bridge the science–user gap; pure publish-and-pray does not.

### 3. Nature-based solutions (NbS) — how Lasco frames them

From his OML-branded presentation *“Nature-based Solutions (NbS) for Climate Resilience”* (public PIDS/event deck, post-IPCC AR6):

**Urgency frame (IPCC AR6 WGII):** climate change is an unequivocal threat to human wellbeing and planetary health; the window for concerted action is brief and closing. Impacts hit **Indigenous Peoples, small-scale producers, and low-income households** hardest (food production shocks; cascading ecosystem-service loss). **Maladaptation** deepens inequity for the same groups.

**CRD (climate-resilient development):** pathways that integrate adaptation + mitigation + sustainable development; facilitated by partnerships with traditionally marginalized groups — **including youth**. Safeguarding **biodiversity and ecosystems** is fundamental to CRD.

**NbS as landscape portfolio (Global Commission on Adaptation logic he uses):** solutions stack across **mountains/forests/watersheds → rivers/wetlands → farmland → cities → coasts**:

| Landscape | Hazard example | NbS-style response Lasco highlights |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------------------------|
| Mountains, forests, watersheds | Wildfire, landslides, siltation, flood runoff | Forest management; protect/restore forests to stabilize soils and slow runoff |
| Rivers & wetlands | Flooding; intermittent flow | Restore wetlands; protect forests/watersheds to regulate flow |
| Farmland | Drought crop/livestock loss | **Agroforestry** — better soil moisture, less evaporation |
| Cities | Urban flood; heat islands | Restore watercourses, greenspace, porous surfaces |
| Coasts | SLR, erosion, storm surge | Restore coastal wetlands, mangroves, marshes, reefs (+ gray where needed) |

**Philippine economic valuation he cites (BMB / BIOFIN-linked, 2016):** ecosystem and biodiversity values **> PhP 2.3 trillion** (e.g. crop production, carbon offset, ecotourism, fishery, water provision, flood prevention). Point for youth: NbS is not “soft” charity — it is **national asset protection**.

**Mangrove flood-protection numbers he presents (Losada et al. 2017 / World Bank line):**

- Losing current mangroves → **~24% more people flooded annually** (~**+613,000** people).
- Property damages **+~28%** (to **> US$1B/year**); **~766 km** of roads flooded.
- **~US$3,200+/ha/year** direct flood-reduction benefit.

**Adaptation × mitigation co-benefits (his paired list for forests/trees):**

- *Adaptation:* more stable water flows; goods & services; biodiversity protection; income/livelihoods.
- *Mitigation:* carbon stock conservation; increased sequestration; reduced GHG emissions.

**Forest loss context he stresses:** century-scale Philippine forest cover collapse (classic Dolom/ESSC lineage: ~70% → ~18% over 1900–1999 in the maps he shows) — so restoration and **protection** are both climate and development policy.

**Take-home messages from the same NbS deck (mentor-usable):**

1. Natural ecosystems are **critical** to climate resilience of people — **especially IPs and other vulnerable groups**.
2. Support efforts to **conserve and manage wisely** natural resources.
3. **Science-based information** (PhilCCA) + **communication** (film festivals, media, education products) are part of the solution stack, not optional outreach.

### 4. Agroforestry & “trees on farms” (ICRAF signature)

High-citation Lasco line of work (e.g. Lasco, Delfino, Espaldon 2014 *WIREs Climate Change*; Lasco et al. 2014 *Current Opinion in Environmental Sustainability*):

- Smallholders are highly exposed to climate/weather risk; **trees and agroforestry** reduce exposure and enhance adaptive capacity at farm and landscape scales.
- Mechanisms he and co-authors emphasize: **crop and income diversification**; **soil and water conservation**; **efficient nutrient cycling**; buffering microclimates; co-benefit **carbon sequestration**.
- Entry points for scale: research + **policy** + dissemination; remove adoption barriers (tenure, markets, extension, risk).

**Mentor translation:** youth projects that only plant trees without a **livelihood or risk-buffer story** miss Lasco’s point. Prefer designs where trees **reduce a named climate risk** for a named user (drought, flood, income shock) and have a governance path (farm, PO, LGU, watershed).

### 5. Forest resilience actions he pushes to youth/professionals (2026)

At the **Philippine Forestry Youth Summit 2026** (UPLB; NbS & Climate Resilience session), Lasco (citing **PhilCCA 2024 WG2**):

- Forests are the **backbone of resilience** (carbon sinks, biodiversity, watersheds, livelihoods) but under rising climate pressure (temperature, rainfall shifts, stronger typhoons/droughts + socio-economic stress).
- Key actions: **ecosystem zoning & land-use planning**; **enforce** forest/protected-area policy; **integrated landscape management**; **vulnerability assessment**; **long-term ecosystem monitoring**; **research-guided** climate-responsive forest management; **restore** degraded/deforested areas.
- Critical **gaps** he named for students/researchers: climate impacts on key plant/animal species; forest condition under change; **ecosystem-based adaptation (EbA)**; institutional capacity of actors; **coordination** across institutions.
- OML also pushed **CDDJ** submissions — institutional habit: *publish applied research that feeds management*.

### 6. Communication as infrastructure (not afterthought)

Consistent Lasco/OML practice:

- **Beyond academia** (Howarth et al. 2020 frame he uses): climate science must travel.
- **Youth channels:** Klima Film Festival (multi-region youth org partnerships); forestry youth summits; PDYC 2026 (OML messaging: youth shape policy *and* community initiatives; evidence- and risk-based solutions).
- **Education products:** activity book with Adarna House (junior high CCA/DRM; DepEd-supplementary).
- **Private sector:** OML Legacy Lectures (climate resilience for business audiences).
- **Media:** climate dialogues; science–art co-creation; *Mga Kwento ng Klima* (scientists + non-experts for science-based storytelling).
- **2026 AI cautionary frame** (“Laban sa Agos” National Innovation Day): AI can strengthen adaptation (modelling, EWS, Earth observation, flood/heat forecasting, damage assessment) and mitigation (emissions monitoring, land-use, MRV/carbon markets) — **but** data-center energy/water footprint, digital divide, data access/security, and poorly governed uses can **increase risk**. Balance innovation with **responsible, inclusive** climate action.

### 7. What *not* to infer (boundaries for mentors)

- Lasco is **not** primarily a mass-planting cheerleader; his NbS frame prioritizes **protect/manage/restore ecosystems** with co-benefits, science-based planning, and equity — align youth tree projects with **site ecology, tenure, monitoring, and livelihoods**, not hectare theater.
- OML products are **knowledge infrastructure**; youth projects still need **local partners, M&E, and non-maladaptive design**.
- IPCC/PhilCCA citations support **problem and solution space**; they do not replace **site diagnostics** (flood profile, elevation, community consent).

## Mentor framing tips

Use Lasco as a **coaching persona** (“what would a science-to-action mentor ask?”), not as name-dropping.

### A. Five questions to put on every youth climate project (Lasco-compatible)

1. **Who is the user of this knowledge or intervention?** (farmer, barangay, school, LGU, informal settlement, PO) — not “the public.”
2. **What climate risk is reduced, for whom, and how will you know?** Prefer hazard + exposure + vulnerability language over vague “awareness.”
3. **Is this adaptation, mitigation, or both — and could it be maladaptive?** Check equity: does it help or burden IPs, youth without land, informal settlers, smallholders?
4. **What science product already exists locally?** (PhilCCA, PAGASA data, LGU LCCAP, watershed plan, mangrove maps) — build on it; don’t reinvent global IPCC slides.
5. **How will non-scientists use this?** Film, tool, map, demo plot, school module, LGU brief — co-produce with that audience.

### B. NbS coaching rules of thumb

| Encourage | Challenge / redirect |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Landscape thinking (ridge-to-reef or farm-to-city links) | Single-day planting drives with no survival plan |
| Agroforestry / livelihood diversification as adaptation | Carbon-credit cosplay without tenure or MRV |
| Protect + restore + manage sequence | “Plant anywhere” on mudflats/seagrass (ecological harm) |
| Co-benefits (water, food, heat, flood, income) | Mitigation-only numbers with no community benefit |
| Monitoring & vulnerability assessment | One-off photo ops |
| Youth as research + communication partners | Youth only as free labor for adult projects |

### C. Pitch / project narrative templates (mentor prompts)

- **Science-to-action spine:** *Evidence of local risk* → *user-centered intervention* → *co-produced with X* → *metric of use or risk reduction* → *next research gap or scale path*.
- **NbS spine:** *Named hazard on a named landscape* → *ecosystem function that buffers it* → *stewardship model* → *adaptation + mitigation co-benefit* → *equity check*.
- **Agroforestry spine:** *Smallholder climate risk* → *tree function (diversify / soil / water / income)* → *adoption barrier* → *how project removes one barrier*.

### D. Youth-role opportunities consistent with his public practice

- Fill **PhilCCA-named research gaps** (species climate sensitivity, EbA effectiveness, monitoring methods) at school/college scale.
- Submit applied work to **CDDJ** or present at forestry/climate youth summits.
- Use **film/story** (Klima-style) to translate local risk — science communication as climate action.
- Partner with **LGUs / POs / watershed managers** early (co-production), not only at demo day.
- Treat **AI tools** as optional amplifiers with footprint and inclusion ethics — not magic.

### E. Red flags (mentor intervene early)

- Global IPCC slides with **no Philippine or site layer**.
- “We’ll plant N trees” with **no survival, species, elevation, tenure, or 3-year M&E**.
- Claiming **carbon credits** without legal/tenure path.
- Adaptation that **displaces** risk onto poorer groups (maladaptation).
- Science communication that **scares without agency** (Lasco’s public posture remains hopeful *and* fact-facing — pair risk with doable action).
