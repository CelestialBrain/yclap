# R23 — Angat Dam & Metro Manila water security (2024–2026)

**Topic:** Reservoir levels, El Niño / Super El Niño stress, MWSS–NWRB–PAGASA statements, and hard limits for student projects  
**Window:** 2024 through early August 2026  
**Rule for YCLAP:** Document the crisis; do **not** claim to fix, refill, or replace Angat.

---

## Key findings

### 1. System dependence (still structural)

- Angat Dam (Norzagaray, Bulacan), via the **Angat–Ipo–La Mesa** system, still supplies on the order of **~90% of Metro Manila’s raw water**, plus irrigation for Bulacan / parts of Pampanga and hydropower to the Luzon grid.
- Concessionaires (Manila Water, Maynilad) have added **non-Angat sources** since the 2019 crisis (Laguna Lake treatment plants, weirs, and for the East Zone notably **Upper Wawa Dam** as a large buffer). That diversification improves **zone-level resilience** but does **not** remove Metro Manila’s system-level exposure when Angat is critically low.
- Reference elevations used in official and media reporting (approx.):
  - **Normal high:** ~210–212 m
  - **Minimum operating level:** **180 m**
  - **Critical level:** **160 m**
  - **Emergency / low-level bypass concern:** ~**150 m** (allocation could be cut sharply if operations shift to low-level outlet)

### 2. 2024–early 2026: “safe / comfortable,” then progressive drawdown

| Period | Snapshot | Signal |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Late 2023 – early 2024 | Levels often cited near or above ~200–210 m; PNA (Feb 2024) reported Angat ~**207.6 m**, “safely above critical” | Residual El Niño concern but **buffer still large** |
| Mar 2026 | MWSS: Angat ~**199.86 m**; manager Patrick Dizon said critical **160 m** not expected “as in 2010”; supply still described as sufficient | Official **reassurance** while summer heat rose and PAGASA flagged El Niño risk |
| May 2026 | Level fell **below 180 m** min operating (e.g. **178.21 m** reported when NWRB acted); first major allocation cuts of the season | Stress **operational**, not only seasonal noise |
| Late June 2026 | ~**163–160 m** band; PAGASA warned critical **160 m** possible by end-June; **30 Jun ~159.95 m** crossed critical | Turn from “watch” to **crisis framing** |
| July 2026 | Steep daily losses (often tens of cm/day); broke 2010 historical low (**~157.56 m**); **all-time lows ~150.55–150.91 m** (late July) | **Deepest Angat crisis on record** in modern reporting |
| Early Aug 2026 | Slight recovery with habagat / storm rains to mid-**155 m** range (e.g. ~**155.4 m** 1 Aug) — **still well below critical 160 m** | Relief is **partial**; buffer not restored |

**Takeaway:** 2024 looked manageable. **Mid–late 2026 is a structural water-security event**, not a one-week news spike.

### 3. El Niño / Super El Niño as the stress amplifier

- PAGASA and media framed **2026 Super El Niño** (onset mid-year; risk of persistence into **early 2027**) as driving below-normal rainfall over the Angat watershed even when other parts of Luzon flooded.
- Historical parallel: **2010 El Niño** low (~**157.5–157.8 m**) was long treated as the floor; **July 2026 broke that floor by several meters**.
- Forward risk (PAGASA / PIA, late July 2026):
  - Dam needs to approach **~210 m by year-end** to lower **2027 shortage** risk.
  - Mid-horizon projection cited: only ~**172.7 m by end-October** — still **below 180 m** min operating.
  - If recovery fails into a dry **2027**, Metro Manila faces a **multi-month allocation crisis**, not just summer rationing.

### 4. MWSS / NWRB / operator responses (what was said and done)

**Allocation cuts (NWRB → MWSS)**

- Progressive reductions in raw-water allocation as elevation fell (illustrative 2026 sequence from reporting): mid-year cuts from higher CMS rates down through **~52 → 50 → 48 → 46 cubic meters per second (CMS)** bands for multi-week windows.
- May 2026: ~**15%** raw-water allocation cut for MWSS after level dropped below min operating (Khaleej Times / NWRB framing).
- July 2026: further cut **48 → 46 CMS** (16–30 Jul) as elevation hit record lows (Philstar / NWRB ED Sevillo / Sevillano David Jr.).

**MWSS messaging arc**

- **Mar 2026:** Safe levels expected; critical 160 m “not seen” as likely path.
- **July 2026 crisis:** Advisories on adjustments (including to Bulacan); public conservation push; if elevation falls **below ~150 m**, allocation for Metro Manila could be reduced by **nearly one-third** as operations shift toward **low-level bypass** (PIA / MWSS).
- Contingency language across agencies: cloud-seeding over Angat watershed; Laguna Lake filtration / deep wells as augmentation; demand restraint.

**Concessionaire posture**

- Manila Water: public narrative of being **“better prepared than ever”** via diversified portfolio (Laguna Lake WTPs, Tayabasan/Calawis, **Upper Wawa** up to hundreds of MLD buffer, new WTPs coming online) — framed as reducing East Zone interruption risk vs 2010/2015/2019.
- Maynilad and others: conservation + operational balancing; low Angat still means **lower pressure / scheduled interruptions** risk for many areas even when “no total interruption” is claimed on a given day.

### 5. Secondary impacts (beyond household taps)

- **Irrigation:** NWRB / NIA cuts and farmer alarm when Angat is below critical — food-security and rural livelihood co-stress in Bulacan–Pampanga.
- **Hydropower:** Head constraint when elevation is far below normal high / min operating.
- **Watershed communities:** Dumagat and lakeside residents report exposed shorelines, stressed local water access — equity dimension of “Metro Manila’s dam.”
- **Small business:** laundry, refilling stations, filtration shops hit first by rotational supply and quality swings when pressure returns.

### 6. Student / YCLAP limits (do not overclaim)

What **cannot** honestly be a 5-week student “solution”:

| Out of scope | Why |
|--------------|-----|
| “Fix / refill Angat” | Multi-year hydrology + ENSO + national reservoir operations (NPC, NWRB, MWSS, NIA) |
| New large dam / interbasin transfer | Decade-scale infrastructure, billions, EIS, resettlement |
| Replacing Angat for Metro Manila | Utility-scale raw water is a state–concessionaire problem |
| Guaranteeing 24/7 citywide supply | Not controllable by campus or barangay project |

What **is** in scope (demand-side, awareness, last-mile, equity):

- Conservation campaigns with **measurable use reduction** (household, campus, barangay)
- Leak / NRW education partnerships (not claiming citywide NRW fix)
- Heat + water co-stress playbooks for schools / SK
- Community storage / hygiene protocols during **rotational supply**
- Policy literacy: who allocates CMS (NWRB), who treats/distributes (concessionaires), what 180/160/150 m mean
- Linking water stress to **waste clogging / flood** only where evidence-based — not as a substitute for dam hydrology

**One-line guardrail for pitches:** *YCLAP projects adapt communities to Angat risk; they do not solve Angat.*

---

## YCLAP implication

1. **Use Angat 2026 as context and urgency, not as the product.** Elevations near **150–155 m**, Super El Niño into **2027**, and official “need ~210 m by year-end” language are strong **problem narrative** for Metro Manila climate adaptation — especially water–food–heat packaging.
2. **Prefer demand-side and justice-side innovations** over supply cosplay: conservation with metrics, equitable interruption response, campus/LGU efficiency, youth communication of official level thresholds.
3. **Do not compete with MWSS / NWRB / Manila Water infrastructure story.** Upper Wawa and Laguna Lake diversification are **utility** moves; students should not invent fake “alternative dams.”
4. **Good demo hooks:** live PAGASA dam level → plain-language alert for barangay; water diary + % reduction; SK rotational-supply kit; school rainwater *non-potable* use only where code-compliant — always labeled as **offset / buffer**, never as “Metro Manila supply.”
5. **Cohort coaching line:** If a team’s thesis is “we secure Metro Manila water,” **redirect**. If thesis is “we help X households cut use Y% / cope with rationing under Angat stress,” **keep**.
