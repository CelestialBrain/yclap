# R45 — Junkshop Associations & Waste Picker Cooperatives (Philippines): Formalization, Successful Coops, Ethical Scrap Partnership for Gargar

**Agent:** YCLAP research (wave 3)  
**Scope:** Junkshop associations / waste picker cooperatives Philippines — formalization programs, successful coops, ethical scrap marketplace partnership model for Gargar  
**Geography:** National with Metro Manila / Pasig emphasis; Batangas, Nueva Ecija, Dumaguete as coop case sites  
**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Status:** Synthesis for Gargar ethical scrap + justice partnership design  
**Related:** R05 (IWW justice), R06 (scrap price dynamics), campaign canvas, pilot-7-day

## Key findings

### 1. The scrap value chain is informal at the base and consolidates upward

Typical Philippine recovery chain (Metro Manila and provincial towns share the same skeleton):

```
Household / street residual
  → Picker / mambobote / itinerant waste buyer (kariton, pedicab, tribike)
    → Small junkshop (cash buy, sort, store)
      → Larger consolidator / “rekta” buyer
        → Factory / pelletizer / smelter / exporter
          → (optional) EPR obliged enterprise / PRO recovery claim
```

- **Junkshops** are the neighborhood cash node: first formal-looking buyer for most household and street-recovered scrap. Many remain semi-formal (business permit uneven; no worker benefits).
- **Cooperatives and barangay associations** sit across levels: some are pure sorter/picker groups; some run community junkshops; some operate landfill-adjacent MRF-like facilities under LGU partnership.
- Value capture is asymmetric. Documented buy→resale spreads (e.g. PET mineral buy ₱8–11 → sell ~₱28 to pelletizers; ferrous smaller spreads) leave pickers with labor value and shops/consolidators with commodity upside (see R06). **Price opacity is a justice failure**, not only a UX problem.

### 2. Formalization is multi-layered — policy exists; delivery is uneven

| Layer | Instrument / actor | What it does for coops / junkshops / pickers |
|-------|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **National SWM law** | RA 9003 (2000) | Segregation, MRFs, LGU plans. **No full labor code for informal waste sector.** Unauthorized removal of recyclables can criminalize informal recovery when LGU collection is claimed. |
| **IWS framework** | NSWMC *National Framework Plan for the Informal Sector in Solid Waste Management* (~2009; still cited) | Long-standing national plan: integrate informal waste sector as **partner** of public/private SWM; skills, favorable policy, livelihood. Implementation LGU-dependent. |
| **LGU plan mandate** | NSWMC **Resolution No. 1655, s. 2024** | Requires LGUs to **integrate informal waste worker support programs into 10-year SWM plans** — institutional recognition lever for coops and junkshop clusters. |
| **EPR** | RA 11898 (2022); recovery targets ramp 20% (2023) → 80% (2028) | Obliged enterprises must recover packaging. DENR-EMB frames IWW/junkshops as backbone for EPR offtake. Risk: **PRO/corporate capture** of volume without fair offtake to informal base. |
| **DENR-EMB integration program (2024–25)** | Lectures for ~**200 IWWs** (NCR, R3, R4-A) on EPR/ESWM; **weighing-scale grants to 50 Quezon City junkshops**; training on organizational buildout, business planning, financial literacy; market linkage to obliged enterprises | Moves from rhetoric toward **enterprise formalization of junkshops** (scales = fair weight; training = coop readiness). |
| **Rights agenda** | Philippine National Waste Workers Alliance (**PNWWA**, Feb 2024: 12 orgs, 1,000+ members); **Magna Carta for Waste Workers** (SBN-2636 filed Apr 2024; refiled 20th Congress 2025) | Worker-authored demands: hazard pay, health insurance, job security, training, policy participation, right to organize, just compensation. DENR has welcomed formalization of rights/benefits. |
| **Coop law path** | Cooperative Development Authority (**CDA**) registration; pre-membership cooperative training (used in NSWMC/LGU pilot formalization of dumpsite recycler associations) | Converts associations into multipurpose coops with legal personality, dividends, loans, insurance pathways. |
| **Just transition evidence** | DOLE-ILS (2023) on circular-economy waste workers | Even “green”/zero-waste sites show **decent-work deficits** (OSH, insurance, contractual precarity) without social dialogue. |

**Formalization spectrum (practical, not legal taxonomy):**

1. **Recognition** — barangay/LGU ID, listing in 10-year SWM plan, junkshop business permit, sealed scales.  
2. **Organization** — association → CDA multipurpose cooperative; bylaws; elected officers; bookkeeping.  
3. **Economic upgrade** — bulk offtake contracts, fair-price boards, working capital, PPE, storage.  
4. **Social protection** — PhilHealth / accident insurance / sick leave / hazard pay (rare outside mature coops).  
5. **Policy voice** — seat in barangay ESWM committee, LGU SWM board, EPR dialogue, PNWWA.

Most PH groups sit at steps 1–2. **San Jose Sico-type coops** reach 3–4. **Gargar should not invent step 5** but should design partnerships so steps 1–3 become easier and transparent.

### 3. Successful / instructive Philippine models

#### A. San Jose Sico Landfill Multipurpose Cooperative (Batangas City) — flagship CDA coop

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Origin** | Small association of ~13 waste sorters → **CDA-registered multipurpose cooperative in 2003** with 55 founding members; LGU support via CENRO + Office of City Veterinarian and Agricultural Services (OCVAS). |
| **Scale (reported)** | UNEP (2025): ~**500 members** with regular income, accident insurance, paid sick leave. Earlier WWF ops snapshot: ~6 staff, 10 sorters, 17 drivers/helpers; ~**5 tons/day** plastics + food waste from ~48 establishments (mostly food chains). |
| **Activities** | Landfill-adjacent sorting; sell recyclables; convert plastics into hollow blocks; food waste → compost / black soldier fly fertilizer pathway. |
| **Member benefits** | Cash advances, loans, emergency funds, dividends; members report house-building and children’s education from scrap livelihood. |
| **Recognition** | Gawad Parangal “Most Outstanding Cooperative” (small-scale, 2017); special citation for environmental program. |
| **EPR stance** | GM Sherryl Hernandez: wants coops in the EPR chain for collection, MRF ops, and recycling partnership — blocked by **poor source segregation** even among businesses. |
| **Lesson for Gargar** | Long LGU partnership + CDA form + diversified livelihood (not only scrap spot sales) + **social protection as the definition of success**, not kg alone. |

#### B. Tagumpay 83Zero Waste Association (Manila, Barangay 830) — community association + junkshop

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Model** | Women-led network of street sweepers, drivers, creek rangers + **community junkshop**. Serves ~**5,700 residents** + ~24 nearby villages + 5 schools. |
| **Revenue** | Collect/buy recyclables (PET, hard plastics) → sell to recycling facilities; extra income for members. |
| **Support** | UN-Habitat training/funding; NGO millions of pesos for trucks, office, facilities. President Catherine Gabriel: association income alone insufficient for capital assets. |
| **Lesson for Gargar** | Barangay-scale **association-run junkshop** is a Metro-viable partner type (not only CDA coops). External capital still needed for trucks/space. Women leadership is common and should be default design assumption. |

#### C. Dumaguete women waste workers / Mother Earth Foundation pathway

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Model** | MEF zero-waste training (2018+) → women workers form group; door-to-door segregation for ~**400 households/day**; charge **₱50/household/month**; often self-finance PPE and bicycles. |
| **Friction** | Outside formal LGU authorization, workers pay **₱3/sack** to government; RA 9003 “unauthorized removal” tension. Aloja Santos (PNWWA head): excluded from EPR rulemaking; opacity of **plastic-credit** valuations. |
| **Lesson for Gargar** | Community fee + scrap sales hybrid can work, but **legal authorization** and **price transparency** are non-negotiable justice features. Do not romanticize underpaid “green” labor. |

#### D. Fair Circularity / ASKI living-income case (Talavera, Nueva Ecija, July 2024)

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Sample** | n=40 informal waste pickers; **40% cooperative members**, 60% independent. |
| **Earnings** | Full-time worker equivalent avg **₱84.53/hour** (coop **₱88.55** vs independent **₱81.84**); range ₱60.38–100.63. Plastics ~**25%** of revenue. All had vehicle access (*kolong-kolong*, tribike). |
| **Market power** | **72.5%** know material prices before selling; **30% locked to specific buyers** — buyer lock-in is a real constraint Gargar can help break. |
| **Living income** | Family of 4: **₱23,864/month** (~US$411 @ ₱58/$). Most still below living income; **90%** food-adequacy worry; **95%** cannot survive 1 week–1 month without revenue. |
| **Recommendations (study)** | Fair pricing mechanisms; support coops; PPE; register workers with IDs; LGU–coop partnerships; transparent pricing so pickers earn stable fair income. |
| **Lesson for Gargar** | Coop premium is real but **small** without price transparency and offtake. Product metrics should include **₱/kg to collector**, not only kg diverted. Provincial sample — do not paste hourly rates onto Metro Manila street markets. |

#### E. Plastic Bank (private collection-member model)

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Model** | Collectors receive **above-market premium** (cash and/or social benefits) for plastic; blockchain/traceability for brand offtake; PH among operating countries (with Indonesia, Haiti, Brazil, Egypt). Corporate partners (e.g. SC Johnson packaging recovery narratives). |
| **Coop angle** | Public materials describe PH as a site for **emerging cooperative model** for informal plastic collectors. |
| **Lesson for Gargar** | Premium + digital trail + brand offtake is a proven **private** pathway. Gargar can offer **transparency without claiming Plastic Bank-scale premium financing** unless a brand/EPR partner funds it. Avoid pure plastic-credit product framing (YCLAP non-claim). |

#### F. Ecosystem NGOs and practice networks

| Actor | Role |
|-------|------|
| **SWAPP** (Solid Waste Management Association of the Philippines) | NGO member of NSWMC; trainings, LGU capacity, research; historically linked to informal-sector mainstreaming discourse. |
| **Mother Earth Foundation (MEF)** | Zero-waste barangay 10-step model: ordinance, IEC, MRF, **recyclables sold to junkshops/accredited collectors**, residuals only to city. |
| **EcoWaste Coalition / GAIA Asia Pacific** | EPR inclusion advocacy; PNWWA ecosystem; justice framing for circularity. |
| **UN-Habitat** | Direct support to Manila community waste associations (Tagumpay-type). |
| **WWF-PH / SEA Circular (UNEP)** | EPR multi-stakeholder dialogues with landfill coops (San Jose Sico); human-rights approach in EPR toolkit. |

### 4. What “success” looks like (pattern language)

Across PH cases and global waste-picker cooperative literature (WIEGO, SWaCH/Pune comparison, ASMARE/Brazil lineage):

1. **Legal identity** (association or CDA coop) before big offtake contracts.  
2. **LGU partnership** (CENRO/ENRO, barangay ESWM, landfill/MRF access) — not only NGO project cycle.  
3. **Member economic services** (loans, advances, dividends) that smooth scrap-price volatility.  
4. **Diversified revenue** (collection fees + scrap sales + value-add like hollow blocks/compost) — pure spot scrap is fragile.  
5. **Social protection** (insurance, sick leave) as the mature-state marker.  
6. **Source-segregation demand** — coops repeatedly say business/household mixed waste is the #1 ops bottleneck.  
7. **Voice in EPR** — coops want collection/MRF roles; workers report exclusion from PRO design and credit valuation opacity.  
8. **Women in leadership** — Tagumpay, Dumaguete, many sorter lines; design must not default to male truck/hauler norms.

### 5. Risks and anti-patterns

| Risk | Why it matters for Gargar |
|------|---------------------------|
| **Extractive circularity** | Counting kg for a brand/campus while erasing ₱ to collector and worker name. |
| **Buyer lock-in** | App funnels supply to one junkshop that underpays; 30% already locked in Fair Circularity sample. |
| **EPR capture** | Obliged enterprises claim recovery without fair offtake to informal partners. |
| **Plastic-credit opacity** | Workers do not know credit market value of their plastic (PNWWA grievance). |
| **False “formalization”** | Registration without income, PPE, or voice — ILS finds decent-work deficits even in green sites. |
| **Child labor** | Family scrap romanticism is a red line. |
| **Unauthorized removal criminalization** | Partnerships need barangay/LGU blessing so collectors are not fined for doing the system’s work. |
| **Scale grants without organization** | Weighing scales help; without books, bylaws, and offtake, shops stay fragile. |

### 6. Formalization ladder usable in Pasig / Metro pilots

| Step | Actor | Action | Gargar role |
|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| 0 | Field team | Map 1 barangay cluster: pickers, 2–3 junkshops, 1 MRF | Directory + verification visits |
| 1 | Barangay / LGU | List workers; optional IDs; reference Res. 1655 in SWM plan language | Provide simple partner brief |
| 2 | Association | Elect officers; shared rate card; PPE pool | Publish rate card; log kg + ₱ |
| 3 | CDA path (optional, medium-term) | Pre-membership training → multipurpose coop | Do **not** run CDA process; connect to CDA-NCR / LGU coop office |
| 4 | Offtake | Bulk sale to consolidator or EPR-aligned buyer at transparent grade/price | Diversion log as offtake evidence |
| 5 | Protection | PhilHealth enrollment drive, accident micro-insurance | Metric: % partners with PPE + insurance referral |

## Gargar partnership model

### Design thesis

**Gargar is an ethical scrap marketplace interface**, not a hauler and not a carbon-credit product. It reduces information asymmetry among household/student → collector/junkshop → (later) consolidator/EPR offtake, while routing recognition and volume preference toward **organized junkshops and worker associations/coops**.

Aligned with campaign canvas: price transparency + clear path to a collector + diversion proof + justice beat. Aligned with R05: name the workforce; prefer cooperative paths; gender-aware; health non-optional.

### Partner types (priority order for pilot)

| Priority | Partner type | Why | Pilot ask |
|----------|--------------|-----|-----------|
| **P1** | Verified **junkshop** (ideally association-linked or open to fair-rate posting) | Cash offtake today; R06 rate truth | Post weekly reference rates; accept sorted pilot drop; log kg |
| **P2** | **Barangay / community waste association** (Tagumpay-type) | Organized labor + justice story | Directory listing; one collection window |
| **P3** | **LGU MRF** (Pasig multi-MRF context) | Formal handoff; RA 9003 alignment | Showcase trio with junkshop (see pilot-7-day) |
| **P4** | **CDA multipurpose waste coop** (San Jose Sico-type; may be outside Pasig) | Mature model for pitch narrative + future offtake | Learning partner / case study, not day-1 logistics |
| **P5** | **PNWWA-aligned group / EcoWaste / MEF** | Rights literacy; avoid extractive design | Feedback on rate UI and worker messaging |
| **P6** | EPR obliged enterprise / PRO (post-pilot only) | Scale offtake funding | Research conversation only until pilot ethics hold |

### Ethical marketplace rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Rates are reference, shop-dated, grade-specific** — never a national guaranteed price (R06). Label “last checked / subject to change.”  
2. **Collector/junkshop is named** in diversion log where consent given — no anonymous “system recovered X kg.”  
3. **₱/kg to primary collector** is a first-class metric alongside kg diverted.  
4. **No exclusive lock-in** by the app. If multiple shops listed, users can compare. Prefer shops that publish rates.  
5. **Organized partners preferred** when volume is similar: association/coop > lone shop that refuses rate transparency.  
6. **PPE and child-labor clause** in any written MoU: partner affirms no child labor; pilot may co-fund basic gloves/masks for window days.  
7. **No plastic-credit or carbon-credit claim** for pilot kg. Climate frame = landfill load + methane risk reduction + basura→baha co-benefit (R01).  
8. **Consent and safety** for photos/names of workers in showcase materials.

### Operating model (pilot → 90-day)

```
[User: student/household]
        | sees reference ₱/kg + nearest verified collector/shop
        v
[Gargar app: rate card + directory + diversion log]
        |
        +-- handoff window / drop point (campus or barangay)
        v
[Partner junkshop OR association junkshop OR MRF]
        | pays cash at posted grade; logs kg + ₱ (simple sheet or app)
        v
[Optional bulk offtake to consolidator / EPR channel]
        |
        v
[Pilot evidence: users, kg, ₱/kg, % volume via org partner, PPE coverage]
```

### MoU sketch (one page)

| Clause | Content |
|--------|---------|
| Parties | YCLAP–Gargar pilot team + named junkshop/association (+ barangay optional) |
| Term | e.g. 6 weeks pilot + 30-day afterlife |
| Rates | Partner updates Gargar contact with buy prices **weekly** for PET, HDPE/sibak, karton, aluminum cans, bakal (minimum set) |
| Acceptance | Partner accepts clean sorted materials during stated window/hours |
| Payment | Cash at posted rate; no deferred pay without written agreement |
| Data | Kg by material; ₱ paid; optional anonymized collector count. No sale of personal data |
| Ethics | No child labor; basic PPE encouraged; dispute path to barangay / EcoWaste mentor |
| Non-exclusivity | Either party may work with others |
| Public story | Joint credit in showcase; worker photos only with consent |
| Exit | Either party, 7 days notice; data export to partner |

### Metrics that match justice (not only climate)

| Metric | Target sense (pilot) | Justice reason |
|--------|----------------------|----------------|
| Users who priced material | ≥30 | Demand-side engagement |
| Diversion log entries | ≥30 | Proof of handoff |
| Kg diverted | ≥100 (or honest proxy) | Climate/landfill load |
| Collectors/shops verified in person | ≥3 | Anti-scrape fiction |
| **₱/kg median by material** | Track & publish | Fairness, not vanity kg |
| **% volume via association/coop** | Prefer rising | Organizing premium |
| PPE coverage on pilot window days | Track | Health |
| Women collectors/shop leads paid | Track | Gender |
| Child labor incidents | **Zero** | Hard gate |

### What Gargar should *not* try to be

- A **CDA registration bureau** or full coop formation NGO.  
- A **guaranteed living-income payer** without an EPR/brand subsidy (living income gap is large; Fair Circularity).  
- A **PRO substitute** under RA 11898.  
- A **plastic neutrality / credit marketplace** product.  
- A replacement for LGU collection under RA 9003.

### Narrative line for pitch

> Informal junkshops and waste worker associations already run the recovery economy. Gargar makes their rates and locations visible, logs honest diversion, and prefers organized partners — so climate kilos come with fairer pesos and named workers, not invisible labor.

### 90-day afterlife hooks (if pilot holds)

1. One **ongoing Pasig barangay + junkshop rate board** maintained with EcoWaste.  
2. Introduce partner to **Res. 1655** language for LGU 10-year plan (one-pager).  
3. If association wants CDA path: referral to CDA-NCR / LGU coop desk + pre-membership training sources — Gargar stays the marketplace layer.  
4. Optional conversation with DENR-EMB QC junkshop program alumni (scale + literacy) for Metro replication.  
5. EPR brand conversation **only** after rate transparency and worker consent norms are proven.
