# R17 — Local Climate Change Action Plans (LCCAP) Philippines: Quality, Coverage, Metro Manila Examples, Student/LGU Tooling (2015–2026)

**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Scope:** Legal mandate and mainstreaming timeline; national coverage/compliance (2015–2025); plan quality vs implementation gap; Metro Manila cases (Quezon City, Pasig, Marikina); CORE/SUC mentoring, NICCDIES tools, CCET; student and LGU tooling opportunities through 2026  
**Method:** NICCDIES/CCC official pages; DILG-LGA guidebooks (Books 1–4); Philstar/PNA compliance reporting; GIZ Urban-Act policy brief (Aug 2025); ICLEI ACP / Promise of Pasig; QC Enhanced LCCAP 2021–2050 materials; CCC CORE program; preventionweb 2019; academic implementation assessment (Misamis Oriental 2026); COA NCCAP performance audit framing

## Key findings

### 1. Legal mandate and what an LCCAP is

- **RA 9729 (Climate Change Act of 2009, as amended)** positions **LGUs as frontline agencies** in formulation, planning, and implementation of climate action plans consistent with the Local Government Code, the National Framework Strategy on Climate Change, and the **National Climate Change Action Plan (NCCAP) 2011–2028**. LGUs must furnish the Climate Change Commission (CCC) with adopted LCCAPs and subsequent amendments within one month of adoption (Sec. 14).
- **LCCAP definition (NICCDIES/CCC):** the action plan formulated by local governments to address climate change concerns; it covers **both adaptation and mitigation** and describes how the LGU will respond to climate impacts and **mainstream** them into land-use plans, sectoral development plans, and investment programs (AIP/CDP/CLUP).
- **DILG MC 2014-135** treated climate change adaptation as a regular city/municipal function, with provinces providing technical assistance, enforcement, and information management. Later, **DILG MC 2021-068** re-enjoined submission of LCCAPs to CCC and is widely cited as the compliance trigger that drove a second submission wave.
- **Enhanced LCCAP (eLCCAP)** generation (Books 3–4, 2017 onward) adds science-based requirements: **Climate and Disaster Risk Assessment (CDRA)**, community-level **GHG inventory**, low-emission development strategies, and **Climate Change Expenditure Tagging (CCET)** linkage so priority actions enter the Annual Investment Program (AIP).

### 3. Coverage: from sparse to near-universal submission

- **Explosive scale-up:** ~137 LCCAPs in 2015 → ~1,073 by 2019 (~8×) → ~1,472–1,474 by mid/late 2023 (~85–86% of 1,715 LGUs) → ~1,558 (~91%) by mid-2025 in secondary official reporting. CCC publicly targeted **100% compliance by FY 2024**; residual non-submitters remain a small tail of capacity-constrained or politically disrupted LGUs.
- **Submission ≠ quality or implementation.** CCC and DILG count **formulation/submission** on NICCDIES and MC compliance dashboards. There is **no mature, published national scorecard of plan quality, budget linkage, or outcome delivery** for LCCAPs as a class. COA’s 2024 performance audit framing of the NCCAP noted **no formal evaluation mechanisms for LCCAPs** and incomplete national results-based M&E institutionalization.
- **CCET compliance lags plan existence:** GIZ Urban-Act (2025) reports LGU **LCCET compliance averaged ~18% (2016–2020)** and **slid further through 2023** (single-digit to low-teen % of expected submissions in later years), while national NCCET compliance rose sharply post-2021. Plans exist; **tagged investment and expenditure tracing do not scale with them**.
- **Geographic unevenness:** Early CORE waves prioritized Yolanda corridor, coastal municipalities, and IP/Bangsamoro communities. Metro Manila HUCs generally have plans (often enhanced by C40, ICLEI, bilateral city-to-city programs), while 4th–6th class municipalities more often hold **compliance-grade** documents with weak data and thin implementation annexes.

### 4. Quality architecture: what “good” is supposed to look like

**Guidebook-prescribed content (Books 1–4 / eLCCAP):**

1. Local climate and disaster risk profile (hazard, exposure, sensitivity, adaptive capacity; CDRA preferred post-2017)  
2. Sectoral vulnerability analysis (food, water, health, human security, ecosystems, energy, industry/services — aligned to NCCAP themes)  
3. GHG inventory (community-level; stationary energy, transport, waste, wastewater) for mitigation pathways  
4. Adaptation and mitigation programs, activities, and projects (PAPs) with timelines, responsible offices, and rough costing  
5. Mainstreaming matrix into CLUP, CDP, LDRRMP, AIP, and gender/social inclusion  
6. Financing strategy (local budget, NTA, PSF, ODA, PPP, private pledges)  
7. M&E framework with indicators and review cycle  

**Quality Assurance (QA) system:** CCC + DILG-LGA developed an **LCCAP Quality Assurance Review Toolkit** (instrument circulated ~2018; JMC to operationalize provincial/city review committees). Intent: feedback on content/form/process, highlight above-minimum practice, recommend implementation improvements. As of 2025–2026, QA remains **under-institutionalized at scale** relative to the volume of submitted plans — capacity bottleneck at provincial review bodies and HEI coaches.

**Documented quality deficits (cross-source pattern):**

- Plans written as **one-shot compliance products**, not living investment pipelines  
- **Weak or outdated CDRA/GHG baselines** (especially non-metro LGUs without university partners)  
- **PAPs not costed or not tagged into AIP** → disconnect between plan language and annual budget  
- **Election-cycle reset** (3-year LCE term) favors short-horizon PPAs over multi-year resilience infrastructure  
- **Mis-tagging** of geologic-hazard DRR as climate expenditure  
- **No standardized outcome M&E** — progress reported as “plan formulated” rather than reduced exposure, avoided emissions, or lives/assets protected  
- GIZ 2025 dialogue finding: many LGUs are **driven by LCCET compliance more than mainstreaming** climate into planning–programming–budgeting–expenditure management  

### 5. Implementation gap (plan → budget → delivery → outcomes)

- **PSF linkage:** LCCAP (or CRVA / CCA-enhanced CLUP/CDP) is a **documentary prerequisite** for People’s Survival Fund proposals (see R16). Weak LCCAPs therefore block climate finance access for the LGUs that need it most.  
- **Budget path:** Priority climate PAPs should enter the **AIP** via CCET (JMC 2015-01). Without tagging and ordinance appropriation, LCCAPs remain shelf documents. National climate-tagged spending is large in absolute peso terms (CCC: PHP 1.59T climate-tagged 2016–2022; FY 2023 NGA tagged ~PHP 453B) but **LGU-side actual climate expenditure is poorly measured**.  
- **COA / PEFA framing (via GIZ 2025):** no formal evaluation mechanisms for LCCAPs; climate-related expenditures not clearly traceable from AIP Form tags into local budget ordinances and Statement of Expenditures; national RBMES for NCCAP not fully institutionalized.  
- **Academic implementation evidence (2026):** municipal-level assessment in Misamis Oriental (Lumacad et al., *Journal of Interdisciplinary Perspectives*) addresses the **thin empirical base** on how LGUs translate LCCAP initiatives into practice — signaling that “submission success” has outrun implementation research and public M&E.  
- **Vertical/horizontal misalignment:** NDC Implementation Plan 2020–2030 and NAP 2023–2050 assign delivery mainly to national sector agencies; LGUs are in the governance frame but **not cleanly assigned measurable subnational NDC co-financing or outcome targets**. Urban-Act Recommendation 3 (2025) calls for a multi-agency JMC on vertical/horizontal harmonization of climate-related plans and expenditures, supplementing DILG–DHSUD–NEDA–DBM–DOF JMC 2024-1 on local plan harmonization post–Mandanas-Garcia.

### 6. Metro Manila examples

#### 6.1 Quezon City — Enhanced LCCAP 2021–2050 (national benchmark HUC)

- **Adopted 2021** via city council resolution as the city’s official climate roadmap to 2050; led by the **Climate Change and Environmental Sustainability Department (CCESD)**.  
- **Mitigation targets (public materials):** ~**30% GHG reduction by 2030** vs BAU (2016 baseline framing in city-to-city / C40-related docs; ~3.6 MtCO2e avoided in one official summary), with **carbon neutrality / net-zero trajectory to 2050**; emissions peak intended around 2030 under the “ambitious action scenario.”  
- **Scope:** adaptation + mitigation; qualitative climate-impact assessment across natural/ecological, built, and social systems; green economy and inclusive climate education (including accessible tools for children of varying abilities); urban farming, biodiversity, waste-to-resource (e.g., Trash to Cashback).  
- **Partnerships:** C40 Cities technical assistance; Japan city-to-city zero-carbon collaboration; Quezon City University (QCU) formal adoption of LCCAP into campus operations and curricula — a rare **city university ↔ LGU** institutionalization of a municipal climate plan.  
- **Quality signals:** long horizon (to 2050), quantified mitigation target, dedicated department, public-facing communications, university embedding. Residual challenge: city-scale delivery across dense informal settlements, heat islands, and waste/flood interfaces still requires continuous AIP financing and barangay-level translation.

#### 6.2 Pasig City — LCCAP 2016–2026 + “Promise of Pasig” (citizen-centric mitigation model)

- **Baseline LCCAP:** City Government of Pasig crafted an LCCAP with a **2016–2026** planning horizon (referenced in city council Resolution No. 58-11 and related enactments). Vision language: model community of empowered citizens with high quality of life; convergence of mitigation and adaptation.  
- **GHG inventory (2017, with ICLEI):** ~**1.48 MtCO2e**; **~83% from electricity** (commercial/institutional ~52%, residential ~30%); transport ~12%. First Philippine city in the Ambitious City Promises (ACP) cohort to complete a community-level inventory with ICLEI.  
- **Promise of Pasig (ACP / ICLEI–IKI, ~2021):** living climate commitment more ambitious than national-relative BAU framing — **20% emissions reduction by 2030 vs 2017 base year**; **422 specific actions** across energy, transport, waste, ecology, health & DRRM. Instruments span infrastructure, research, partnerships, capacity building, advocacy, ordinance/compliance, and lifestyle shift.  
- **EcoPledge platform (pasigecopledge.org):** citizen pledge portal for energy efficiency, solid waste, and transport/air quality behaviors (e.g., AC set-point ≥25°C); handed over from ICLEI SEAS to Pasig LGU (2021 MOU). Demonstrates **bottom-up accountability** rare in Philippine LCCAP practice.  
- **Quality signals:** inventory-driven prioritization, quantified target vs base year (not only BAU), citizen digital tooling, mayoral political ownership (Sotto administration). Residual challenge: sustaining EcoPledge engagement and converting 422 actions into multi-year AIP-funded capital programs beyond award-winning pilots (bike lanes, green buildings).

#### 6.3 Marikina City — resilience practice stronger than published “LCCAP brand”

- **Public documentation of a single titled “Marikina Enhanced LCCAP” is thinner** than QC or Pasig’s Promise package; climate action is more visible through **flood-risk systems, CDRA/CLUP updates, and mobility–waste programs** than through a flagship long-horizon PDF. FOI requests and DHSUD NCR 2026 capacity work reference Marikina **CDP, CLUP, and CDRA** as the operative risk-planning stack.  
- **CCC Stories of Resilience compendium** highlights Marikina as a **paragon of urban climate resilience via road-sharing**: ~700 km cleared sidewalks, ~52 km bikeways, five bridges for walkers/bikers — co-benefits for low-carbon mobility and floodable-road safety culture.  
- **Flood early-warning practice:** Marikina River stage-based alarms (prepare / evacuate / forced evacuate at successive meters above sea level) are nationally recognized DRR infrastructure that function as **climate adaptation assets**, whether or not labeled “LCCAP PAP.”  
- **Waste and greening:** local solid-waste and greening initiatives appear in secondary city strategy materials; integration with LCCAP/LDRRMP/CLUP is the governance question more than absence of action.  
- **Quality signal vs gap:** strong operational DRR and mobility heritage; opportunity to **repackage existing programs into a modern eLCCAP** with GHG baseline, heat-risk module, and CCET-tagged multi-year investment plan — a student/LGU tooling use case (see §8).

**Metro Manila comparative takeaway**

| City | Plan brand | Horizon | Quantified GHG target | Distinctive asset | Gap |
|------|------------|---------|----------------------|-------------------|-----|
| **Quezon City** | Enhanced LCCAP 2021–2050 | 2050 | 30% vs BAU by 2030; net-zero 2050 | Dedicated CCESD + QCU adoption + C40 | Scale of dense-city delivery |
| **Pasig** | LCCAP 2016–2026 + Promise of Pasig | 2026 / 2030 | 20% vs 2017 base year | GHG inventory + EcoPledge + 422 actions | Sustaining citizen pledges → capital AIP |
| **Marikina** | CDRA/CLUP/LDRRMP-led practice | Rolling | Less publicly branded | Flood EWS + road-sharing bikeways | Explicit eLCCAP packaging & M&E |

### 7. Institutional tools and capacity infrastructure (already exist)

- **NICCDIES** (niccdies.climate.gov.ph): primary CCC platform for climate data, action plans, and resources — LCCAP page, CORE modules (NRA, PSF, CCET, GIS-CDRA, community GHG), Enhanced Guidebooks Books 3–4, community GHG spreadsheet tool, CCET analysis tools for cities/municipalities and provinces.  
- **CORE (Communities for Resilience, 2016–present):** five modalities — eLCCAP mentoring, LCCAP QA, Climate Resiliency Field School, multi-stakeholder consortia (e.g., Northern Panay), CICARP for IP/Bangsamoro/urban poor basic adaptation services. Trained **122+ HEIs** and large LGU cohorts; SUCs intended as durable local coaches.  
- **GeoRisk Philippines, CliMAP, PAGASA products:** hazard and climate information layers for CDRA — underused by capacity-thin LGUs without GIS staff.  
- **CCET tools + visual process guides** in Local Budget Memoranda: intended sequencing of risk assessment → strategy → mainstreaming → budget tagging.  
- **International city networks:** C40 (QC), ICLEI ACP (Pasig), GIZ Urban-Act (Antipolo, Bacolod, Tagbilaran pilots + national policy workstream), Japan city-to-city (QC). These raise quality ceilings for partner cities but **do not replace** nationwide QA/M&E.

### 8. Student and LGU tooling opportunities (2015–2026 → product space)

Gaps create a clear product and service market for **youth/university–LGU collaboration** (YCLAP-relevant):

1. **eLCCAP drafting copilots**  
   - Structured templates that force CDRA fields, GHG sectors, PAP costing, AIP tag codes, and gender markers  
   - Auto-checklists mapped to Books 3–4 and the LCCAP QA instrument  
   - Export packs: council resolution draft, CCC submission cover, PSF concept-note seeds  

2. **Open Metro Manila climate plan registry**  
   - Machine-readable inventory of HUC LCCAPs / Promises / CDRA summaries with version dates, targets, and AIP-linked PAPs  
   - Diff tool for plan updates (election-year churn)  

3. **Barangay translation layer**  
   - Convert city PAPs into barangay action cards (waste segregation, cool roofs, evacuation drills, urban farming) with simple KPIs students can help monitor  

4. **Citizen pledge + MRV lite (Pasig EcoPledge model)**  
   - Replicable open-source pledge platforms for other cities/municipalities  
   - Link pledges to sector emission factors so LGUs can report co-benefits  

5. **CCET ↔ AIP ↔ SOE traceability helper**  
   - Student-built tools that map tagged AIP lines to appropriation ordinances and Statement of Expenditure lines — directly addresses GIZ/PEFA “not clearly traceable” finding  

6. **GHG inventory sprint kits for 4th–6th class LGUs**  
   - Simplified community inventory workbooks using NICCDIES GHG tool + publicly available activity data (fuel sales, electricity franchise data, waste tonnage)  
   - HEI extension teams (CORE-trained SUCs) as coaches; students as enumerators  

7. **QA pre-review bots / scorecards**  
   - Rubric scoring against CCC QA instrument before provincial review  
   - Heatmaps of missing sections for MPDO/CENRO teams  

8. **Outcome dashboards (beyond plan existence)**  
   - Track flood EWS function tests, waste diversion rates, bike-km, green space ha, heat mortality proxies — co-designed with LDRRMO and CCESD-type offices  
   - Bridge to PSF project M&E and future PSCCSI climate statistics  

9. **Marikina-style “repackage existing assets” studio**  
   - Capstone projects that rewrite operational DRR/mobility/waste programs into eLCCAP format with GHG and finance annexes — high educational value, high LGU utility  

10. **Vertical alignment briefers**  
    - One-pagers linking local PAPs to NDC sector PAMs, NAP 2023–2050, and PDP CCAM chapter for LCE/SP briefings — reduces “compliance theater”  

**Demand drivers through 2026:** residual ~10% non-submitters; widespread need for **first genuine update/enhancement** of 2015–2019 plans; Mandanas-Garcia fiscal space + JMC 2024-1 plan harmonization; NDC conditional finance readiness; PSF proposal quality bar; COA pressure for M&E; Urban-Act recommended toolkits on CRE monitoring and GHG communication.

### 9. Risks and failure modes for tooling

- **Compliance theater automation:** tools that only help LGUs “pass QA” without budget linkage worsen shelf-plan problem.  
- **Data privacy / security** for barangay vulnerability datasets.  
- **Political cycle:** tools must survive LCE turnover (open data + multi-office ownership).  
- **Fragmentation:** proliferating uncoordinated university apps vs NICCDIES integration. Prefer **NICCDIES-compatible** exports and CORE module alignment.  
- **Equity:** tooling that only serves Metro Manila HUCs widens the quality gap; design for low-bandwidth / offline-first municipal use.

## YCLAP implication

1. **Primary product wedge:** YCLAP should treat LCCAP not as a PDF repository but as a **living pipeline** — risk data → prioritized PAPs → CCET tags → AIP/ordinance → citizen co-action → outcome metrics. The national system already produces *plans*; it fails at *quality, linkage, and M&E*.  
2. **Metro Manila pilots:**  
   - **QC:** partner with CCESD/QCU on youth-facing accessibility and barangay heat/waste modules under the 2021–2050 Enhanced LCCAP.  
   - **Pasig:** extend EcoPledge logic (open MRV, school pledges, sector dashboards) and help track the 422 Promise actions against AIP.  
   - **Marikina:** offer an eLCCAP “repackaging” studio that formalizes flood EWS + road-sharing + waste into a QA-ready enhanced plan with GHG baseline.  
3. **Student workforce model:** mirror CCC CORE’s HEI coaching design — student teams as **CDRA data collectors, GHG inventory assistants, QA pre-reviewers, and citizen-platform maintainers** under SUC/LGU MOUs.  
4. **Policy narrative for pitch:** cite the 137 → ~1,500+ submission arc as proof of *mandate absorption*, and the CCET/M&E gaps (GIZ 2025, COA framing) as proof of *implementation market*. YCLAP fills the missing middle between plan existence and climate results.  
5. **Finance hook:** every improved LCCAP improves **PSF / ODA / NDC-aligned** proposal bankability (cross-link R16). Tooling that outputs PSF-ready concept notes from LCCAP PAPs is dual-use.  
6. **Avoid:** reinventing NICCDIES; building closed-source tools LGUs cannot own after election cycles; climate apps without ordinance/budget path.
