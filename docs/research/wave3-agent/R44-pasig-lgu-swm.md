# R44 — Pasig City LGU Solid Waste Management & Environment Office: Public Programs, Ordinances, MRFs, Partners, and Student-Pilot Channels (2020–2026)

**Scope:** Pasig City Solid Waste Management Office (SWMO) / City Environment and Natural Resources Office (CENRO) public programs ~2020–2026; Ecological SWM ordinances; Materials Recovery Facilities (MRF) and recycling infrastructure; recycling/refill drives; partner organizations; public contact channels usable for student pilots.  
**Method:** Official LGU citizen charters and ordinance PDFs; World Bank Metro Manila SWM assessment; USAID / UNDP / Japan / EU / Nordic embassy communications; ICLEI *Promise of Pasig*; EcoWaste Coalition MOA reporting; peer policy reviews (Andaya et al. 2025–26 on MRF policy).  
**Status:** Research agent note for YCLAP-Gargar (wave 3). Not an official LGU inventory.

---

## Key findings

### 1. Institutional setup: SWMO + CENRO (same head)

Pasig runs a dedicated **Solid Waste Management Office (SWMO)** distinct from, but tightly coupled with, **CENRO**. In practice the same official has served as **SWMO Department Head / Acting CENRO**:

| Role | Name | Notes |
|------|------|--------|
| SWMO / Acting CENRO Department Head II | **Allendri B. Angeles** | Named on citizen charter, EcoWaste Coalition 2025 launch, MMDA SWMIS LGU page |
| Waste & Industry TWG (climate LEDS) | CENRO + CSWMO/SWMO | *Promise of Pasig* (ICLEI) Climate Core Team |
| Citywide collection contractor (from Jul 2023) | **Phil. Ecology Systems Corp. (PhilEco)** | Citywide garbage collection, hauling, disposal for ~29 barangays (+ hauling-only for Pinagbuhatan) |

SWMO regulates ordinance compliance (OVRs), junkshop clearances, special collection requests, and seals private/commercial MRFs. CENRO leads broader environment policy and sits on the city Climate Core Team with SWMO for waste-and-industry workstreams.

**Ten-year SWM plan:** Pasig maintained a **Ten-Year Solid Waste Management Plan (2015–2024)** (cited in World Bank 2022 Metro Manila assessment). ICLEI *Promise of Pasig* (LEDS) records a city commitment to **reduce waste generation ~20% by 2025** via segregation, collection schedules, and designated disposal. Public FOI requests for a post-2024 plan update were filed as late as 2023–2026; a successor plan status should be reconfirmed with SWMO before any pilot claim of “current 10-year plan targets.”

**Reported diversion (baseline still cited into 2020s):** **~60%** diversion (MMDA Oct 2020 snapshot). World Bank classifies Pasig among **Tier-1 NCR LGUs** suited for **centralized dry-recyclable recovery** investment (alongside Parañaque and Muntinlupa). Treat the 60% figure as a formula-sensitive MMDA metric (collected/processed vs residual), not a verified material-flow audit.

### 2. SWM ordinances and legal architecture (still operative 2020–2026)

| Instrument | Year | Core provisions relevant to public programs / pilots |
|------------|------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| **Ordinance No. 9, s. 2010** | 2010 | **Plastic bag ban** on dry goods; regulates plastic bags on wet goods; bans **styrofoam**. Post-Ondoy (2009) flood-and-drainage response. Still enforced and cited in 2025 regional reporting as a decade-plus plastic-free retail norm. |
| **Ordinance No. 01, s. 2018** — *Ecological Solid Waste Management Code of Pasig City* | 2018 | City ESWM Code implementing **RA 9003**. **Ch. V Sec. 10:** every barrio **or cluster of barangays** must establish an **MRF** on city/barangay-owned or leased land (or other open space approved by the SWM board). **Ch. VII Sec. 14:** malls, commercial complexes, condominiums, and similar structures must have **own MRFs**, subject to **SWMO seal of approval**. Clean-up day: **every Wednesday**. SWMO functions, enforcement, and fee structure sit under this Code. |
| **Ordinance No. 1, s. 2024** (related citation) | 2024 | Cross-references segregation rules under the 2018 ESWM Code (appears in other city ordinance text; not a full repeal of Ord. 01-2018). |
| **Citizen Charter — Ordinance No. 01 s. 2018 enforcement** | (current charter) | **Ordinance Violation Receipt (OVR)** penalties: individuals/establishments **₱1,000 / ₱2,000 / ₱3,000** (1st–3rd offense); collection contractors **₱5,000 / ₱10,000 / ₱15,000**; junkshop owners **₱5,000 / ₱10,000 / ₱15,000**. |
| **Junkshop Clearance to Operate** | (current charter) | SWMO inspection (₱500) + accreditation (₱1,000); annual renewal; non-renewal penalties tiered. Formalizes junkshops into the recovery chain (~69 junkshops in WB tables). |

**Takeaway for student pilots:** Any school or community MRF/drop-off node, sachet buy-back, or public “eco-brick” demonstration should be framed under **Ord. 01-2018** (barangay/cluster MRF + commercial MRF seal) and **Ord. 09-2010** (SUP reduction / refill preference). Enforcement is live via OVR and junkshop licensing—not only IEC.

### 3. MRF and recycling infrastructure stock (2020–2026 window)

#### City / barangay MRF baseline (World Bank 2022 tables still used in 2025–26 policy reviews)

| Facility type | Count (approx.) | Notes |
|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Central / city-scale MRFs** | **5–6** | Strongest central MRF cluster among NCR cities in WB Table 7 |
| **Barangay MRFs** | **~17** | Incomplete relative to 30 barangays |
| **MRS (Materials Recovery System / junkshop MOU)** | **1** | Low MRS reliance vs Manila/QC |
| **Junkshops** | **~69** | Licensed via SWMO Clearance to Operate |

Pasig also hosted a **private mechanized mixed-waste MRF** (IPM Environmental Services) reported at ~**2,000 m³/day** capacity; contract ended **Feb 2021**. Annual renewal of collection/processing contracts is a known **CapEx-hostile** structural risk for mechanized plant investment (World Bank / R08 cross-ref).

#### Flagship facilities and mobile assets (2023–2026)

| Asset | Date | Partners | Function |
|-------|------|----------|----------|
| **Mobile Materials Recovery Facility + biogas digester** | **19 Apr 2023** turnover | Embassy of Japan, UNDP, DENR under **ACE** (“Accelerating NDCs through Circular Economy in Cities”) | Mobile MRF for barangay-level sorting; digester for organic waste. Still showcased in 2025–26 ICE Hub study tours. |
| **Community EcoHub — Brgy. Manggahan** | Inaugurated **14 Nov 2023** | **USAID Clean Cities, Blue Ocean (CCBO)** + **Green Antz Builders, Inc.** + LGU (Mayor Sotto, VM Jaworski, Rep. Romulo) | ~**₱14M** modern recycling hub: **up to 1 tpd organic composting**; upcycles low-value plastic (sachets, bags) into **eco-bricks/pavers** (~17% shredded plastic content). E-waste collection vehicles/equipment also under CCBO. Long-term ops intended to transfer to city. |
| **Innovation for Circular Economy (I.C.E.) Hub** | Opened/co-designed **2023**; formal inauguration **26 Jan 2026** | **UNDP** + Japan (ACE) + **EU–PH Green Economy Partnership** (GEPP / Global Gateway) + Nordic embassies (Sweden, Denmark) + USAID (early CCBO years); also Australia, Norway, Canada, Switzerland, Brazil, New Zealand cited as supporters | First LGU pilot for circular economy as **public service**. Sorting lines, composting, upcycling workshops; training for MSMEs, women-led textile upcyclers, waste pickers, queer community enterprises. Inclusive MRF redesign (safe work areas, free tools). **MMDA linkage:** City presentation materials also show cooperation on **Metro Manila Flood Management Project — Minimizing Solid Waste in Waterways** (mobile MRF framing). River-basin plastic load (Pasig–Marikina) remains a residual leakage risk despite above-average central MRF stock.

## Partnership angles

1. **Student IEC + Wednesday clean-up attachment**  
   Low-friction entry: piggyback **Ord. 01-2018 Wednesday clean-up** and SWMO IEC. Students run segregation coaching, waste audits (mini-WACS), or estero-plastic intercept documentation with barangay + SWMO monitoring officers. Fits citizen-charter feedback loop (email/hotline).

2. **ICE Hub as living lab for student pilots**  
   ICE Hub is explicitly a **shared testing ground** for circular ideas, MSMEs, and inclusive innovation. Angles: textile/plastic upcycling workshops, informal-worker dignity research, digital tracking of redemption center transactions, or social-enterprise business models. Approach via SWMO + UNDP PH GEPP SO2 contacts; cite EU-PH Green LGUs replication narrative.

3. **EcoHub / eco-brick + school STEM**  
   Manggahan EcoHub’s **sachet-to-paver** and **1 tpd compost** line are tangible demo assets. Student pilots: material science of plastic-aggregate pavers, compost quality testing, low-value plastic recovery rates, or barangay replication feasibility under Ord. 01-2018 commercial/barangay MRF rules. Partner: Green Antz + SWMO; USAID CCBO legacy framing helps grant narratives.

4. **Refill / plastic-free retail with EcoWaste Coalition**  
   10 refill stores + Sabon Express MOA (2025) is an active city–CSO pipeline. Student role: behavior-change surveys, store operator support, market IEC, sachet baseline counts. Aligns with **Ord. 09-2010** plastic bag/styro ban culture and city zero-waste rhetoric under Mayor Sotto.

5. **Mobile Recyclable Redemption Center incentives**  
   Recyclables-for-groceries is a high-visibility community mechanism. Student pilots: optimize stop schedules, measure kg recovered per visit, test school drop-off nodes, or design app/logbooks for household participants.

6. **Nordic–school upcycling / carton stream**  
   Existing embassy + Tetra Pak school programs in Pasig are the cleanest **student-facing** channel already legitimized by LGU study visits to Denmark/Sweden. Expand cartons/paper streams, youth upcycling showcases at ICE Hub, or “waste-to-worth” fairs with SK Federation.

7. **Junkshop formalization + informal sector inclusion**  
   ~69 junkshops under SWMO clearance; ICE Hub narrative includes waste-picker recognition. Student research pilots: price discovery, gender in recovery chain, MRS vs fixed MRF comparative recovery—feed SWMO policy, not extractive mapping only.

8. **River / flood co-benefit framing**  
   MMDA waterways solid-waste mobile MRF and Pasig–Marikina basin leakage allow pilots that link **SWM + flood risk** (YCLAP multi-hazard narrative). Partner SWMO + CDRRMO (both on Climate Core Team).

9. **Replication / peer-LGU learning products**  
   Pasig is a **donor showcase** (EU model for 10 LGUs; Lanao del Sur study visits). Student teams can produce open playbooks (barangay MRF SOP, redemption SOP, refill SOP) that SWMO can hand to peer LGUs—high political value for the Mayor’s Office.

10. **Post-2024 SWM plan refresh**  
    If the 2015–2024 plan has not been publicly replaced, a student/academic partnership to support **WACS update + plan revision** (RA 9003 10-year plan requirement) is a high-leverage, policy-grade engagement with SWMO/CENRO/CPDO.
