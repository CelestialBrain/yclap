# R13 — Quezon City / Metro Manila urban heat risk, cooling interventions, and school heat policy (2024–2026)

**Agent:** YCLAP research (wave 3)  
**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Geography:** Quezon City primary; Metro Manila / NCR context  
**Scope:** Heat index practice; barangay high-risk maps; cooling interventions (parks, cool roofs, water/cooling stations); school heat policies 2024–2026

## Key findings

### 1. Heat index: how risk is measured and lived in QC / Metro Manila

- **PAGASA heat-index bands** (operational for public messaging and many LGU thresholds):
  - **Caution:** 27–32°C  
  - **Extreme caution:** 33–41°C  
  - **Danger:** 42–51°C  
  - **Extreme danger:** ≥52°C  
  Health framing: extreme caution raises risk of heat cramps/exhaustion; **danger** makes heat stroke possible with continued exposure; extreme danger makes heat stroke imminent.

- **Heat index ≠ air temperature.** It combines temperature and humidity (how heat *feels*). Urban surfaces also drive **land surface temperature (LST)** and the **urban heat island (UHI)**; satellite LST often exceeds ambient air readings and is what spatial risk maps use when dense weather-station networks are thin.

- **Quezon City operational practice:** The LGU publishes a public **Heat Index** page with near-daily dashboard images (multi-month archive for 2025–2026 hot seasons). QCDRRMO and partner messaging have flagged city heat index values in the **danger range** (e.g. ~41°C with ~35°C air / high humidity; multi-day stretches in March–April). City communications (2026) state that QC experienced **high heat indices averaging ~42–44°C in 2024 and 2025**, with continued upward pressure on welfare, outdoor livelihoods, and electricity/water stress.

- **Seasonality:** National and NCR evidence converge on **March–May (hot-dry / MAM)** as the peak UHI and heat-health season. PhilSA-linked MODIS work (2020) found UHI strongest in MAM and **Metro Manila among the highest heat-health risk zones** nationally—driven by dense population, extensive impervious cover, and poverty-related exposure (informal housing, limited cooling access).

- **Lived impacts (2024–2026):** Danger-range heat indices drove **widespread school suspensions and schedule shifts** in Metro Manila LGUs (including QC), higher cooling energy costs for households, and elevated risk for **outdoor / informal workers, children, elderly, and people without safe indoor cooling**. UN climate profiling of QC notes heat index already **~41°C by March 2025**, remaining dangerous through April, layered on top of typhoons, flooding, and UHI from dense urbanization.

### 2. Barangay-scale high-risk maps (Quezon City)

**Primary local product:** Aerol Cedrick Treyes (2025), *When the city heats up: mapping urban heat risks through environmental and socioeconomic factors in Quezon City, Philippines* (*Environmental Research: Climate*).

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| Study frame | Hot-dry season **2020–2024**; 142 barangays; La Mesa Watershed excluded as natural cool sink |
| Method | Localized **Heat Risk Index (HRI)** = weighted mix of environmental exposure (LST, NDVI, NDBI, surface albedo) + socioeconomic vulnerability (population density, age vulnerability, nighttime lights as SES proxy); AHP expert weights |
| High-risk share | **81% of barangays** (115/142) in **high-risk HRI 0.61–0.80**; remaining 19% moderate (0.41–0.60) |
| Population share | High-risk barangays hold **~70% of city population** |
| City mean HRI | **0.605** (range ~0.235–0.763) |
| Highest mean HRI | **Barangay Manresa (0.731)**; then San Jose (0.724), Paang Bundok (0.723), Damar (0.719), Masambong (0.718)—clustered south/southwest |
| Lower HRI examples | Bagong Silangan, UP Campus, Pansol, Greater Lagro, Old Capitol Site—often near **La Mesa** or large green campuses/parks |
| Spatial pattern | High risk concentrated in **Districts 1, 3, 4, 6** (south–southwest): dense urbanization, low vegetation, high LST. Commercial hubs (e.g. Cubao/Araneta City) elevated. |
| Intraurban heat islets | **~31% of study area** above local LST hotspot threshold; strong overlap with high-HRI barangays (80 of 81 islet barangays also high HRI) |

**Drivers (study + Metro Manila literature):**

- Low vegetation / high built-up intensity (NDVI–NDBI–LST tightly linked)  
- Low-albedo roofs and pavements (heat absorption)  
- Population density and age vulnerability as **exposure multipliers** (even where LST is not the absolute peak)  
- Poverty and informal settlement conditions amplify harm (crowding, poor ventilation, limited AC, outdoor labor)—national UHI heat-health mapping (Vergara & Blanco / PhilSA–UP, 2023) flags Metro Manila as most exposed/vulnerable nationally  

**Policy link:** Results are framed to operationalize QC **Enhanced LCCAP 2021–2050**, especially **Strategy 5 (nature-based solutions for heat and drought)**—prioritize green corridors, pocket parks, streetside planting, rooftop/vertical gardens, and reflective surfaces in the **high-HRI south/southwest belt**.

**National/NCR backdrop:** Estoque et al.–style heat health risk rankings place QC among high-risk PH cities; MODIS UHI work shows Metro Manila hotspots co-located with densest population and elderly concentrations.

### 3. Cooling interventions: parks, cool roofs, water / cooling stations

#### A. Nature-based and green infrastructure (primary structural path for QC)

- **LCCAP Strategy 5** and HRI guidance: expand **pocket parks**, **green corridors** linking fragmented canopy, **streetside vegetation**, and **rooftop / vertical gardens**; use native / drought-tolerant species for longevity under heat–dry stress.
- **Existing cool anchors** that suppress local HRI: **La Mesa Watershed**, Quezon Memorial Circle, Ninoy Aquino Parks and Wildlife, Veterans Memorial Golf Course, **UP Diliman / Arboretum**, Ateneo de Manila and Miriam College campuses, Balara Filters Park (Pansol). These demonstrate that large green and blue assets still matter even inside a megacity.
- **C40 Cool Cities Accelerator** (launched late 2025; QC among partner cities with technical assistance): global framing pairs **cool shelters**, **tree planting**, and **urban greening** as scalable heat-resilience measures. QC is repeatedly cited for **tree-planting–led cooling** (contrasted with Phoenix-style cooling centres in C40 communications).
- Evidence base (global, transferable): tree canopy and parks cool via shade + evapotranspiration; siting greening in **hot, low-canopy, high-population** barangays yields the highest equity return.

#### B. Cool roofs, green roofs, and building standards

- **QC Green Building Ordinance** lineage (2009+) already enabled incentives such as realty-tax discounts for **green roofs and walls** (historic reporting).
- **Quezon City Green Building Code of 2025** (SP-3439, S-2025; C40-highlighted): strengthens thermal comfort and energy efficiency; promotes **passive cooling, cool roofs, improved shading**; sets **24°C HVAC setpoint** in government offices; aims to cut UHI contribution, improve indoor quality, and lower AC demand. Heat Health Information Network summary: explicit cool-roof / passive-cooling pathway.
- **Physics of cool roofs:** high solar reflectance + high thermal emittance → cooler roof and lower indoor heat load; **green roofs** add insulation, runoff control, and some ambient cooling but cost more and need water/structure. For dense QC informal and low-rise fabric, **cool (reflective) roof coatings** are often the more deployable first step; green roofs suit institutional/new mid-rise stock.

#### C. Cooling centers, hydration, and institutional “heat action”

- **June 2026 — QC Heat Action Team (QC-HAT):** Mayor Joy Belmonte chairs; Chief Heat Action Officers from **CCESD** (Andrea Villaroman — planning/policy), **QCDRRMO** (Ma. Bianca Perez-Diamante — response coordination), and **QC Health / epidemiology** (Dr. Rolando Cruz — heat illness surveillance). Mandate: city **Heat Action Plan**, community vulnerability assessments, mitigation strategies, council policy recommendations, stakeholder expansion of cooling initiatives.
- **32 community libraries designated as cooling centers** for seniors, PWDs, and children during extreme heat (QC government, 2026).
- **April 2026 Urban Heat and Drought Summit** (QC-hosted): multi-stakeholder convening on risk literacy and inclusive cooling.
- **Hydration / water stations:** less publicly quantified as a formal QC network than libraries-as-cooling-centers; literature and international practice treat **free drinking water, misting, and pop-up hydration** as standard heat-response packages alongside cooling centers. Treyes policy discussion explicitly recommends **public cooling stations in high-risk barangays** plus heat-health awareness and DRR plan integration. YCLAP should treat **mapped, stocked hydration + shade nodes** in high-HRI barangays as a gap-filling student-scale complement to formal cooling centers—not a substitute for canopy and roof albedo.

#### D. Equity and co-stressors

- Heat compounds **power cost**, **water stress in dry spells**, and **education disruption**. Informal outdoor workers lack both shade and recovery space. Interventions that only cool formal campuses leave the **south/southwest high-HRI belt** under-served unless deliberately targeted.

### 4. School heat policies, 2024–2026

| Year | Instrument / practice | Substance |
|------|----------------------|-----------|
| **2024 (peak heat crisis)** | DepEd **DO 037, s. 2022** reiterated | School heads may suspend F2F and shift to **ADM** when extreme heat (or other calamities) threatens learners/staff. Thousands of schools (~**4,000+** reported mid-crisis) suspended onsite classes; Metro Manila LGUs (Manila, QC, others) issued city-level suspensions for “dangerous heat.” |
| **2024** | **DepEd Order No. 22, s. 2024** — *Revised Guidelines on Class and Work Suspension in Schools During Disasters and Emergencies* | **No automatic national suspension solely for extreme heat.** Authority to suspend F2F for heat sits with **Local Chief Executives (LCEs)** and/or **Schools Division Superintendents (SDS)**; schools then use ADM / modular / online / blended / makeup. Reiterated in 2025 division memos amid rising heat index. |
| **2024 (QC local)** | QC automatic-suspension practice (news contemporaneous) | Public reporting: QC moved toward **forecast-based automatic suspension** when heat index approached **~40°C** (early 2024 framing); later refined. |
| **2025 (QC)** | **Memorandum Circular No. 3, s. 2025** (Mayor Belmonte); aligned with DepEd | Public schools (CDC, Kindergarten, G1–12, ALS): **full F2F suspension if iRISE UP forecasts heat index ≥42°C**; **40–41°C → limited F2F + shortened hours + alternative modalities**. Private schools and HEIs may decide independently. Multi-hazard memo also covers weather, earthquake, air quality. |
| **2025 (national operational)** | DepEd heat-season advisories | Adjust schedules (earlier starts, afternoon modular), prioritize learner/staff health; reiteration of DO 22 discretion chain. |
| **2026 (July)** | DepEd draft **executive-order pathway** for standardized automatic suspensions | Proposal under nationwide consultation: automatic suspend/resume of F2F and onsite work when agency thresholds hit—including proposed **heat index ≥40°C**, rainfall warnings, vog, air-quality risk. Local CEOs and school heads retain granular suspend/resume; flexible delivery when F2F unsafe. **Not yet enacted as of research date**—still draft/consultative. |

**Implications:**

- **Threshold mismatch risk:** QC local rule anchors full suspension at **≥42°C (PAGASA “danger”)**, while DepEd’s 2026 draft floats **≥40°C** automatic. Cohort projects that track school days lost should state **which threshold** they use.
- **Learning continuity vs safety:** Policy stack still prioritizes ADM over structural school cooling (shade, cool roofs, ventilation). Youth projects that **cool school yards or map cool routes to school** address the gap between suspension policy and everyday heat exposure on non-suspension days (33–41°C extreme caution is still harmful for PE, outdoor assembly, and un-air-conditioned rooms).
- **Ateneo / HEI note:** QC guidelines leave HEIs to self-determine; campus heat risk is real but **administratively different** from DepEd basic-education rules.

## YCLAP optional co-benefit

Heat / cooling equity is already flagged in the master brief as **under-addressed but high-leverage for YCLAP** (QC: ~81% barangays high heat-risk). It is **not** the recommended flagship (Gargar scrap-to-value / organics–methane remains primary) but is a strong **optional co-benefit lane** if the cohort pivots or dual-tracks.

**Fit for a 5-week student pilot (Ateneo–QC node):**

1. **Cool-route / heat-equity map (Science + Mobilize)**  
   - Overlay public Treyes high-HRI barangays (or open LST/NDVI proxies) with **campus–barangay cool corridors**, existing parks, and the **32 library cooling centers**.  
   - Deliverable: walkable “shade + water + rest” map for students and outdoor workers; hero metric = **people reached / cool nodes verified**, not °C citywide.

2. **School / SK heat playbook (Mobilize + Story)**  
   - Package DO 22 + QC MC 3 thresholds into a **1-page SK/school decision card** (what to do at 40–41 vs ≥42; ADM checklist; heat-illness signs).  
   - Optional field test with one high-HRI barangay SK or partner public school (invited only).

3. **Micro-cooling intervention (Build, only if facilities partner exists)**  
   - Reflective roof patch, temporary shade sail, or **hydration station** at a school/barangay hall—measure pre/post surface temp or user rest-minutes.  
   - Align language with LCCAP Strategy 5 and QC-HAT Heat Action Plan narrative (support city plan; do not claim to replace it).

4. **Cross-link to Gargar (preferred if dual-benefit needed)**  
   - Waste diversion → less open burning / less clogged drainage is **flood** co-benefit first; heat co-benefit is secondary (cleaner micro-sites, possible tree-planting partnerships on cleared lots).  
   - Do **not** claim carbon credits or citywide UHI reduction from a campus pilot.

**SDG hooks:** 13 (climate action), 11 (sustainable cities), 3 (health), 4 (education continuity).  
**Justice beat:** Target the **south/southwest high-HRI belt** and outdoor/informal workers—not only green campus interiors that already sit in lower-HRI pockets (UP, Ateneo, La Mesa edge).

**Anti-patterns:** Pure tree-planting theater without maintenance plan; cool-roof startup cosplay at city scale; dependence on unpassed national EO; “solve Metro Manila UHI” as pitch.
