# R20 — Sangguniang Kabataan / Youth Roles in Disaster Preparedness & Flood Response (Philippines, 2020–2026)

**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Scope:** Legal mandates for SK and youth in DRRM/climate; good SK climate–DRRM practice examples; flood protocols usable by youth organizers (2020–2026)  
**Lens for YCLAP:** Mobilize lane — SK/KK as partner surface for last-mile flood early action and waste–flood co-benefit, not a new national DRRM agency  
**Method:** Secondary synthesis of RA 10121, RA 10742, RA 11768, RA 9729, RA 12287; NYC/PYDP/CCC materials; Fernandez & Shaw SK–DRR field research; DILG Operation L!STO / Listo si Kap; LGU/youth training cases (QC, Pasig, Naga, Balaoan); UNICEF/PRC household readiness templates

## Key findings

### 1. Legal stack: youth are *named* actors in DRRM — not optional “volunteers only”

| Law / instrument | Year | What it mandates for SK / youth |
|------------------|------|----------------------------------|
| **RA 10121** (Philippine DRRM Act) | 2010 | **§14** integrates DRRM into school curricula **and the SK program**; requires NDRRMC, RDRRMCs, LDRRMCs, LDRRMOs, BDRRMCs **and SK councils** to encourage community—**specifically youth**—participation (quick-response groups in disaster-prone areas; DRRM as part of SK programs/projects). **§13** covers accreditation/protection of disaster volunteers (ACDVs). Barangay DRRM sits in the **BDRRMC** under the barangay development structure. |
| **RA 10742** (SK Reform Act) | 2016 | SK must partner with the **Local Youth Development Council (LYDC)** on advocacies including **climate change adaptation, disaster risk reduction and resiliency**. CBYDP/ABYIP must prioritize among others **environmental protection, CCA, DRR and resiliency**. SK holds **10% of barangay general fund** with fiscal independence for youth PPAs. SK chair is *ex officio* Sangguniang Barangay member (Youth & Sports Development committee). Pederasyon presidents sit on local special bodies (LDC, LCPC, etc.)—**not automatically full LDRRMC voting seats**. |
| **RA 11768** (strengthening SK / governance reforms) | 2022 | Explicit **budget priority language**: CBYDP/ABYIP must give priority to PYDP thrusts **including** programs that **“locate the youth at the forefront of climate action, environmental protection and conservation… and enlist their involvement in calamity preparedness, information dissemination and other disaster-related activities”** (§20 as amended). Honoraria/training reforms improve SK capacity to *execute* PPAs, not rewrite DRRM hierarchy. |
| **RA 9729** (Climate Change Act) + IRR | 2009+ | Pro-children / pro-poor framing; DepEd integration of climate literacy; CCA–DRR mainstreaming. Youth are not the sole duty-bearers but are core of **intergenerational** resilience framing used by CCC/NYC after 2020. |
| **RA 12287** (Declaration of State of Imminent Disaster) | 2025 | Legal basis for **anticipatory action** (commonly cited 3–5 day lead) and pre-positioning / pre-emptive measures **before** impact. Youth/SK value: pressure LGUs to write AA triggers into LDRRM plans; run household early-action drills—not unlock national funds alone. |
| **PYDP 2023–2028** (NYC) | 2023– | Environment, disaster management, and CCA as a standing youth-development priority; NYC convenes interagency sub-task forces (DepEd, DENR, CCC, DOLE, TESDA, NAPC Youth, etc.). Disaster preparedness also called a **priority program** under PYDP messaging in SK DRRM trainings. |
| **HB 6272** (Yap, 19th Cong.) & similar | 2022– | Proposed **Youth Participation in DRRM Act**: NYC chair on NDRRMC; youth orgs on all DRRM council levels. Signals a **recognized gap**: RA 10121 encourages youth; it does **not** guarantee SK a permanent seat on every LDRRMC. Status: legislative proposal, not enacted as of research date. |

**Punchline:** Philippine law already tells SK to **fund, design, and run** climate + DRRM PPAs and to **mobilize youth** into community DRR. It does **not** make the SK chair the BDRRMC chair, and it does **not** replace the punong barangay / LDRRMO chain of command. Good youth flood work is **protocol + IEC + inclusive lists + drills** under BDRRMC/DRRMO—not freelancing rescue.

### 2. Mandate vs practice: classic implementation gap (still the story in 2020–2026)

Field and policy research (Fernandez & Shaw 2013 on Infanta + Makati SKs; still the most cited SK–DRR empirical baseline) documents a durable pattern that later trainings and RA 11768 try to close:

| Indicator | Ideal (law / by-laws) | Observed practice (baseline + recurring pattern) |
|-----------|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Budget | DRR as priority PPA; older SK by-laws even contemplated dedicated disaster coordination shares | Many SKs spent heavily on **sports / pageants**; Infanta SKs in sample allocated **~0%** to DRR; Makati later used federation resolution to push **~5%** DRR allotment |
| Knowledge of RA 10121 | Officials know roles of BDRRMC, SK, quick-response groups | Low law literacy among student SK officials; urban Makati scored higher than rural Infanta but still incomplete |
| Seat at BDRRMC | Youth representation encouraged | Only a minority of sample barangays listed youth members on BDRRMC org charts; “youth” sometimes meant adults outside 15–30 |
| Program type | Preparedness + CCA + risk reduction | When SK did DRR, it skewed to **one-off first-aid / response volunteerism**, not multi-year flood early-action protocols |

**2020–2026 update, not reversal:** NYC/LGU/DRRMO trainings (e.g. Balaoan SK DRRM readiness; Naga flood drill including SK and BDRRM members; ARISE–QC/Pasig youth DRR foundations 2025–26) show **capacity-building is scaling**, but **protocol ownership and budget share remain uneven** across 42,000+ barangays. SGLG4SK / SAIL FAR-type recognition systems raise the bar for SK performance documentation—including environment and disaster-related PPAs—without creating a national flood playbook for every SK.

### 3. Good SK / youth climate–DRRM examples (usable models, not celebrity projects)

| Example | What worked | Transferable lesson for YCLAP |
|---------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| **Makati SK Federation resolution** (post-Fernandez reform path) | Citywide pressure to earmark SK budget % for DRR; first-aid / volunteer pipeline | **Federation-level budget rule** beats one heroic barangay SK |
| **ARISE Philippines + QCYDO + QCDRRMO** “Ready, Set, Resilient” (Pasig 2025 batch → QC Feb 2026 batch; ~70 youth/batch) | Foundational modules: hazard–exposure–vulnerability, DRM cycle, national policy frame; OCD NCR, UNICEF, NYC resource persons | Metro Manila **institutional pathway**: Youth Development Office × DRRMO, not campus alone |
| **Naga City DRRMO flood drill + IEC** | Drill + IEC aimed at **BDRRM members and SK** together | Youth are trained *with* barangay machinery, not parallel |
| **Local SK PPAs (scattered but real)** | SK-funded **early-warning devices / flood signage**; “Project READY”-style resilience education and drills; disaster prep + first-aid symposiums (e.g. barangay Molopolo design type); tree planting / river cleanup as CCA–mitigation co-benefit | Hardware only works if tied to **alert → action script** and O&M |
| **Balaoan (La Union) SK DRRM training** (NYC-linked) | Governance + DRRM readiness framed as **PYDP priority** | Align SK CBYDP language with PYDP environment/DRR pillars when partnering |
| **CCC Children & Youth Forum on DRRM** (Oct 2024; APMCDRR Manila week) | National youth statement path; “frontline readiness” + science-based prevention message from CCC leadership | Youth advocacy *and* household readiness both legitimate—but **advocacy ≠ evacuation protocol** |
| **Community gold standard (non-SK but youth-compatible): Buklod Tao, Brgy Banaba, San Mateo** | Hazard maps, simple river EWS, drills, zero-casualty narrative across seasons | **People’s org + clear thresholds + practice** > orphaned gadgets |

**Pattern of good practice:** (1) SK **budget line** for DRR/CCA, (2) **joint** BDRRMC–SK drill calendar, (3) **IEC in Filipino** with local landmarks, (4) inclusive vulnerable-person lists, (5) partnership with city DRRMO/NYC/CSOs—not standalone SK “rescue teams.”

### 4. Flood protocols for youth organizers (2020–2026 operating picture)

#### 4.1 Where youth sit in the official chain

```
PAGASA (rain color / river Alert–Alarm–Critical / flood advisory)
        ↓
OCD–NDRRMC / RDRRMC / media / cell broadcast–SMS
        ↓
City–Municipal DRRMO / LDRRMC
        ↓
Punong Barangay + BDRRMC  ← SK chair (SB member) + SK committees plug here
        ↓
Purok leaders / text brigade / siren–megaphone / youth runners
        ↓
Households → pre-emptive evacuation → school / covered court → headcount
```

**Youth-safe roles (legal + practical):**

| Phase | SK / youth organizer role | Do **not** own alone |
|-------|---------------------------|----------------------|
| **Peace-time preparedness** | CBYDP/ABYIP DRR–CCA PPAs; drills; go-bag IEC; inclusive household roster with BHW; laminated sitio map from NOAH/HazardHunter; waste–clog flood co-benefit cleanups | Declaring state of calamity; ordering forced evacuation |
| **Watch (Yellow rain / Alert level)** | Amplify official advisories; check go-bags; buddy assignments for elderly/PWD; clear drainage chokepoints if trained and safe | Inventing unofficial “all clear” or competing weather products |
| **Warning (Orange / Alarm)** | Door-to-door / group chat activation of pre-agreed script; assist pre-emptive move of listed vulnerable persons **under BDRRMC direction** | Entering floodwater for “hero” rescue without accreditation |
| **Critical / Red** | Shelter support (registration, water, women/child-safe spaces), documentation of needs, rumor control | Leading water rescues; managing QRF funds |
| **After impact** | Relief packing logistics; mental-health referral links; rebuild IEC; after-action notes for next CBYDP | Diverting relief goods (RA 10121 prohibited acts) |

#### 4.2 Alert → action card (youth-facing translation of national products)

| Signal (public) | Household meaning youth should teach | Youth organizer action |
|-----------------|--------------------------------------|------------------------|
| **Yellow** rainfall (~7.5–15 mm/hr class) | Flooding *possible* — monitor | Post official PAGASA/LGU link only; check roster & go-bags; clear known basura choke points if dry |
| **Orange** (~15–30 mm/hr class) | Flooding *threatening* — prepare / pre-emptive readiness | Activate buddy pairs; confirm shelter open hours with barangay; start pre-emptive support for PWD/elderly on list |
| **Red** (serious flooding) | Evacuate immediately where ordered | Assist BDRRMC movement; **do not** re-enter danger zones for social content |
| Local **river Alert / Alarm / Critical** | Site-specific bankfull fractions | Teach landmark translation: “Kapag umabot ang tubig sa [tulay / poste], gawin ito” |

#### 4.3 DILG barangay readiness frame (use, don’t rewrite)

- **Operation L!STO** (DILG–LGA multi-year manuals): standardized pre-disaster checklists for governors, mayors, **punong barangays** (“Listo si Kap”).  
- **Listo si KAP** (enhanced Apr 2025; MC 2025-035 context): barangay-focused protocols for pre-disaster vigilance, imminent-hazard actions, post-disaster needs assessment; BDC continues as barangay DRRM council form.  
- **Youth organizer use:** Map SK drill scripts and IEC to **Kap checklists** so SK work is legible to DILG and LGU auditors—not a third parallel system.

#### 4.4 Household & inclusion standards youth can run

| Tool | Source pattern | Youth/SK use |
|------|----------------|--------------|
| **72-hour go-bag** | UNICEF PH Emergency Go-Bag checklist; PRC / NDRRMC 72-hour kit practice | SK Linggo ng Kabataan or pre-habagat clinic: pack demo + photo checklist in Filipino |
| **Inclusive roster** | DiDRR practice; Muntinlupa 2025 visual EWS for deaf as city-scale proof | Name, mobility need, buddy, estimated carry-out time; held by BHW+SK; **not** public web scrape |
| **Buddy system** | Community CBDRRM best practice | Pair youth with elderly/PWD/infants; practice once before peak typhoon season |
| **Evacuation barrier audit** | Informal-settler literature + R15 last-mile research | Document theft fear, livelihood lock-in, dark alleys, stairs without rail—not blame residents |
| **Basura → baha co-benefit** | RA 9003 / urban drainage reality | Youth cleanups timed **before** forecast heavy rain; link to diversion logs if dual-lane with Gargar |

#### 4.5 Safety, liability, and volunteer hygiene (non-negotiable)

1. Work under **BDRRMC / DRRMO instruction** during operations.  
2. Prefer **accredited** pathways (ACDV / LGU volunteer lists under RA 10121 §13) for response roles.  
3. No minors in water rescue; SK officials are persons in authority for many purposes but **are not** licensed rescuers by election alone.  
4. Never invent NDRRMC “ayuda” links (2025 scam wave eroded trust).  
5. Document PPAs for COA/SGLG4SK: photos, attendance, budget line, after-action—not only Facebook live.

### 5. Synthesis for 2020–2026

1. **Law is sufficient to justify SK flood/CCA work**; the scarce resource is **practiced local protocol + budget share + BDRRMC integration**.  
2. **Urban federation SK** (Makati-type) outperforms fragmented rural SK when rules force DRR budget and training.  
3. **2024–2026 policy window** (PYDP environment pillar, CCC youth DRRM forums, RA 12287 anticipatory action, Listo si KAP, ARISE city youth trainings) makes a **one-barangay flood early-action pilot** more legible to partners than it was pre-2020.  
4. **Pending bills** for automatic youth seats on all DRRMCs are evidence of a gap—not a reason to wait.  
5. Youth flood value is **last-mile conversion of warning → action**, especially for PWD, elderly, and informal settlers—not competing with PAGASA sensors.
