## Key findings

### 1. Fourth global coral bleaching event (4GBE): timeline and scale

| Fact | Detail | Source class |
|------|--------|--------------|
| Confirmed | NOAA + ICRI, **15 April 2024** — 4th global event on record; second in ~10 years | NOAA |
| Heat-stress window | Early **2023 → mid-2025** (bookended after Western Australia severe bleaching early 2025; confirmed quiet austral summer Dec 2025–Feb 2026) | NOAA NESDIS (2026-06-02); GCRMN (2026-06-08) |
| Global extent | Bleaching-level heat stress hit **~84%** of world reef area (later CRW update ~**84.4%** through 30 Sep 2025); mass bleaching in **≥83** countries/territories | NOAA CRW; ICRI; GCRMN |
| Escalation vs prior global events | Heat-stress footprint: **21%** (1998) → **37%** (2010) → **68%** (2014–17) → **84%** (4th) | GCRMN / ICRI |
| Alert scale | NOAA Coral Reef Watch added **Alert Levels 3–5** during 4GBE (Level 5 ≈ risk of >80% colony mortality on a reef) | ICRI / CRW |
| Status mid-2026 | Event **likely concluded mid-2025**; near-annual bleaching risk remains; El Niño return can restart widespread heat stress | NOAA NESDIS 2026 |

**PH framing:** 4GBE is not a remote “Great Barrier Reef story.” Philippine seas sat inside the Pacific basin heat-stress map; field programs (UP MSI WPS transects; Tubbataha bleaching surveys; DENR BMB awareness + alert messaging) document local bleaching concurrent with the global event.

### 2. West Philippine Sea (WPS) hard coral cover loss — primary field figures

**Primary academic/government chain:** UP Marine Science Institute (UP MSI) project *“Impacts of Marine Heatwaves in Select West Philippine Sea Reefs”* (DOST-PCAARRD funded; lead PI Dr. Maria Vanessa Baria-Rodriguez; international partners Japan/Indonesia on Western Pacific MHWs). Underwater photoquadrat surveys **2022–2025**. Public synthesis: DOST-PCAARRD QID (12 Nov 2025); re-reported Manila Bulletin (20 May 2026).

| Site (western PH seaboard / WPS-facing) | Bleaching intensity (hard coral) | Hard coral cover (HCC) change |
|------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Bolinao & Anda, Pangasinan; Iba, Zambales** | **60–77%** of hard corals bleached; mostly **severe** | **−51% to −59% HCC in ~two years** |
| **Lobo, Batangas; Puerto Galera, Oriental Mindoro** | Milder: **20–43%** of corals bleached | **Puerto Galera: −2%**; **Lobo: +20%** HCC |

**Interpretation (from UP MSI / PCAARRD):**

- Impacts are **site-specific**, not uniform “WPS collapsed.”
- Severe sites show **half-or-more living hard coral lost in two years** — the headline figure for narrative use: **up to ~59% HCC loss** at worst monitored western-coast sites.
- Milder / more resilient sites show near-stable or even increasing cover — evidence for **refugia prioritization**, not blanket despair.
- Severe bleaching + death expected to drive **long-term shifts in reef community composition**; heat-tolerant taxa may dominate → **biodiversity risk** even where some cover remains.
- Partner tool: **Marine Heatwave Tracker** (`mhwtracker.science.upd.edu.ph`) — daily SST/MHW updates (UP MSI + DOST-PCAARRD; Dr. Charina Lyn Amedo-Repollo).

**Caveats on the −51–59% figure:**

- Relative decline in **living hard coral cover** at **selected severe sites**, not a nationwide or whole-WPS spatial average.
- Monitoring window overlaps 4GBE peak heat (esp. **2024** SST extremes) but also pre-existing local stress (e.g. Bolinao-Anda historically pressured by fishing, aquaculture, runoff).
- Bleaching % ≠ immediate mortality %; mortality is inferred from multi-year cover loss, not single-pass colony death counts in the public brief.

### 3. Other PH reef signals (2023–2025) — context, not WPS substitute

**Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park (Sulu Sea; World Heritage; not WPS EEZ core but PH flagship reef):**

- **2020:** bleaching affected up to **~20%** of park HCC; incomplete recovery at some sites into 2021.
- **Oct–Nov 2023:** localized bleaching near ranger station (Acropora, Pocillopora); extreme low tides + NOAA warning; not park-wide.
- **2024 bleaching survey (TMO):** bleached **HCC 0.04%–43%** by site (5–7 m); **Elbow Mac 43%**, **Malayan Wreck 0.04%**; water **29–31°C**; western sides more bleached than eastern; in affected sites **50–100%** of colonies bleached; sensitive genera include *Millepora*, *Heliopora*, branching *Porites*, *Pocillopora*, *Seriatopora*. IUCN Outlook 2025 upgraded climate/bleaching concern; shallow HCC declining ~**1.2%/yr** (typhoon + bleaching + eutrophication).
- Full park-wide mortality/cover-loss synthesis still pending TMO–DLSU reef-wide assessment as of Outlook 2025 write-up.

**National baseline (pre-4GBE):**

- Licuanan et al. nationwide fringing-reef assessments: weighted mean HCC **~22%** (2017 initial) / **22.8% ±1.2 SE** (2019 *Marine Pollution Bulletin*) — already **~10 percentage points below** late-1970s era averages cited in PH discourse.
- Licuanan perspective work continues to stress: **no national reef qualified as “excellent”** under older Gomez-style scales; one-third hard-coral loss framing used in later management pieces.
- GCRMN East Asia (1983–2019): mixed regional trends; WPS subset reported **cover rise 10% (2008) → 32% (2018)** in MPA-skewed datasets — **not comparable** to Licuanan’s nationwide fringing mean; use carefully.

**WPS reef estate (background, not 4GBE mortality):**

- CARE-CaDRES / PhilCHM: WPS reef area estimate **~4,640 km²** (464,000 ha).
- WWF-Philippines (2023): WPS holds **~30% of PH coral reefs** and contributes **~27% of commercial fisheries production**; also framed large historical reef area loss to reclamation/encroachment (order of **~16,000 ha** cited as “Quezon City–sized” loss narrative). That figure is **geopolitical/habitat destruction**, distinct from **thermal bleaching mortality**.

### 4. DENR / institutional PH posture (2024–2025)

- **DENR–Biodiversity Management Bureau (BMB):** Coral Bleaching Awareness Month messaging (Nov 2024–2025 cycles); public explainers on bleaching mechanics; Facebook updates citing active **Alert Level 2** pockets (e.g. parts of Sibuguey Bay and other areas — dynamic, not a static national map).
- Regional DENR offices amplify awareness; Oceana PH and partners repost CRW-style Alert Level maps for PH waters (Batangas, Mindoro, etc. at various peaks).
- **DAO 2025-08** (BMB, Mar 2025): policy support language for **National Program for Coral Reef Conservation and Recovery** and related emerging initiatives — governance signal more than a published national mortality statistic.
- UP MSI **Philippine Coral Bleaching Watch** (citizen science / reporting): ongoing presence/absence bleaching and COTS reporting channel for early warning.

**Gap:** DENR has **not** (as of this research cut) published a single official nationwide **% hard coral cover loss for 2024–2025** equivalent to the UP MSI WPS site numbers. Policy = awareness + program scaffolding; **science numbers = UP MSI / Tubbataha / academic**.

### 6. Global hard-cover context (for scale, not PH substitution)

- GCRMN *Status of Coral Reefs of the World: 2020*: global live coral cover **−14% (2009–2018)**, driven largely by repeated large-scale bleaching.
- 4GBE aftermath: full global synthesis expected in **GCRMN Status 2025** report (under development as of mid-2026 GCRMN note) — PH national update should be watched there.
- IPCC / science consensus still used in PH outreach: **1.5°C → 70–90%** warm-water reef loss risk; **2°C → >99%** (order-of-magnitude risk language, not a measured 2024 PH number).

### 7. Numbers to use / avoid

**Safe headline stack (high confidence):**

1. World in **4th global bleaching event** (confirmed Apr 2024); heat stress on **~84%** of reefs (2023–mid-2025); event **likely ended mid-2025**.
2. UP MSI WPS monitoring: severe sites **Bolinao, Anda, Iba** — **60–77%** hard corals bleached; **51–59% hard coral cover lost in two years**.
3. Same study: not uniform — **Puerto Galera −2%**, **Lobo +20%** HCC.
4. National pre-event baseline HCC **~22–23%** (Licuanan et al.).
5. Tubbataha 2024: site bleached HCC up to **43%** of cover (not whole-park average mortality).
6. WWF-PH: WPS ≈ **30%** of PH reefs, **~27%** commercial fisheries share.

**Avoid or qualify:**

- “PH lost 59% of all reefs” — **false** (site-level severe sites only).
- Treating WWF reclamation hectare loss as bleaching mortality.
- Using GBR 2024–25 regional cover drops as PH data.
- Claiming DENR has published a national 2024–25 HCC loss %.

## YCLAP narrative (not product)

**Story spine:** The Philippines sits at the apex of the Coral Triangle with the world’s third-largest reef area — and during the fourth, worst global bleaching event on record, **western seaboard reefs did not just “turn white.” At the hardest-hit monitored sites, living hard coral cover fell by about half to three-fifths in two years.**

That sentence is the evidence-backed core. It is local (Pangasinan, Zambales), measured (UP MSI transects), timed to record SST (2024 global event), and already in government science communication (DOST-PCAARRD). It does not require inventing a national average.

**Why it matters for climate youth narrative (without pitching a coral app):**

1. **Climate is not only typhoons.** Reefs are a second face of the same heat: marine heatwaves, not just land floods. Students who only know “baha” can connect ocean heat to food, fish, and coastal protection.
2. **WPS is double-stressed.** Geopolitical habitat destruction (WWF-PH reclamation framing; ~30% of PH reefs; fisheries share) **and** thermal bleaching stack on the same seascape. Sovereignty talk without ecology is incomplete; ecology talk without people and fish is incomplete.
3. **Uneven loss is the point.** Lobo gained cover; Bolinao-class sites lost half. That is a **justice and priority** story: which communities lose nurseries first, which LGUs get monitoring and refugia status, who is left with dead rubble and fewer fish.
4. **Recovery windows are closing.** Global science says successive bleaching events leave less recovery time; GCRMN links cover decline to heat anomalies. Locally, PCAARRD already names the strategy: real-time heatwave tracking, protect resilient patches, restore with survivors of heat stress — **all of which still fail if emissions and local stressors are ignored**.
5. **Urgency without productization.** For YCLAP, coral bleaching is **narrative evidence and problem ranking**, not a five-week hardware pitch. The deep research brief already tags coral bleaching as *urgent but not student-solvable alone*. Use the figures to **teach systems** (heat → bleach → cover loss → fish/food/coast), to **ground climate justice** in PH seas, and to **support campaigns** that amplify science (UP MSI, DENR awareness, MPA integrity) — not to invent a coral-credit or sensor product as the flagship.

**One-paragraph talk track (usable as-is):**

> From early 2023 through mid-2025 the world lived through its fourth global coral bleaching event — the widest on record, with bleaching-level heat on about 84% of reefs. In the Philippines, UP MSI teams walking West Philippine Sea–facing reefs found that impact was brutally uneven: in Bolinao, Anda, and Iba, six to eight in ten hard corals bleached, and living hard coral cover fell 51 to 59 percent in only two years. Other sites barely moved, or even gained cover. That is what marine heatwaves look like at home — not a distant reef documentary, but measurable loss of the living structure that feeds fisheries and buffers coasts, on a seascape that already holds roughly a third of our reefs and a quarter-plus of commercial catch.

**Tone rules:** Lead with **measured HCC loss** and **site names**. Name **4GBE** once with the **84%** global frame. Separate **bleaching** from **island-building**. Do not overclaim national averages. End on **uneven risk + need for emissions + local protection**, not a startup pitch.
