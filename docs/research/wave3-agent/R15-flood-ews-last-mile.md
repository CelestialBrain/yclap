# R15 — Philippines flood early warning last-mile (2020–2026)

**Scope:** PAGASA national EWS, Project NOAH legacy, local apps/sensors, SK/barangay protocols, inclusive alerts (PWD, informal settlers), and what fails after the siren.  
**Lens for YCLAP:** Not “build another sensor network.” Map where warning stops converting into household action—and what a 5-week youth pilot can still ship.  
**Date:** 2026-08-07

---

## Key findings

### 1. National stack is strong; the last mile is still the bottleneck

The Philippines already has a multi-layer flood early-warning architecture:

| Layer | Actor / tool | What it does | Last-mile gap |
|-------|----------------|--------------|---------------|
| Hazard science | DOST-PAGASA | Rainfall (yellow / orange / red), river basin Alert–Alarm–Critical water levels, General Flood Advisories, tropical cyclone bulletins | Warnings often province- or basin-scale; households need *sitio*-level meaning |
| Hazard maps & impact science | UP NOAH Center (ex–DOST Project NOAH) | Barangay-level flood / landslide / storm-surge maps; impact-based flood forecasting on noah.up.edu.ph | Public map use spikes *after* disasters; few LGUs pull maps into drills and land use |
| Dissemination | NDRRMC SMS / cell broadcast; LGU Facebook; radio/TV; PAGASA PANaHON app (2026) | Mass push of advisories | Power/internet outages; app download bias toward middle class; SMS lag vs cell broadcast |
| Local sensing | GIZ LFEWS (~16 systems, ~750k beneficiaries since 2007); STREAM–EWS (PIN + PAGASA, expanding 2025–26); city/barangay sirens | Upstream water-level sensors → ops center → barangay | Sensors without *action protocols* still leave people wet; O&M and budget fragility |
| Anticipatory legal frame | **RA 12287** (Declaration of State of Imminent Disaster Act, signed Sep 2025) | 3–5 day lead-time anticipatory measures, pre-positioning, pre-emptive evacuation, LDRRMF use before impact | IRR + LGU practice still forming; “imminent disaster” ≠ automatic household move |

**Punchline for YCLAP:** National forecasting and sensors are not the primary youth-innovation gap. **Actionability after the alert** is.

### 2. PAGASA: color codes people half-understand; river levels that few can translate

**Heavy rainfall warnings (common public-facing product):**
- **Yellow** (~7.5–15 mm/hr): flooding *possible* — monitor  
- **Orange** (~15–30 mm/hr): flooding *threatening* — prepare / pre-emptive evacuation readiness  
- **Red** (>~30 mm/hr): serious flooding — evacuate immediately  

**Telemetered river basins** (Pampanga, Agno, Bicol, Cagayan, Cagayan de Oro, Pasig–Marikina–Tullahan, etc.):
- **Alert / Alarm / Critical** water levels (~40% / 60% / 100% bankfull framing in public explainers)  
- Dissemination path: PAGASA → OCD / NDRRMC / DSWD / media → LGU DRRMOs → BDRRMC  

**2020–2026 upgrades worth knowing:**
- Impact-based forecasting push (what will *happen to people*, not only mm of rain)  
- Greater Metro Manila / Laguna Lake complex FFWS partnerships (e.g. Taguig–PAGASA–KOICA style MOAs)  
- **PANaHON** website → mobile app (formally launched Typhoon and Flood Awareness Week 2026): location-relevant TC bulletins, rainfall, flood advisories  
- **STREAM–EWS Phase 2** (People in Need + PAGASA, GSMA Innovation Fund, Apr 2026): expand from 12 Mindanao pilots to 8 more municipalities (Cagayan, Camarines Norte, Eastern Samar, Bukidnon) with rainfall/water-level sensors, SMS, and LGU digital decision tools  

Even with these upgrades, public understanding often stops at “orange = bad.” Households rarely know: *which purok floods first, how many minutes of lead time, which door of the covered court opens, who carries Lola.*

### 3. Project NOAH legacy: science that outlived its DOST budget

| Period | Status |
|--------|--------|
| 2011–12 | Launched after Typhoon Sendong (Washi); DOST flagship for ~6-hour flood lead-time goal + high-res hazard maps |
| 2012–17 | Sensors, LiDAR/DREAM flood maps, ClimateX rainfall models, open Web-GIS (noah.dost.gov.ph → noah.up.edu.ph) |
| Mar 2017 | DOST funding ends; UP adopts as **UP NOAH Center** under UP Resilience Institute |
| 2017–26 | Open hazard maps + impact-based flood tools continue; cater mainly to LGUs that *ask*; bills pending to re-nationalize / expand (e.g. HB/SB packages for UP National Climate and Resilience Institute) |

**2025 lesson (Tino / Uwan season):** VERA Files and Lagmay interviews note that barangay-level NOAH maps already flagged many disaster areas—yet map viewers during Tino were reported in the low thousands. Maps that are open but not *practiced* do not evacuate anyone.

**YCLAP takeaway:** Do not rebuild NOAH. **Translate NOAH (or HazardHunter / MGB) layers into a 1-page sitio playbook** a SK chair can laminate.

### 4. Local apps and channels — many pipes, uneven flow

| Channel | Strength | Failure mode |
|---------|----------|--------------|
| NDRRMC SMS | Wide reach | Can lag; used by scammers post-flood for fake “ayuda” links (2025 OCD warnings) eroding trust |
| Cell broadcast | Faster than SMS for life-safety | Not everyone knows how to act on short text |
| LGU / PDRRMO Facebook | Local color + Tagalog/Filipino | Generic posts; weak “what to do now / where exactly” |
| Radio + TV | Still #1 in last-mile surveys when power holds | Fail during outages; one-way |
| PANaHON / UP NOAH apps | Rich maps + alerts | Requires smartphone, data, literacy; low penetration in informal settlements |
| Barangay mobile car / megaphone / siren | Works offline | Coverage holes; deaf residents hear nothing; night-time confusion |
| Relatives / neighbor chains | High trust | Uneven; fails if first node is wrong or asleep |
| Indigenous / local environmental cues | Still used (esp. upland / IP communities) | Not a substitute for basin-scale sudden rise, but good co-signal |

Northern PH last-mile research (Yanquiling 2024, Pangasinan sitios; post-Mangkhut): **TV dominated (~80%)**; relatives and traditional knowledge still major; **village siren use was very low**; **power and internet connectivity rated as negative factors** for accessing EW during the typhoon. Generic social-media posts under-perform when people need: *when to leave, where to go, how to move elderly/PWD, how to secure livelihood assets.*

### 5. SK / barangay protocols — the real “product surface”

Under **RA 10121**, every barangay should run a **BDRRMC**. Practice varies wildly.

**Typical last-mile chain (when it works):**
1. PAGASA / river gauge / local sensor hits threshold  
2. City/Municipal DRRMO notifies barangay captain / BDRRMC  
3. Siren / megaphone / text brigade / purok leaders  
4. Pre-emptive evacuation to school / covered court  
5. Headcount + vulnerable-person list  

**Sangguniang Kabataan (SK) niche (documented but underused):**
- Youth mobilization for drills, IEC, relief packing  
- Social-media amplification in youth-dense barangays  
- Occasional SK-funded early-warning devices (local examples exist)  
- Policy research (e.g. Infanta / Makati SK DRR participation studies) shows **mandate exists; consistent protocol execution does not**  

**Community gold standard (still relevant):** Buklod Tao in Barangay Banaba, San Mateo, Rizal — community hazard mapping, simple EWS, zero-casualty narrative over multiple flood seasons when linked to river monitoring. Proof that **people’s organizations + clear thresholds + drills** beat orphaned hardware.

**What “after the siren” needs as a protocol (not an app):**
- Color / water-level → **one sentence action** in Filipino + local language  
- Named **buddy pairs** for PWD / elderly / children  
- **Go-bag** content that includes IDs, meds, phone power, livestock/livelihood micro-plan  
- Evacuation route that avoids the first street that floods  
- Shelter rules that do not strand informal workers who fear theft of belongings left behind  
- Drill cadence: at least once before peak habagat / typhoon season  

### 6. Inclusive alerts: PWD and informal settlers are systematically last

#### Persons with disabilities (PWD)
- Sirens and megaphones are **auditory-only** by default → deaf / hard-of-hearing excluded  
- **Cebu Disability-inclusive DRR Network** and DiDRRN-style practice: flashing lights on alarms, large-print/audio info, sign language in drills, accessibility audits of evacuation centers  
- **Muntinlupa (Nov 2025):** first citywide **Early Warning System for the Deaf** (visual beacon lights) layered on 17 existing audible sirens — national proof-of-concept that inclusive EWS is LGU-scale feasible  
- National DiDRR guidance exists; **most barangays have no disaggregated “who needs carry-out help” list ready at 2 a.m.**

#### Informal settlers
- Highest exposure (esteros, river easements, coastal reclamation edges, low-lying Metro Manila cities)  
- Barriers to evacuation that outlast the siren:  
  - **Livelihood lock-in** (cannot leave sari-sari, scrap, fishing gear, or day-labor post)  
  - **Fear of theft** if home is left empty  
  - **Distrust of relocation** history (resettlement far from jobs → return to danger zones)  
  - **No tenure → less investment** in house elevation / safe room  
  - **Drainage + solid waste** compounding flood depth (basura → baha link; co-benefit with waste pilots)  
- Warning without **secure-belongings + return plan + cash-for-early-action** often produces “stay and ride it out”

### 7. What fails after the siren (failure taxonomy, 2020–2026)

Synthesized from Sendong→Yolanda legacy literature, GIZ LFEWS experience, STREAM design notes, Yanquiling 2024 last-mile study, PRC Early Action Protocols, 2024–2025 multi-storm seasons (incl. consecutive typhoons), and 2025–26 policy shifts:

| # | Failure mode | What it looks like on the ground | Design implication |
|---|--------------|----------------------------------|--------------------|
| F1 | **Alert without action script** | Household hears orange / siren; freezes or waits for neighbor | Every alert level needs “do X in Y minutes” |
| F2 | **Lead time too short or unused** | Flash flood / urban drainage rise faster than basin bulletin cycle | Local sensors + pre-agreed triggers; not more national apps |
| F3 | **Power / connectivity blackout** | Apps and Facebook go dark exactly when needed | Offline playbook, radio, siren, neighbor chain |
| F4 | **Cry-wolf / false-alarm fatigue** | Repeated orange without local flooding → ignore next red | Impact-based + localized thresholds; explain uncertainty |
| F5 | **Trust collapse** | Ghost flood-control projects discourse; fake NDRRMC ayuda SMS | Trusted local messenger > national brand alone |
| F6 | **Shelter refusal** | Crowding, gender risk, no PWD access, lost income | Inclusive shelter standards + early cash / asset protection |
| F7 | **Vulnerable not pre-listed** | PWD, elderly, solo parents found last | SK/BHW-maintained inclusive roster + buddy system |
| F8 | **Maps not practiced** | NOAH maps exist; nobody drilled on them | Laminated sitio map + annual drill beats website |
| F9 | **Hardware without O&M** | Dead sirens, unpaid SIM on gauges | LGU budget line + community caretaker |
| F10 | **Anticipatory finance lag** | Action still starts after damage despite RA 12287 | Youth can pressure LGU to write AA into LDRRM plan; cannot alone unlock NDRRMF |

**Core insight:** Casualties and losses increasingly happen **after people already “knew” a storm was coming**. The gap is conversion of warning → **early action**, especially for those least able to move.

### 8. Policy window that makes a 2025–26 pilot newly legible

- **RA 12287 (2025):** legal basis for anticipatory action 3–5 days before impact; LGUs must fold AA into LDRRM funds/plans.  
- **PRC Flood / Typhoon Early Action Protocols:** operational templates (beneficiary lists, triggers, rolling stores, evacuation support) YCLAP can *borrow language from*, not reinvent.  
- **EW4All / STREAM expansion:** national–NGO–telco coalitions active; youth layer can own **community protocol + inclusion**, not compete on IoT.  
- Public anger over **flood-control integrity** (2025–26 discourse): a youth product that is *transparency + household readiness* (not concrete) is politically readable and lower-risk than pretending to fix dikes.

## YCLAP playbook alternative (if not Gargar)

**Working title:** **Baha Protocol** — *Last-mile flood early-action playbook for one SK / one flood-prone barangay (Metro Manila or lagoon/river LGU partner).*

### Why this is YCLAP-fit (and not a sensor cosplay)

| Criterion | Fit |
|-----------|-----|
| Youth-solvable in ~5 weeks | Yes — protocol + demo kit + drill, not national infrastructure |
| Climate adaptation (not mitigation cosplay) | Direct; SDG 13 / 11 / 10 |
| Uses existing national science | PAGASA colors + NOAH/HazardHunter maps + LGU DRRMO |
| Justice / inclusion | PWD roster, informal settler evacuation barriers, Tagalog-first |
| Demo-able at Innovation Showcase | Live drill video + laminated playbook + “alert → action” card |
| Complements Gargar | Optional dual lane: waste clog → flood co-benefit *and* early action |

### Product slice (what to actually ship)

1. **1-page sitio Flood Action Card (Filipino + English)**  
   - Yellow / Orange / Red + local river gauge meaning  
   - “Kapag ganito ang tubig sa [landmark], gawin ito”  
   - Go-bag mini checklist; emergency numbers; shelter pin  

2. **Inclusive household roster template (offline-first)**  
   - PWD type / mobility need / buddy name / carry-out time estimate  
   - Elderly, pregnant, infants; pets/livestock note  
   - Privacy rule: held by BHW + SK, not published online  

3. **SK drill script (90 minutes)**  
   - Map walk with NOAH/HazardHunter printout  
   - Siren → buddy → assembly → headcount  
   - Debrief: what failed (dark stairs, blocked alley, no light for deaf resident)  

4. **“After the siren” micro-video (≤60s)**  
   - One real barangay pathway; no stock footage  

5. **Campaign Canvas frame**  
   - Problem: warning ≠ action for those most exposed  
   - Solution: practiced protocol + inclusive roster  
   - Metric: e.g. ≥1 partner barangay drill completed; ≥N households with filled roster; % of PWD households with named buddy  
   - **Not claiming:** new forecast skill, national casualty reduction, or flood-control reform  

### Explicit non-goals
- Do not pitch a competing weather app to PAGASA/NOAH.  
- Do not promise siren hardware procurement as the “innovation.”  
- Do not require RA 12287 IRR completion to start a drill.  
- Do not frame informal settlers only as “risk” without agency and livelihood constraints.

### Partner path (realistic for Ateneo cohort)
- City/Municipal DRRMO + 1 barangay captain + SK chair  
- Optional: PDAO / OPD for deaf-friendly beacon checklist (Muntinlupa model as reference)  
- Optional: PRC chapter for EAP language  
- Science evidence lane: map interpretation (NOAH / HazardHunter), rainfall color pedagogy  
- Story lane: drill film + Canvas  

### If cohort still wants software
Ship a **static / offline PWA or printable generator** that turns barangay inputs into the Action Card + roster PDF—not a live alert feed. Live alerts remain PAGASA/NDRRMC’s job.

### Decision rule vs Gargar
| Choose **Gargar** if… | Choose **Baha Protocol** if… |
|------------------------|------------------------------|
| Cohort energy is circular economy + informal worker justice | Cohort energy is DRRM / SK / adaptation / inclusion |
| Easier Pasig collector network access | Easier DRRMO / flood barangay access |
| Measurable kg diversion is the showcase metric | Measurable drill + inclusive roster is the showcase metric |
| Want mitigation + flood co-benefit narrative | Want pure last-mile adaptation narrative |

Both can coexist as dual rack items; **do not dilute both into half-demos.**
