# R22 — Green Jobs Demand for Youth in the Philippines (Skills Gap, Circular Economy, Waste Formalization, TESDA 2020–2026)

**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Scope:** Philippines green-jobs demand for youth; skills gap; circular economy / waste-sector formalization; TESDA and related green-skills programs (2020–2026)  
**Method:** Secondary synthesis of DOLE/ILS, TESDA, ILO, World Bank S4YE, EDC, EPR/RA 11898 coverage, EU–PH Green Economy Partnership, and recent policy/media reporting  

---

## Key findings

### 1. Policy demand is real; delivery lag is the binding constraint

- **Green Jobs Act (RA 10771, 2016)** remains Asia’s pioneering green-jobs statute: it mandates skills identification, training, and certification for work that preserves or restores environmental quality *and* meets decent-work standards (fair income, rights, social protection, social dialogue).
- **National Green Jobs Human Resource Development Plan (NGJ HRD Plan) 2020–2030** is the operational roadmap (DOLE lead; TESDA + PRC co-creators). Released 2020; research update and strategic refresh advanced 2024–2025; formal launch of the updated plan mid-2025. Goals cluster around opportunity creation, skills systems, awareness, inclusive participation/social protection, and monitoring.
- **PDP 2023–2028** names green-job creation as a socio-economic priority. Updated NGJ HRD Plan work identifies **six high-growth green employment sectors**: (1) construction, (2) manufacturing (food + advanced), (3) sustainable agriculture, (4) transport, (5) energy/renewables, (6) waste and related environmental services.
- **Implementation gap:** Through 2023, green-goods/services assessment standards and enterprise incentives under RA 10771 were still unfinished; TESDA’s Green Technology Center (Taguig) was reported as under-staffed and under-utilized relative to its 2015 vision. Progress accelerated 2024–2026 (standards push, dual-training pacts, CE courses), but subnational rollout and private offtake remain uneven.

### 2. Demand signals: growing green employment, skills supply lagging

| Signal | Evidence (approx.) | Youth relevance |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------|
| Green share of employment | ~17% of total employment (ILO-based figure circa 2016; expected to grow) | Large “greening of existing jobs” surface, not only new niche roles |
| Green construction | ~211k jobs (2014) → ~440k (2024 est.); ~11% of construction employment | High volume for TVET-level trades + digital/green building skills |
| Renewable energy | ~90k–350k jobs by 2030 (range by scenario); solar + hydro dominate | Install, O&M, design pathways for young tech-voc and engineering grads |
| Global green-skills gap | LinkedIn: green-talent *demand* growing ~2× *supply* (recent years); gap projected wider to 2030 | Youth enter a market where credentials + demonstrated skill both matter |
| PH formal waste jobs (official) | ~19.7k workers; ~0.05% of employment (Jobs Group GLD snapshot) | Massive **undercount** of informal collectors/pickers/junkshop labor |

- TESDA’s own labor-market work (2018 baseline, still cited) projected **higher CAGR for green subsectors than conventional ones**, with construction the largest green employment pool. Solid waste & wastewater appeared as a priority HRD sector but with thin certified pipeline.
- **Skills Audit / industry feedback (incl. TESDA 2022 signals cited in later ILS work):** occupational shortages, missing green credentials, and **mismatch between training offerings and employer needs**. Cross-cutting gaps include regulatory literacy (waste, energy, buildings), sustainable business practices in traditional sectors, climate adaptation/mitigation literacy, and circular-economy know-how—plus soft skills (problem-solving, communication, entrepreneurship).

### 3. Skills gap specifics for Filipino youth

- **Definition fog:** Until green careers/skills databases and enterprise certification standards mature, workers (and youth) struggle to see “green” as a wage or mobility signal. TESDA specialists have reported low uptake for solid-waste qualifications historically (e.g., Garbage Collection NC I / Sanitary Landfill Operations with **zero enrollees** in early reporting years).
- **Trainer and capacity bottleneck:** Shortage of qualified green-technology trainers; public-sector pay limits expert retention. GTC focused early years more on master-trainer pipelines than mass youth throughput.
- **TVET greening progress (trajectory 2018→2025):**
  - ~2018: ~20 Training Regulations with green competency components.
  - ~2023 reporting: ~30% of ~300 TESDA courses “greened” (construction, PV, welding, automotive, housekeeping, tourism, ICT examples).
  - ~2025 case-study figure: green competencies embedded in **~108** training courses across agriculture, forestry, and other sectors.
- **What youth actually need in demand sectors:**
  - *Technical:* PV install/service, energy-efficient construction methods, RAC with responsible refrigerant practice, MRF/recycling operations, composting/organic waste, water-wastewater basics, e-waste safe handling.
  - *Circular / digital:* material ID and sorting standards, EPR documentation, diversion logging, price discovery for scrap, co-op and micro-enterprise operations.
  - *Just-transition / soft:* OSH, cooperative governance, negotiation with LGUs and producers, climate literacy without pure “certificate cosplay.”
- **Global youth preference vs capability:** Capgemini–UNICEF-type signals show high Gen Z *interest* in green jobs globally, but large shares feel under-skilled—mirrors PH pattern where aspiration outruns accessible, industry-linked pathways.

### 4. Circular economy jobs and the waste value chain

- **Scale of the materials problem:** PH generates on the order of **~2.7 million metric tons of plastic waste per year**; large shares leak to dumps, waterways, and ocean. Organic waste → landfill methane remains a mitigation + flood (basura–baha) co-benefit issue for Metro Manila and secondary cities.
- **EPR Act (RA 11898, 2022)** amends RA 9003 logic for plastic packaging: large enterprises must implement EPR programs. Progressive recovery targets commonly cited as **20% (2023) rising toward 80% (2028)**. Law language explicitly contemplates **partnership with LGUs, communities, junkshops, and the informal waste sector**.
- **Value-chain structure (youth job types):**
  1. **Upstream collection** — low entry barrier, high informality, high hazard, low pay; accessible to urban poor youth but rarely “decent work.”
  2. **Sorting / aggregation / junkshops** — semi-formal MSMEs; negotiation and quality grading skills matter.
  3. **MRFs, semi-reprocessors, recyclers** — more formal, higher skill, safer potential; still thin capacity outside major cities.
  4. **Manufacturing with secondary materials / redesign / EPR compliance roles** — professional and tech-voc growth edge.
- Official labor data show **Philippines waste employment heavily skewed to collection** (~91% collection vs sorting/reprocessing/manufacturing in cross-country GLD charts)—signaling **underdeveloped downstream formal circular jobs** and dependence on informal recovery.
- **E-waste:** studies estimate a large majority (often cited ~80%) handled informally with hazardous methods—another formalization + youth OSH training priority.
- **ILO circular-economy employment framing (global):** circular work is large, **>50% informal** in many estimates; Asia–Pacific hosts the bulk of informal circular workers. Skills systems must reach informal workers and youth, not only formal TVET graduates.
- **EU–PH Green Economy Partnership (~€60M / ~₱3.67B, 2023–2028):** DENR-led Global Gateway program on circular plastics, RE/efficiency, and **Green LGUs (SO2)** with explicit **youth and gender** inclusion. Creates LGU-level demand for diversion systems, co-ops, and local green enterprise—not only national certificates.

### 5. Waste-sector formalization: opportunity with decent-work risk

- **Why formalize:** Recognition (IDs, occupation status), stable offtake under EPR, PPE/OSH, social protection, bargaining power via cooperatives, pathway from scavenger stigma to “environmental service worker.” Quezon City–style registration/ID models are repeatedly cited as entry points.
- **DOLE–ILS field research (2023, multi-LGU zero-waste / transition sites):** Even where green waste practices expand jobs, **decent-work deficits dominate**—contractualization, missing hazard pay and insurance, unsafe conditions, narrow skills training, and **absent social dialogue** in waste planning. Transition without rights is not a just transition.
- **Risks of bad formalization:** Forced, top-down registration can destroy flexibility and household incomes for subsistence pickers. Recent circular e-waste research argues for **progressive / phased formalization**, economic-first interventions (buy-back, fair price), and co-ops that share compliance costs.
- **Youth angle in formalization:**
  - Young workers can enter **higher-skill nodes** (sorting standards, digital weighing/logging, MRF ops, EPR chain-of-custody apps, co-op bookkeeping) rather than only street picking.
  - Youth-led social enterprises can intermediate **price transparency** and **diversion metrics**—the missing glue between informal collectors and corporate EPR compliance.
  - Without deliberate inclusion, modernization (MRFs, producer take-back, private haulers) can **displace** informal youth instead of upgrading them.

### 6. TESDA and green-skills programs, 2020–2026 (timeline highlights)

| Period | Development |
|--------|-------------|
| Pre-2020 baseline | Green Jobs Act (2016); TESDA Green Technology Center established as green-TVET hub; early greening of TRs; LMIR “Green Skills for Green Jobs” (2018) |
| 2020 | DOLE releases **NGJ HRD Plan 2020–2030** |
| 2021–2022 | Pandemic slows standards/incentives work; Skills Audit signals shortages; EPR Act passes (2022) |
| 2023 | Context/TRF reporting: GTC underused; ~30% of courses greened; solid-waste courses struggle for applicants; standards still near finish line; CCC notes technology and awareness gaps for just transition |
| 2024 | DOLE–ILS research refresh on NGJ HRD Plan pathways; World Bank S4YE waste–youth brief; EPR implementation and LGU roles intensify |
| 2025 | Updated NGJ HRD Plan launch; **EDC Green Skills PH case study** (Opportunity 2.0-aligned) documents green competencies in ~108 courses, six priority sectors, master-trainer cascade via DepEd ALS + TESDA; **GPCCI–TESDA** pact on dual training / green skills incl. waste & circular economy; e-TESDA / GTC push **Developing Awareness on Circular Economy** and related free courses |
| 2025–2026 | Regional Green TVET (e.g., solar community-based training); EU–PH Green LGUs and youth circular exchanges active; tenth anniversary of Green Jobs Act (Apr 2026) used for advocacy |

- **TESDA instruments available to youth:** National Certificates with greened competencies; GTC short courses (PV, landscaping/hydroponics, e-vehicle servicing, waste recovery themes); e-TESDA online CE and environmental literacy modules; enterprise-based / dual training expansion via industry pacts.
- **Persistent weak spots:** waste qualifications still culturally and economically unattractive without linked **wages, PPE, and offtake**; green enterprise incentives under RA 10771 still harder for MSMEs to access than for large firms; subnational TESDA–PESO–LGU–producer coordination thin outside pilot cities.

### 7. Synthesis: where youth demand is sharpest (2026)

1. **Greened construction and RE install/O&M** — volume + clearer wage pathways.  
2. **Circular waste midstream** — sorting quality, MRF ops, scrap aggregation, EPR compliance support (high policy tailwind, high informality risk).  
3. **Organic waste / composting / methane diversion** — city climate + flood co-benefit; still under-productized.  
4. **Cross-cutting green work-readiness** — ALS/TVET integrations that pair soft skills with sector practice.  
5. **Not** pure “green certificate platforms” without employer offtake, plastic-credit flips, or utility-scale RE cosplay for student teams.

## YCLAP implication

YCLAP’s flagship logic (**Gargar scrap-to-value + diversion log; EcoWaste as evidence lane**) sits in the **highest-leverage youth green-jobs niche** the research supports: **circular midstream + just transition for informal waste workers**, not abstract “green jobs certs.”

| Research finding | Direct product / program implication |
|------------------|--------------------------------------|
| EPR needs informal inclusion but pricing/OSH/formalization lag | Build tools and pilots that **raise collector income transparency** and document **kg diverted**—compliance evidence *and* justice narrative |
| Official waste jobs undercount informal labor; decent-work deficits | Treat collectors as **primary users / partners**, not props; design for co-op or barangay registration pathways, not displacement |
| TESDA waste NCs historically empty; youth don’t feel employability gain | Prefer **demoable skill + offtake** (sellable scrap, LGU diversion numbers, EPR partner story) over certificate chasing alone; if linking TESDA, pair with real junkshop/MRF hours |
| Green skills gap is technical *and* cross-cutting | Cohort lanes should mix **ops skill** (sort grades, safety, logs) with **mobilize/story** (LGU, EPR, RA 9003/11898 literacy) |
| EU–PH Green LGUs + NGJ HRD Plan momentum 2025–2028 | Pitch outcomes in **LGU SWM / circular economy language** and DOLE just-transition language—not only campus sustainability |
| Construction/RE absorb more formal green jobs | Optional adjacent projects OK, but **do not abandon waste**—it is where Metro Manila youth can ship climate mitigation + flood co-benefit + justice in one pilot |
| Overhyped traps (carbon credits, plastic neutrality, cert platforms without demand) | Stay aligned with existing deep-research brief: **metrics in kg and pesos for workers**, not tokenized greenwashing |

**Program design tips for Aug–Sep 2026 cohort**

1. Map **one barangay or campus scrap flow** → price board → diversion log (Gargar core).  
2. Interview **1–2 junkshops + 1 informal collector group**; log OSH and pay realities (ILS findings as interview guide).  
3. Cite **RA 11898 partnership clause + RA 9003 + Green Jobs Act decent-work definition** in Campaign Canvas legal frame.  
4. If partnering TESDA/GTC or e-TESDA CE modules, use them as **skill modules inside a live offtake pilot**, not as the product.  
5. Success metric for “green jobs”: **number of youth hours in safer, higher-value circular tasks + kg diverted + ₱ improved for partner collectors**—not certificates issued.

**SDG frame:** 8 (decent work), 12 (SCP/circular), 13 (climate), 11 (cities); just transition as cross-cut.
