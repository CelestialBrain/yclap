# R38 — Campaign Canvas & climate campaign design for youth

**Scope:** Campaign Canvas / climate campaign design best practice for youth cohorts — problem–solution–beneficiary–channel–metric spine; lean/climate canvas variants used in accelerators and social-change design sprints.  
**Flagship application:** YCLAP Campaign Canvas (Gargar pilot + EcoWaste) for Youth CLAP Innovation Showcase.  
**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Does not claim:** A single universal “climate campaign canvas” standard; Eloqua/Braze marketing-automation “campaign canvas” tools (CRM orchestration, not social-change design).

---

## Key findings

### 1. Two different “canvases” get confused — only one fits YCLAP

| Family | Origin | Purpose | Best for |
|--------|--------|---------|----------|
| **Campaign Canvas (social change)** | Mobilisation Lab (MobLab); Commons Library | One-page campaign strategy: vision → change theory → people → story → asks → metrics | Youth climate *campaigns* and advocacy sprints |
| **Lean / Social Lean Canvas** | Ash Maurya Lean Canvas; Social Lean Canvas (Yeoman / Moskovitz / Ākina) | One-page venture model: problem, solution, segment, channel, metric, cost/revenue + impact model | Youth climate *ventures*, social enterprise pilots, accelerator demos |
| **Climate Action Canvas** | Alina Adams (Stanford LEAD climate group lineage) | Business climate strategy: objective, climate risk, impact on climate, climate action | Corporate / climate-tech *strategy*, not student campaigning |
| **Marketing Campaign Canvas** | Eloqua, Braze, Adobe Journey Optimizer | Multi-step marketing automation workflows | Irrelevant to YCLAP (name collision only) |

**Practical rule for Youth CLAP:** Use **Campaign Canvas language** for the showcase story and mentor pack; borrow **Lean/Social Lean** discipline for problem–beneficiary–channel–metric falsifiability of the pilot product. Do not submit an Eloqua-style journey map.

### 2. MobLab Campaign Canvas — the social-change gold standard

Mobilisation Lab’s **Campaign Canvas** (part of the **Campaign Accelerator Toolkit**, CC BY 4.0) is the most cited one-page template for people-powered campaigns. Print as poster; fill with sticky notes; revisit after real-world input.

**Thirteen blocks (MobLab / Commons Library):**

1. **Vision** — long-term future the campaign wants  
2. **What needs to change?** — problem diagnosis  
3. **How can we create that change?** — system disrupt / culture change / institutions / amplify alternatives  
4. **Who can help?** — influence, affected people, collaborators  
5. **What influences?** — trends, relationships, events that sustain or shift the status quo  
6. **Goals + objectives** — specific project goal and steps  
7. **Outcomes + indicators** — what success looks like; how you know  
8. **What’s the story?** — new narrative elements  
9. **What do people need to do?** — audience/ally asks  
10. **What do we need to do?** — team activities that enable action  
11. **Assumptions** — why the plan should work  
12. **Risks** — failure modes, resistance, safety  
13. **Tracking & measuring** — progress toward objectives  

**Campaign Accelerator process (5 modules, design-thinking hybrid):** Define → Sense → Create → Prototype & Test → Plan & Pitch. The canvas is introduced in **Define** alongside problem diagram, context map, system map, and iceberg (root-cause) tools. Core method: people at the centre, field research with audiences, rapid prototype of tactics, then a plan with success indicators — not a 40-page strategy deck.

**Related canvases in the same ecosystem:**

- **Movement Building Canvas** (Social Change Agency) — shared identity, journey, enablers for multi-org movements (heavier than a 5-week pilot).  
- **Theory of Change Canvas** (School of Social Impact / Ganz-style templates) — “If X, then Y, because…” power pathway.  
- **Magic Boxes / Campaign Strategy Planning Template / Log frame** — alternate one-pagers; useful if MobLab layout feels crowded.

### 3. Problem–solution–beneficiary–channel–metric (the spine accelerators actually score)

Across MobLab, CARE youth advocacy, Lean Canvas, and Social Lean Canvas, high-performing youth climate designs force the same five answers early:

| Block | Campaign language | Lean / venture language | Youth climate best practice |
|-------|-------------------|-------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Problem** | What needs to change; root cause (iceberg / problem tree) | Top 1–3 customer problem + existing alternative | **Local, human, quantified** — not “climate change is bad.” Name the friction (e.g. opaque scrap rates) and the system effect (landfill methane, basura→baha). |
| **Solution** | How we create change; key activities + audience asks | Product/service that delivers UVP | **One working loop**, not a slogan. Prototype and test with real users (MobLab Module 4). Bind solution late; problem first. |
| **Beneficiary** (who gains) | Affected people + allies + decision targets | Customer segment + early adopter; impact “participants” | Split **user** (who acts) vs **beneficiary** (who gains) vs **target** (who has power to change policy). For justice campaigns, make informal workers visible — not erased. |
| **Channel** | Where audiences are reached; engagement pyramid | Marketing + distribution channel | Meet people where they already gather: campus orgs, barangay SK, dorm chats, TikTok/IG Reels for awareness; **face-to-face** for power targets. Youth toolkits stress multi-channel but **one primary conversion path**. |
| **Metric** | Indicators + tracking | Key metrics / impact ladder (short → long term) | SMART goals; **falsifiable pilot numbers** (users, kg, partners) over unfalsifiable tCO₂e. Track both **output** (posts, booths) and **outcome** (behavior, diversion, policy ask accepted). |

**CARE’s eight-step advocacy cycle** (used in Global Center on Adaptation *Toolkit for Youth on Adaptation & Leadership*, Module 7) maps cleanly onto this spine:

1. Identify issue (problem tree)  
2. Context (PESTLE)  
3. Goals & objectives (SMART) + Theory of Change  
4. Who to persuade (primary decision-maker vs secondary influencer)  
5. Policy asks + key message  
6. Resource map  
7. Implement tactics  
8. Monitor & evaluate  

**Youth-specific tactic pattern (GCA / Plan International / UNICEF climate activist kits):** problem analysis before content production; primary targets named *as people*, not “the Ministry”; mix of op-eds, policy briefs, petitions, social media, local rallies *only if safe*; social platforms (TikTok/Reels) for reach, **in-person power mapping** for wins.

### 4. Lean Canvas family — what accelerators teach climate startups

**Lean Canvas (Ash Maurya, from Osterwalder BMC)** — nine blocks optimized for uncertainty:

1. Problem (+ existing alternative)  
2. Customer segment (+ early adopter)  
3. Unique value proposition  
4. Solution  
5. Channel  
6. Revenue stream  
7. Cost structure  
8. Key metric  
9. Unfair advantage  

Maurya’s deliberate design: the **solution box is small** so teams do not fall in love with features; problem–segment pair is filled first; key metrics replace “key resources.” Youth climate accelerators (e.g. sessions reporting Lean Canvas models for youth climate action; social-enterprise bootcamps; IRENA NewGen-type climate-tech programs) use this as the default **venture** sheet.

**Social Lean Canvas** (Yeoman, Moskovitz, Ākina Foundation; adapted from Maurya + Osterwalder; CC BY-SA 3.0 Unported lineage) adds:

- **Purpose** — inspirational, simple, high-level, rooted in a fundamental social/environmental situation  
- **Impact model ladder** — Issue → Participant → Activity → Short-term outcome → Medium-term outcome → Long-term outcome → Impact  
- Explicit split: **business model** (how money works) vs **impact model** (how activities create lasting change)  
- Customer types may include **non-paying user, government funder, sponsor** — critical for youth pilots that are not SaaS  

**Lean Impact / impact-entrepreneur variants** (Good Tribe Lean Impact Entrepreneur BMC; various “Lean Impact Startup Canvas” write-ups) stress:

- Dual segments: **funder + beneficiary**  
- Jobs-to-be-done + current alternatives for each  
- MVP key activities tied to measurable social/environmental outcome  
- Avoid treating “impact” as a tenth decorative box; integrate into the model  

**Use in accelerators:** Ākina/BPP materials and OECD OPSI list Social Lean Canvas as a toolkit for validating offerings and prioritising what to develop. SEntrA and Youth Business International-type youth social-entrepreneurship programs teach BMC/Lean for green/social ventures. Climate-specific accelerators often combine Lean Canvas week-1 with impact measurement (IRIS+, carbon footprint, or simple diversion logs).

### 5. Climate-specific canvases and analysis frames

**Climate Action Canvas (Alina Adams)** — four parts for *business* climate strategy (not youth campaigns):

1. Climate business objective  
2. Risk to business (physical + transition; financial metric)  
3. Impact on climate (e.g. carbon footprint, brand effect)  
4. Climate action (mitigation/adaptation plan linked back to objective)  

Useful when a cohort member’s idea is a climate-tech or campus operations product; weak for pure advocacy.

**Four Stages of Climate Action (Nicky Ison)** — diagnostic for *institutional targets*, not the campaign team:

| Stage | Signal | Campaign implication |
|-------|--------|----------------------|
| Denial | Deny science / block solutions | Consequence + public legitimacy work |
| Delay | Admit problem; long targets; greenwash; “gas as transition” | Expose delay discourses; force short-term asks |
| Diversification | Both fossil and climate solutions | Champions + ambition loop; solutions-first framing |
| Decarbonisation | Science-aligned phase-out | Defend progress; raise ambition further |

Youth campaigns targeting universities, LGUs, or brands can place the target on this ladder and choose tactics accordingly (e.g. campus admin in *delay* needs short-term measurable commitments, not another 2050 pledge).

**Ambition loop:** public support ↔ policy ambition ↔ visible projects — a positive feedback frame for solutions-based youth campaigns (WWF-Australia lineage via Ison).

### 6. Youth climate campaign design — what works in practice

Synthesised from MobLab, GCA youth toolkit, Climate Action Reserve advocacy tips, Commons Library climate tools, UNDP youth engagement guidance, and observed youth climate accelerator practice:

1. **Cut the issue** — Climate is too big. “Bite-sized” campaign focus (MobLab / Rose): one norm, policy, or practice you can move in a defined window.  
2. **Root cause over symptom** — Problem tree / iceberg: event → pattern → structure → mental model. Student waste campaigns that stop at “people are lazy” fail; ones that surface *opacity, missing infrastructure, and power* can design real solutions.  
3. **Power map before content** — Spectrum of allies; primary vs secondary targets; Circles of Commitment / Engagement Pyramid (crowd → congregation → committed → core).  
4. **Theory of Change in one sentence** — *If* [audience does X via our channel], *then* [behavior/policy shifts], *because* [mechanism]. Ganz: clear ToC is a prerequisite to an effective campaign.  
5. **Story + ask** — New narrative (what we want people to believe) paired with a concrete ask (what we want them to *do*). Awareness without ask is content, not campaign.  
6. **Prototype tactics** — MobLab insists on testing messages/actions with real audiences before scale. Youth mistake: launch TikTok first, strategy never.  
7. **Safety and civic space** — GCA toolkit: know rights before protest; some contexts make high-risk tactics inappropriate. Digital + local education may be the only safe path.  
8. **Metrics that mentors trust** — Output (reach, events) + outcome (sign-ups, kg, policy meetings held) + learning (what failed). Avoid carbon-credit cosplay and unmeasured planetary claims.  
9. **Justice as design, not add-on** — Beneficiaries who already work the system (informal collectors, frontline communities) must appear in the canvas, not only middle-class student “saviors.”  
10. **Afterlife** — 30–90 day continuity (org handoff, SEEDS/AIS, partner window) belongs on the canvas under activities/risks, or the pilot dies at showcase.

### 7. Mapping canvas families onto YCLAP’s five fields

| YCLAP field | MobLab Campaign Canvas | Social Lean Canvas | Climate Action Canvas |
|-------------|------------------------|--------------------|------------------------|
| **Problem** | What needs to change + influences | Problem + existing alternative + purpose issue | Risk + impact on climate |
| **Solution** | How we create change + team activities | Solution + UVP + unfair advantage | Climate action plan |
| **Beneficiary** | Who can help (affected + influencers) | Customer segment + impact participants | Stakeholders in objective |
| **Channel** | What people need to do + where engaged | Channel (marketing focus) | (Implicit in action plan) |
| **Metric** | Outcomes, indicators, tracking | Key metrics + impact ladder | Risk/impact KPIs (e.g. footprint) |

**Recommended hybrid for a 5-week youth climate pilot (YCLAP-shaped):**

- Front of sheet: **problem, climate framing (mitigation / adaptation co-benefit / justice / non-claims), audience table, SMART goal, theory of change, offer, tactics, ask, metrics, risks, afterlife** — already the shape of `docs/campaign-canvas.md`.  
- Optional reverse / appendix: **Social Lean** impact ladder if the project is venture-shaped; **power map** if advocacy-shaped.  
- Skip corporate Climate Action Canvas unless the team is pitching campus operations or a climate-tech product with real financial model.

### 8. Common failure modes (youth climate canvases)

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Slogan solution | “Raise awareness” as the whole plan | Force a behavior or policy ask + channel |
| Everyone is the audience | “Youth / public / government” | One early adopter + one power target |
| Metric theater | Only likes, followers, or speculative tCO₂e | Pilot-falsifiable: users, kg, partners, meetings |
| Problem after product | App features listed first | Rewrite problem until a stranger feels the pain |
| Erased beneficiaries | Collectors / communities as backdrop | Put them in audience table with a need and channel |
| No assumptions/risks | Canvas looks perfect | MobLab blocks 11–12: what must be true; what breaks |
| Canvas as homework | Filled once, never updated | Sticky-note poster; revise after each pilot week |
| Tool confusion | Marketing automation canvas screenshots | Stick to MobLab / Lean / Social Lean only |

## YCLAP canvas tips

### A. Keep the existing Campaign Canvas spine; tighten five fields

`docs/campaign-canvas.md` already matches best practice. For mentor (Ms Vicky Tan) and showcase reviewers, make these five fields unmissable:

1. **Problem** — One local friction + one system effect (already: rate opacity + landfill/flood). Keep ≤5 lines.  
2. **Solution / offer** — Name the product loop (rates → directory → diversion log), not a feature list.  
3. **Beneficiary** — Explicitly three rows: student user, household near node, **informal collector/junkshop** (justice). Mentors punish “youth save the planet” without workers.  
4. **Channel** — One primary conversion path per segment (e.g. BOx/MEcO for campus; in-person verification for junkshop). Social is secondary, not the plan.  
5. **Metric** — SMART pilot targets already set (≥30 user, ≥3 collector, ≥100 kg or honest proxy). Label **non-claims** next to climate metrics so judges do not hear carbon-credit pitch.

### B. Theory of Change (one line, Ganz/CARE form)

Keep and stress-test:

> *If* people can see the price and reach a collector in one flow, *then* they sort and divert more recyclables, *because* friction and opacity — not values — are the binding constraint.

If a field week falsifies this (e.g. people know rates but still do not sort), rewrite the canvas before the pitch.

### C. Fill order for cohort workshops (MobLab + Maurya hybrid)

1. Problem + who is hurt (15 min sticky notes)  
2. Existing alternative (what people do today)  
3. Early adopter (not “all of Ateneo”)  
4. Offer / UVP in ≤12 words  
5. Channel for that early adopter only  
6. Key metric that would falsify success in 5 weeks  
7. Climate framing stack + non-claims  
8. Risks + assumptions  
9. Ask (mentor feedback / partner / 90-day next step)

Do **not** start with logo, SDG wallpaper, or social media calendar.

### D. Accelerator-style dual canvas (when useful)

| When team is… | Use |
|---------------|-----|
| Showcase campaign / advocacy | MobLab Campaign Canvas blocks (vision → tracking) |
| Product pilot (Gargar) | Lean / Social Lean for problem–segment–channel–metric + impact ladder |
| Both (YCLAP default) | Campaign Canvas as **public mentor sheet**; Lean blocks as **internal Builder QA** |

Social Lean **Purpose** line for Gargar (example shape):  
*Make scrap value visible so diversion is easy and fair for households and informal collectors — cutting residual waste that feeds landfills and floods.*

### E. Metrics discipline (mentor-safe)

| Prefer | Avoid |
|--------|--------|
| Users who priced material; log entry; kg diverted; collectors verified in person | Unaudited tCO₂e, “plastic neutral,” national flood reduction |
| SMART date (e.g. by 12 Sep 2026) | “Raise awareness this semester” |
| Output + outcome pair | Vanity reach alone |
| Proxy + plan if 100 kg slips | Silent failure or invented numbers |

### F. Climate framing on the canvas (three layers + non-claims)

Copy the layered table pattern:

- **Mitigation** — diversion → less landfill methane load (NDC waste sector co-benefit story)  
- **Adaptation co-benefit** — less basura in drains  
- **Justice** — price transparency + fair path for informal recovery  
- **Not claiming** — carbon credit product, national flood-control reform, plastic-neutrality offsets  

This matches Climate Action Reserve-style advocacy clarity (issue → entity → power analysis → ask) without corporate Climate Action Canvas overhead.

### G. Channel mix for Metro Manila youth pilot

| Segment | Primary channel | Secondary |
|---------|-----------------|-----------|
| Ateneo student / org | Org chat, booth, BOx / MEcO / AIS | IG Reels |
| Household / dorm near node | SK, sari-sari, dorm flyer, collector handoff window | Barangay FB |
| Junkshop / MRF | In-person visit, EcoWaste directory verify | Call before “book” claims |
| Mentor / CCC showcase | Canvas + demo + 3-min pitch | Exhibit board |

Rule: **no phone/collector claim without field verification** (already a canvas risk row — keep it).

### H. Five-week cadence (Campaign Accelerator lite)

| Week | Focus | Canvas update |
|------|-------|---------------|
| 1 | Define problem + early adopter | Problem, audience, ToC |
| 2 | Sense (talk to 5 users + 2 collectors) | Assumptions, channel |
| 3 | Prototype loop (rate → directory → log) | Offer, solution |
| 4 | Test + measure | Metrics actuals vs targets |
| 5 | Plan & pitch | Risks, ask, afterlife |

### I. What *not* to put on the YCLAP canvas

- Eloqua / Braze multi-step marketing automation diagrams  
- Full Movement Building Canvas (overkill for 5 weeks)  
- Corporate Scope 1–3 inventory as the pilot KPI  
- SDG icon strip without a primary SDG (use **12** primary; 13, 11 secondary)  
- “Everyone” as beneficiary  

### J. Pitch–canvas consistency

R39 (3-min pitch) and the Campaign Canvas must share the same problem sentence, same pilot numbers, and same non-claims. Judges score honesty under time pressure; a canvas that claims 1,000 kg while the pitch says 100 kg kills trust.
