# R14 — Katipunan corridor & Ateneo de Manila: heat, microclimate, green space, student mobility exposure, campus climate action (2020–2026)

**Wave:** 3 (agent deep research)  
**Geography:** Katipunan Avenue / C-5 (Loyola Heights), Ateneo de Manila University Loyola Heights campus, adjacent QC institutional green (Miriam College, UP Diliman)  
**Window:** Primary evidence 2020–2026; selected pre-2020 anchors where still used in 2020s literature  
**Date compiled:** 2026-08-07

## Key findings

### 1. Metro Manila / Quezon City heat context is acute; 2024 heatwaves made it a student-systems issue

- Metro Manila ranks among the Philippines’ highest heat-health risk cities; Quezon City has been ranked ~15th nationally on heat health risk index (HHRI) metrics used in national comparative work (Estoque et al. lineage cited in 2025 QC studies).
- Hot–dry season (March–May) is the critical exposure window. In 2024, Metro Manila recorded extreme air temperatures (public reports of ~38.8 °C historic highs) and heat indices in the PAGASA “danger” band (~42–51 °C range), driving widespread class suspensions and shifts to online/asynchronous modalities across the capital region and nationally.
- School heat closures are now a recurring public-health + education disruption mechanism (2023–2025 seasons), not a one-off anomaly—relevant for any campus that still depends on outdoor/last-mile travel for daily access.

### 2. Katipunan–Loyola Heights sits in a *relative cooling zone* within QC, driven by campus green mass—not by the highway itself

- **2025 heat-risk mapping (Treyes, *Environmental Research: Climate*):** A barangay-level Heat Risk Index (HRI) for Quezon City (hot–dry seasons 2020–2024) found **~81% of barangays (≈70% of population) in high-risk HRI (0.61–0.80)**. High-risk clusters concentrate in dense south/southwest QC (e.g., Manresa mean HRI 0.731). Intraurban heat islets (LST threshold method) cover **~31%** of the study area and strongly overlap high-HRI barangays.
- The same study explicitly lists **Ateneo de Manila University and Miriam College campuses** among the city’s prominent green spaces that **provide significant cooling effects and lower HRI in adjacent areas**—alongside Quezon Memorial Circle, Veterans Memorial Golf Course, Ninoy Aquino Parks and Wildlife Center, and UP Diliman/Arboretum.
- **2019 geospatial UHI work on QC** (cited in campus journalism): central-eastern barangays around UP Campus and Loyola Heights showed **lower UHI strength** despite heavy student footfall—attributed to university green + park mosaics, vs. industrial/commercial southwest QC.
- **Implication:** Loyola Heights is a **cool island / heat refuge** at city scale, but **Katipunan Avenue (C-5)** itself is a high-impervious, car-priority corridor that **breaks** that refuge for anyone moving *between* campus gates, transit, malls, and dorms.

### 3. Ateneo green infrastructure is a measurable microclimate asset (~½–⅔ of campus as green/open space)

| Asset | Best available figure | Role for heat |
|-------|----------------------|---------------|
| Loyola Heights campus area | ~89–90 ha | Institutional land use island inside dense QC |
| Green / open space share | **~½ to ⅔** of campus (AIS official: two-thirds of 89 ha; campus journalism often cites ~half of 90 ha) | Shade, lower surface heat, infiltration, biodiversity |
| Tree inventory | AIS geo-tagged **1,809 trees** (SY 2025–2026, Loyola Heights) | Canopy / shade stock for pedestrian comfort |
| Arboretum of Threatened Philippine Trees | **101** endemic / threatened Philippine trees | Biodiversity + education + local cooling pocket |
| Wildlife Sanctuary, pollinator pockets, open fields (e.g., Bellarmine Field) | Ongoing AIS biodiversity program | Microclimate + wellbeing + education |

- Since ~2016, AIS leadership has publicly described **avoiding concrete paving** where possible because concrete absorbs/reflects heat more than soil and vegetation—trading muddy shoes in rain for lower heat trapping and flood contribution.
- Campus green is **not infinite**: development pressure (roads, buildings) repeatedly collides with forest patches (e.g., past SOM Forest road-widening pushback). Cooling services depend on **retention and canopy quality**, not brand alone.

### 4. Student mobility heat exposure is structural: car-centric Katipunan + thin shade + long outdoor last mile

- **Corridor character (Ateneo Institute of Sustainability feature, 2022):** Post-pandemic return to onsite classes refilled Katipunan; congestion worsened on C-5. Sidewalks are discontinuous, often used as parking; bike lanes are invaded by motorcycles/buses; high-rise construction has **narrowed** pedestrian space. Street lighting is sparse at night—compounding safety with heat-related day risks.
- **Pedestrian infrastructure deficit:** Across the **~3.38 km** C-5 Katipunan stretch from Ateneo to UP, only **seven footbridges** serve crossing—forcing long detours or unsafe at-grade crossings. Move As One / AltMobility PH have proposed at-grade crossings, refuge islands, road diets, protected bike lanes, BRT-style median operations, and **more green space** on the corridor.
- **Ateneo Traffic Group (2012 report, still cited in 2020s discourse):** Ateneo was estimated to contribute **~20%** of Katipunan congestion then; recommended walkable Katipunan, better LRT-2 access, dorms, carpool, and shuttles. Partial adoption (campus shuttles, drop-offs, some sidewalks) has **not** solved corridor congestion.
- **Heat exposure pathway for students (synthesis, limited direct sensors on Katipunan):**
  1. **Last-mile walk / wait** in full sun on asphalt/sidewalk (jeepney, UV Express, bus, LRT-2 Katipunan station transfers).
  2. **Queueing / loading** without shade or cooling (hot–dry season heat index danger days).
  3. **Crossing C-5** via long footbridge climbs (extra metabolic heat) or dangerous at-grade runs.
  4. **Relief on campus** once inside the green mass—creating a **sharp microclimate gradient**: hot corridor → cool campus. That gradient means **exposure is front-loaded on the commute**, not evenly distributed through the school day for those who live off-campus.
- Direct peer-reviewed **Katipunan sidewalk WBGT / mean radiant temperature** time series for students was **not found** in public sources for 2020–2026; exposure arguments currently rest on (a) city LST/HRI maps, (b) mobility infrastructure audits, (c) national school-heat disruption evidence. **This is a measurement gap**, not evidence of low risk.

### 5. Campus sustainability & climate actions (2020–2026) — strong on energy/governance; thinner on *corridor* heat adaptation

**Governance & planning**

- **Laudato Si’ University pathway:** Commitment to become a Laudato Si’ university (target window articulated toward **2029**); Integral Ecology as Lux Domino 2030 priority.
- **Climate Action Plan (CAP):** Core Working Group convened **2024** (chaired with leadership from Fr. Jose Ramon Villarin SJ / Manila Observatory link; AIS as secretariat). CAP covers mitigation, adaptation, and climate-positive behavior; public launch targeted with **Biodiversity Action Plan** around Intersession **2025**.
- **Sustainable University Master Plan (Board of Trustees, 15 Nov 2025; Memo U2526-070, 12 Feb 2026):** Seven principles; seven-part definition / **22 end states**; **55 targets** (short 2027 / medium 2030 / long 2050). Feeds CAP, Biodiversity Action Plan, Land Use Master Plan. Access: go.ateneo.edu/SustainableAteneo.
- **UNFCCC Race to Zero:** University listed as participant (SDG 7 reporting, updated into **2025**).

**Energy / carbon (mitigation-heavy)**

- **Ateneo Solar Power Project (launched 2020):** By **2025**, solar on five key buildings (Arete, New Rizal Library / First Pacific Hall, PLDT-CTC/JGSOM, Senior High School Main, Grade School Covered Courts)—combined **~695.50 kWp**, **~725,072 kWh/yr** (~**6%** of campus energy needs in reported figures).
- **May 2024 RE supply agreement** with SolX Technologies + Shell Energy Philippines: transition of Loyola Heights **grid electricity toward 100% renewable supply**, described as **~6 years ahead** of **2030 carbon neutrality** goal. Reported 2024 mix: grid renewable contract covering large kWh share; combined with on-site solar, **~87.8%** of total electricity consumption from renewable sources in that reporting year (exact scope/definitions should be re-checked in CAP GHG inventory).

**Biodiversity / green (adaptation co-benefit)**

- Continuous AIS campus biodiversity program: arboretum, wildlife sanctuary framing, pollinator pockets, citizen science (Ateneo Wild), bird-window-strike mitigation, community cat program, Biodiversity Action Plan process aligned with CAP.
- **Cooling is a co-benefit of biodiversity policy**, not always framed as a quantified heat KPI in public materials.

**Mobility (relevant but incomplete for heat)**

- Discourse and proposals for sustainable Katipunan mobility (2022+): road diets, protected active transport, more crossings, green corridor thinking (AltMobility PH / Move As One; student/engineering social media redesigns).
- Campus-side: shuttles, drop-off rationalization, walkability language in older ATG work—**corridor redesign remains multi-stakeholder** (DPWH/LGU/C-5, universities, commercial strip, residential towers).

**What is still thin publicly**

- Campus **outdoor thermal comfort standards** (shade targets, cool materials, hydration points at gates).
- **Measured** student commute heat dose (time-in-sun, WBGT on Katipunan segments).
- Formal **heat-health SOPs** for extreme heat index days that cover *commuters at the gate*, not only indoor class modality.
- Binding **Katipunan green corridor** delivery with QC LCCAP Strategy 5 (nature-based solutions, streetside vegetation, green corridors)—campuses are cooling *nodes*; the avenue is still the weak link.

### 6. City policy hook: QC Enhanced LCCAP 2021–2050

- Treyes (2025) maps HRI as decision support for **QC Enhanced Local Climate Change Action Plan 2021–2050**, especially **Strategy 5 (nature-based solutions)**—pocket parks, rooftop/vertical gardens, **expanded streetside vegetation**, green corridors connecting fragmented green, native/drought-resilient trees.
- Katipunan between Ateneo–Miriam–UP is a natural **green-corridor candidate**: three large institutional cool islands already exist; the missing piece is **continuous shade + safe active transport on the right-of-way**.

## YCLAP campus angle

**Why this site fits YCLAP (youth climate action / product lens)**

1. **Lived heat injustice is spatial:** Many students enjoy a cooler campus microclimate but still take a **hot, car-hostile last mile** on Katipunan. That is a clear problem statement for campaigns, design sprints, and measurement pilots without needing national policy capture.
2. **Evidence stack already exists for storytelling:** QC HRI maps (2025), campus green metrics (AIS), mobility redesigns (Move As One / AltMobility), 2024 school-heat closures—enough for a **Campaign Canvas + demo** without inventing science.
3. **Cooling equity is under-productized:** YCLAP deep brief already flags **heat / cooling equity** as high-leverage and under-addressed for Metro Manila innovators. Katipunan is a concrete, walkable (ironically, *not* walkable) pilot geography for student teams.
4. **University is a willing institutional partner in theory:** CAP, SUMP, Biodiversity Action Plan, AIS, Laudato Si’ framing, Race to Zero—students can **plug into existing targets** (shade, trees, mobility, adaptation behavior) rather than invent a parallel sustainability brand.
5. **Avoid overclaim:** RE procurement and solar are **mitigation wins**; they do **not** by themselves cool a sidewalk at 1:30 PM. YCLAP projects should **not** sell “carbon neutrality” as the heat solution for commuters.

**High-fit student project directions (non-exhaustive)**

| Direction | What it does | Metrics that matter |
|-----------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Katipunan heat walk audit** | Low-cost sensors / phone-based MRT proxy + shade mapping from gate to LRT-2 / jeepney nodes | °C / WBGT spots, % route shaded, wait-time in sun |
| **Cool corridor prototype** | Temporary shade sails, misting, hydration, tree-pit advocacy at 1–2 hotspots with LGU/barangay | Peak pedestrian comfort delta; adoption days |
| **Gate heat-health protocol** | Student + admin SOP for PAGASA danger days (water, delayed outdoor activities, shaded queueing) | Compliance rate; heat illness near-miss log |
| **Campus–corridor canopy join** | Align AIS tree inventory / pollinator logic with Katipunan streetside planting proposals under QC LCCAP | Trees planted + 2-year survival; continuous canopy meters |
| **Active mobility × heat** | Protected walk/bike segment pilot that **includes** shade (not bike paint alone) | Mode share trial; perceived thermal comfort |
| **Cool-island communication** | Map “where heat hits” for freshmen / commuters; counter green-gentrification naïveté | Reach; behavior change (leave earlier, hydrate, shade route) |

**Product / campaign constraints (YCLAP discipline)**

- Prefer **adaptation + co-benefit** (shade, mobility safety, health) over pure carbon-credit or RE cosplay.
- Partner with **AIS, AGILA / Ateneo Wild, Move As One, barangay Loyola Heights / adjacent**, and QC environment/climate offices—not only university marketing.
- Measure **people-minutes in dangerous heat index**, not only “trees planted” vanity counts.
- Design for **all corridor users** (Ateneo + Miriam + UP + residents + workers), or the project collapses into gated-campus comfort.

**One-line YCLAP thesis**

> *Ateneo’s green campus is a proven cool island in a high-heat city; Katipunan is the exposure gap. Close the gap with shade + safe mobility + heat-health practice—aligned to CAP/SUMP and QC LCCAP—not with another indoor sustainability slogan.*
