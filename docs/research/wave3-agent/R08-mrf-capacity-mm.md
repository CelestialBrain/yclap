# R08 — Materials Recovery Facilities (MRFs) in Metro Manila: Capacity, Utilization, Failures, and City Cases (2020–2026)

**Scope:** Metro Manila (NCR) MRF stock, throughput, utilization gaps, common failure modes; deep cases for Quezon City, Pasig, Marikina, and City of Manila; window ~2020–2026 with earlier baseline where needed.  
**Method:** World Bank Metro Manila SWM assessment (2022), MMDA Master Plan, EMB-NCR / NSWMC / COA / PSA counts, ADB MRF toolkit, LGU eco-profiles and ordinances, secondary peer-reviewed/policy reviews (2025–2026).  
**Status:** Research agent note for YCLAP-Gargar (wave 3). Not an engineering inventory.

---

## Key findings

### 1. Metro Manila capacity is thin and mostly “shed-scale”

| Indicator | Figure | Year / source |
|-----------|--------|----------------|
| Barangays in Metro Manila | 1,710 | World Bank 2022 |
| Barangays with own MRF | **334 (~20%)** | World Bank 2022 (same figure repeated in 2025–2026 policy reviews) |
| Barangay MRFs (count) | ~335 | WB Table 7 |
| Centralized (city-scale) MRFs | **12–13** | WB: Caloocan 1, Mandaluyong 1, Marikina 1, Muntinlupa 2, Parañaque 1, Pasay 1, Pasig 5–6 |
| Typical barangay MRF floor area | **20–100 m²** (EMB-funded often ~50 m²) | World Bank 2022 |
| Centralized MRF footprint | ~**200–1,000 m²** | World Bank / Andaya et al. 2026 |
| Manual processing norm | **~1–5 tpd** mixed waste (conveyor-aided semi-auto possible) | ADB Materials Recovery Facility Tool Kit |
| Design range (PH toolkit) | **&lt;1 tpd** (small barangay) to **~200 tpd** (large city) | ADB toolkit |
| National MRF stock | 10,722 (~33% of barangays, NSWMC ~2020); 11,637 serving 39% (COA 2021); **12,855** (+8.7% YoY, PSA 2024) | NSWMC / COA / PSA |

**Utilization is the weak variable.** World Bank states explicitly that **outputs of MRFs and Materials Recovery Systems (MRS) are not known** and cannot be properly evaluated. Capacity is often **unspecified on paper**, so “number of MRFs” is a coverage proxy, not a throughput metric. Persistent **sorting on collection trucks and at disposal sites** is treated as evidence of a structural **recycling gap**: Level-2 MRF/MRS recovery is incomplete relative to generation.

Metro Manila generation is on the order of **~9,500 tpd** (early-2020s WB framing), projected toward **~10,400 tpd by 2025**. Even if every barangay MRF ran at a generous 3 tpd, 335 facilities would process only ~1,000 tpd—far below city waste volume. Centralized units (dozen-scale) cannot close that gap alone.

**MMDA-reported diversion rates (Oct 2020)** averaged **~48%** across LGUs (range ~10% Taguig to ~78–79% Malabon/Valenzuela). Formula used was essentially *volume collected / estimated generation*, which **overestimates true diversion** (does not properly isolate processed recyclables + biodegradables from residual haul). Correct framing: *processed waste / generated waste*.

### 2. Dual architecture: MRF vs MRS (junkshop MOUs)

Where land is scarce, barangays substitute **MRS** (memorandum of understanding with a junkshop; eco-aides sort on pushcarts / at collection points) for fixed MRFs.

| LGU (focus set) | Barangays | Central MRF | Barangay MRFs | MRS | Junkshops (approx.) | Notes |
|-----------------|-----------|-------------|---------------|-----|---------------------|--------|
| **Quezon City** | 142 | 0 | 61 (WB) / 57 (QC Eco Profile 2018) | 84 | 273 | Large land area but residentialized; MRS complements sparse lots |
| **Pasig** | 30 | **5–6** | 17 | 1 | 69 | Strongest centralized MRF presence in NCR table |
| **Marikina** | 16 | **1** (Sto. Niño) | 7 | 0 preferred | 34 | City prefers own MRF stack over MRS |
| **Manila** | 897 | 0 | **3** (WB) / ~11 “operational” (PSC 2023) | **642** | 62 | Extreme density → MRS-dominant model |

**Takeaway:** Counting “MRFs” without distinguishing storage sheds, full process MRFs, and MRS **inflates compliance** under RA 9003. Pasay’s “156 MRFs” are often **5–10 m² storage boxes**, not plants—an important calibration for any capacity claim.

### 3. Common failures (2020–2026 evidence base)

1. **Land scarcity in HUCs** — Primary reason Metro Manila cannot meet “every barangay or cluster” MRF mandate. Small barangays (esp. Manila) cannot site 50–100 m² facilities.
2. **Paper compliance / phantom capacity** — Facilities exist as structures or budget line items; throughput, weighing, and diversion logs are weak or absent (World Bank data-gap finding).
3. **Post-build abandonment / maintenance failure** — Documented pattern outside and inside NCR (e.g., barangay facilities that lapse after capital construction when O&M budget and staff vanish). Dirty MRFs generate **leachate and odor** (ADB toolkit).
4. **Weak source segregation** — Contaminated inflows raise labor cost and lower resale grade; “recycling gap” material still appears on trucks and landfill floors.
5. **Contract / PPP design failure (mechanized)** — **Pasig** case: private mechanized MRF (IPM Environmental Services) reported at **~2,000 m³/day** mixed-waste processing; contract **ended Feb 2021**; successor Eco-Waste expected to rebuild. **Annual renewal** of collection/processing contracts **blocks recovery of capital** for mechanized plant O&M—LGU preference for zero-CapEx contractor build is structurally anti-investment.
6. **Understaffing** — City-scale MRFs (Marikina, Mandaluyong, Parañaque) run with **30–50** sorter/sweeper staff; barangay sheds often **3–10**. Thin staffing = low recovery hours.
7. **Enforcement gap on RA 9003** — National reviews (NEDA, COA, World Bank, Andaya et al. 2026): decades after the law, barangay MRF coverage remains **~30–39% nationally** and **~20% in Metro Manila**. Only ~37% of LGUs were fully compliant as of late-2010s snapshots still cited in 2025–26 literature.
8. **Informal sector sidelined or informalized only** — High-value recyclables are captured by waste pickers and junkshops *before* formal MRFs; low-value SUPs (sachets, straws, styrofoam) are **rejected by many QC junkshops**, so MRFs that only “buy what junkshops buy” do not close plastic leakage.
9. **No design standards / inconsistent capacity definitions** — World Bank notes **no PH design standard** for waste recovery facilities; “200 tons” claims (see Marikina) are often **ambiguous** (daily capacity vs batch/storage).
10. **Diversion metric theater** — High reported % diversion can coexist with landfill-bound residual growth if the MMDA formula is used uncritically.

### 4. City cases (2020–2026)

#### Quezon City

- **Stock:** Eco Profile (2018 data tables still baseline for many citations): **57 barangays with MRF**, **85 with MRS**; World Bank later table: **61 MRFs + 84 MRS**, **0 city central MRF** in the centralized list. Special-size barangay facility: **Brgy. Immaculate Concepcion ~2,800 m²** (MMDA Master Plan site visits).
- **Generation:** Highest volume in NCR; Eco Profile projected **~3,169 tpd** solid waste (2018, from WACS + population); per-capita rising (~0.66 kg → 0.88 kg in successive WACS; further growth assumed).
- **Model:** Package clean-up contractors + barangay committees; **organized waste pickers / junk traders** manned some city MRF stations (Environmental Management Program 2018)—a formalization path. City-hall segregation project supported by an on-site MRF. Hazardous/e-waste (e.g. fluorescent bulbs) directed to special recovery points by ordinance (SP-1483 lineage).
- **Diversion (reported):** **56%** (MMDA, Oct 2020)—among mid-high NCR rates, but subject to overestimation caveat.
- **Gaps / failures:** Still **far below** one-MRF-per-barangay; World Bank flags **Quezon City as primary Tier-2 target for MRF enhancement** (and segregation bins for CBD plastics). Space is constrained by residential land use despite large city area. Plastic chair/brick and brand take-back programs exist but do not substitute for throughput infrastructure. Interest in **WTE** coexists with incomplete RA 9003 front-end recovery.
- **2023–26 note:** Project COPE / partner programs report **1 improved MRF** among multi-city MRF builds—incremental, not systemic catch-up.

#### Pasig

- **Stock:** **Strongest central MRF cluster** in NCR tables: **5 central + 17 barangay MRFs + 1 MRS** (WB Table 7; narrative also cites six including mechanized). Ordinance **No. 1, s. 2018** (Ecological SWM Code): every barrio/cluster must have MRF on city/barangay land; malls/condos must have own MRFs with SWMO seal.
- **Mechanized capacity:** IPM-operated mixed-waste plant **~2,000 m³/day** (density-dependent tonnage; illustrative order-of-magnitude hundreds of tpd if dense). Contract end **Feb 2021**; Eco-Waste to build successor. **Annual contract renewal = structural failure mode** for private CapEx recovery.
- **Community EcoHub (Nov 2023):** USAID + Green Antz + city in **Brgy. Manggahan**—~₱14M facility: **up to 1 tpd organic composting** + low-value plastic (sachets/bags) into **eco-bricks/pavers**. Functions as modern MRF-adjacent node, not city-scale residual plant.
- **Diversion (reported):** **60%** (MMDA Oct 2020). World Bank classifies Pasig among **Tier-1** LGUs suited for **centralized dry-recyclable recovery facility** investment (with Parañaque, Muntinlupa).
- **Gaps / failures:** High ordinance ambition vs **investment-hostile contracting**; reliance on private hauler plant without multi-year bankable term; barangay coverage still incomplete relative to generation; residual collection leaks still feed Pasig–Marikina river plastic load (shared basin).

#### Marikina

- **Stock:** **City centralized MRF, Brgy. Sto. Niño** — lot **~922 m²**, publicly cited capacity **“up to 200 tons”** (city/EMB-NCR communications; treat as **design/handling capacity**, not verified sustained tpd throughput). Plus **7 barangay MRFs**. **No MRS preference**—city wants recovery through its own facilities.
- **Operations:** City MRF among those with **30–50** personnel (sorters + street sweepers). Ordinance **No. 74, s. 2005** established the city MRF under the waste management office. MMDA Master Plan cites Marikina as a **city-managed** (not only barangay) model, operated with private hauler (ISWMS) interface.
- **Reputation:** Frequently showcased for **segregated collection, food-waste recovery, and community discipline** (LGU study tours into 2025–26). Diversion reported **50%** (MMDA Oct 2020).
- **Gaps / failures:** Small city (16 barangays) makes centralization easier than Manila/QC—**success is not automatically scalable**. Capacity figure ambiguity; limited independent tonnage audits in open literature 2020–26. Still dependent on downstream landfill system shared with metro.

#### City of Manila

- **Stock:** **897 barangays** but only **3 MRFs** in World Bank table, offset by **642 MRS** arrangements—the purest **MRS-for-MRF substitution** case in NCR. Dense, tiny barangay lots (**40–60 m²** typical where any facility exists—MMDA site visits). Plastics Smart Cities (2023) cites **~11 operational MRFs** of 896 barangays—still **&lt;2%** fixed-facility coverage if true, and still MRS-dominant.
- **Diversion (reported):** **33%** (MMDA Oct 2020)—below metro average.
- **Programs:** Brand/NGO cash-for-waste and Aling Tindera-type SUP recovery exist; do not replace barangay processing capacity.
- **Gaps / failures:** **Physical impossibility of universal MRFs** without cluster facilities or vertical/modular designs; residual and plastic leakage risk high where MRS residuals are poorly monitored (World Bank: residuals left along routes can enter waterways). World Bank recommends **MRS enhancement** (pushcarts, weighing, residual pickup discipline) **and** segregation bins in CBD—not pretending every barangay can build a 50 m² plant.

### 5. What “capacity” and “utilization” mean for planning (2020–2026)

| Layer | Typical reality | Utilization signal |
|-------|-----------------|--------------------|
| Household / source | Partial segregation | Contaminated streams |
| Barangay MRF shed | Storage + light sort; 1–5 tpd theoretical | Often unknown kg sold |
| MRS (junkshop MOU) | Mobile sort, no fixed plant | No residual tracking |
| City central / mechanized | Compost + dry sort; rare full mixed-waste automation | Contractor contract risk |
| Truck / landfill floor | Illegal/informal sort | Proof of upstream under-recovery |

**Rule of thumb for YCLAP:** If an LGU cannot produce **weighed monthly kg** of recyclables and organics leaving each MRF/MRS node, treat “diversion %” as **political**, not operational.

## YCLAP-Gargar implication

1. **Do not build the RA 9003 MRF that LGUs failed to build.** Gargar’s edge is **last-mile scrap-to-value + price transparency + informal collector path**, not another 50 m² shed with no O&M. Partner **with** existing barangay MRFs, EcoHubs, and junkshops; measure **kg diverted** and **collector income**, not facility ribbon-cuttings.

2. **Target the recycling gap the World Bank named.** Material still sorted on trucks and landfills is the market failure. Gargar should pull **clean dry streams and organics** *before* residual haul—especially low-value plastics junkshops reject (sachets, films) where EcoHub-style upcycling or offtake exists (Pasig Manggahan model).

3. **Pilot geography:** **Pasig** (dense EcoHub + ordinance muscle + Tier-1 investment logic; Ateneo-adjacent) and **Quezon City** (volume + waste-picker formalization history + World Bank “enhance existing MRFs” target). Use **Marikina** as **operational benchmark** (discipline, central MRF staffing), not as copy-paste. **Manila** is MRS-world—design for **mobile / app-mediated recovery**, not fixed plant siting.

4. **Organic waste is under-served by dry-centric MRFs.** Many facilities process recyclables only; biodegradables (~50%+ of MSW composition in QC WACS) drive **landfill methane**. Gargar + compost/biodigester partnerships address both **climate (SDG 13)** and **flood co-benefit (basura → baha)** better than plastic-credit cosplay.

5. **Avoid annual-contract traps.** Any B2G pilot needs multi-year offtake or barangay-level cash flows that do not depend on city PPP renewal. Prefer **barangay + junkshop + school/market nodes**.

6. **Metrics that match the evidence gap:**  
   - kg recyclables / kg organics diverted per week  
   - % contamination  
   - ₱/kg paid to collectors (justice metric)  
   - residual kg **not** going to truck floor  
   Explicitly **do not** depend on carbon-credit revenue as product thesis (markets immature; integrity risk).

7. **Narrative for LEARN / pitch:** “Metro Manila has MRF *structures* covering ~20% of barangays and almost no *audited throughput*. Gargar closes the utilization and justice gap—not the building permit gap.”
