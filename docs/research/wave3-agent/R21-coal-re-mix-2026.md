# R21 — Philippines electricity generation mix mid-2025/2026: coal vs RE, capacity vs generation, why student RE-developer cosplay fails

**Agent:** YCLAP research (wave3)  
**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Scope:** DOE-reported and secondary 2025 full-year + early-2026 rolling mix; installed capacity vs actual generation; GEAP/pipeline reality; implications for 5-week student climate projects  

---

## Key findings

### 1. Generation mix (what actually ran) — 2025 calendar year (DOE)

| Source | Share of **power generation** (GWh) | Notes |
|--------|-------------------------------------|--------|
| **Coal** | **~57%** | Still dominant; down from **62.5% in 2023** and ~**63% in 2024** (IMF/secondary) |
| **Natural gas** | **~17%** | Transition fuel narrative; LNG import exposure |
| **Oil-based** | **~2%** | Peaking / alert hours |
| **Fossil total** | **~76%** | ~3 of every 4 kWh |
| **Renewables (all)** | **~25%** | **DOE headline end-2025**; up from a long plateau at **~22%** |

DOE messaging (May–June 2026): RE generation share hit **25% by end-December 2025** — framed as a step toward PEP targets of **35% by 2030** and **50% by 2040**. Coal remains the single largest source at **57%**.

### 2. Rolling 12-month check (Apr 2025 – Mar 2026)

Independent compilation (Low-Carbon Power / Ember-style stack) for the last 12 months through early 2026 is consistent with DOE’s year-end story:

| Source | Share |
|--------|--------|
| Coal | **56.4%** |
| Gas | **17.9%** |
| Hydropower | **11.1%** |
| Geothermal | **7.9%** |
| Solar | **4.0%** |
| Wind | **1.0%** |
| Biofuels | **1.0%** |
| Other fossil | **~0.6%** |
| **Low-carbon total** | **~25%** |

Carbon intensity remains high (~**564 gCO₂eq/kWh** in that window). Per-person low-carbon electricity is essentially flat (~256 kWh/person).

**Takeaway:** Mid-2025 → early-2026 is not a coal collapse. It is a **modest RE climb from the low-20s to ~25% generation**, with coal still majority baseload.

### 3. Capacity vs generation — the structural trap students miss

| Metric | RE | Coal / thermal |
|--------|----|----------------|
| **Installed capacity share** | **~31.2%** of total capacity end-2025 (IRENA: **10,436 MW** RE; share up from 30.1% in 2024) | Coal still large in absolute MW; Luzon coal fleet cited flat at **8,637 MW** through 2023–2025 (DOE USec Guevara / CASE) |
| **Generation share** | **~25%** (DOE 2025) | **~57%** coal alone |
| **Why the gap** | Solar/wind have low capacity factors; hydro is seasonal; new MW are often **midday solar** | Coal (and geothermal) run as **baseload** with high capacity factors (~60%+ historically for PH coal fleets) |

Rough system scale (secondary, 2024–2025): total installed capacity on the order of **~29–33 GW** (Enerdata / BNEF-class figures; exact DOE 2025 Power Statistics tables published mid-2026 should be treated as the official update when downloaded). GlobalData-class outlooks still show **coal capacity rising** (e.g. ~13 GW → ~15 GW class by 2035) even under RE build-out — **moratorium ≠ fleet retirement**.

**Critical system fact (2023–2025 stress data, DOE USec Guevara):**  
- Luzon peak demand broke **14,000+ MW**.  
- Alerts cluster **1600H–2000H**; solar peaks **1000H–1300H** — windows **do not overlap**.  
- RE share of Luzon **peak-hour** dispatch **fell** from ~12.8% (2023) to ~10.7% (2025) even as RE **capacity** grew — demand grew into the evening, new RE was mostly solar.  
- Evening peaks still need **dispatchable thermal** (including oil) and firm RE (geothermal, hydro when available) or **storage**.

Capacity growth ≠ generation share ≠ peak-hour reliability. Pipeline slides that only show MW awarded systematically overstate “transition done.”

### 4. Pipeline vs online — awards ≠ electrons

| Layer | Order of magnitude | Reality check |
|-------|--------------------|---------------|
| **Operational RE capacity** | **~10–10.5 GW** end-2025 (IRENA ~10.4 GW; IMF ~9.5 GW for 2024) | What is actually on the grid |
| **GEAP auction awards** | GEA-4 alone **~10.2 GW** (Nov 2025); cumulative GEAP target **≥25 GW through 2035** | Contracts for future delivery, not today’s mix |
| **Service contracts / “potential”** | BOI/DOE tables show **tens to 100+ GW** of awarded/potential RE paper | Much of this is pre-construction; DOE has been **culling non-performers** |
| **Terminations** | **84 RE service contracts** terminated in 2025 alone (~**5.4 GW** potential); **163** terminated/relinquished across **2024–2025** | Non-performance is systemic, not rare |

Flagship scale (for cosplay contrast): **MTerra Solar** (Meralco) first phase synchronized **Feb 2026**, ~**250 MW** solar by March with **~450 MWh** storage into evening hours; full plan **3,500 MWp + 4,500 MWh** BESS — utility/IPP capital, not campus.

### 5. DOE / policy snapshot (what officialdom is saying in 2026)

- **PEP targets:** RE **35% of generation by 2030**, **50% by 2040** (and higher under clean-energy scenarios).  
- **2025 accomplishment claim:** generation RE share **25%** (from ~22%).  
- **Installed RE targets (Clean Energy Scenario class):** on the order of **~30.5 GW RE by 2030** and **100+ GW by 2050** in optimistic scenarios — requires multi-GW annual build, transmission, storage, and firm capacity.  
- **Price narrative:** Luzon wholesale LWAP fell roughly **USD 105 → 65 / MWh (2023→2025)** despite higher peaks — DOE analysis attributes much of this to zero-bid variable RE in WESM + GEAP fixed-price contracting + less fuel-index exposure. That is **system-operator / auction architecture**, not a student product.  
- **Coal policy gap:** no full national **coal phase-out date** in planning; previously permitted coal projects can still complete; remaining coal capacity mid-century still multi-GW under REF-type scenarios.

### 6. Why student “RE developer” cosplay fails

1. **Wrong unit of agency.** Utility-scale RE is financed and built by IPPs, conglomerates (Meralco Terra, ACEN, Aboitiz, SMC energy arms, etc.), GEAP winners, and offtakers under ERC/CSP rules. A 5-week YCLAP team cannot hold a DOE service contract to COD.  
2. **Capital and time.** Hundreds of MW to multi-GW projects need **billions of pesos**, multi-year permitting, land, interconnection queues, and construction. Showcase demos that “plan a 50 MW solar farm” are fiction theater.  
3. **Regulatory stack.** DOE contracts → performance milestones → ERC → NGCP grid impact → DU PSA/CSP or GEAP → environmental and LGU clearances. Non-performance is actively **terminated** (84 contracts in 2025). Students have zero standing in that pipeline.  
4. **Pipeline fantasy.** Pitch decks quote **100+ GW “awarded”** potential while only **~10 GW RE** is online and coal still supplies **majority of kWh**. Confusing awards with generation is the core cosplay error.  
5. **Physics of the Philippine peak.** Evening heat-driven peaks need **storage, firm RE, flexible thermal, or demand response** — not another midday-only solar slide. Solving coal’s **generation** share requires **capacity factor and time-of-day**, not nameplate alone.  
6. **Wrong climate problem for student leverage.** National coal lock-in is real (mitigation rank ~5 in YCLAP brief), but student-solvable slices are **demand-side / campus / LGU / community** (efficiency, rooftop awareness, heat, waste-methane), not IPP development. Mentors (Porio / Lasco / Tan) reward **local vulnerability, feasibility, partners** — not mega-infrastructure LARPing.  
7. **Reputation risk.** Claiming “we will decarbonize the grid” without offtake, capital, or grid studies teaches bad climate entrepreneurship and fails Campaign Canvas honesty.

**What *is* honest student-scale energy work:** campus load audit, behavioral demand reduction, small rooftop/solar learning kits *with real kWh metering*, heat/cool equity, SME efficiency, or advocacy that **explains** capacity vs generation (this note) without pretending to be an IPP.

---

## YCLAP implication

- Treat **“PH is still ~57% coal generation / ~25% RE generation as of 2025”** as the accurate 2026 classroom baseline — not “RE boom means coal is over.”  
- **Capacity ≠ generation:** RE can be ~**31% of capacity** and only ~**25% of energy**; coal can be a smaller capacity share and still dominate GWh. Force this distinction in every RE pitch.  
- **Flag utility-scale RE developer cosplay as overhyped** (aligns with deep-research brief §1 / §4). Do not build the Aug–Sep 2026 showcase around fake MW pipelines.  
- If energy appears in a project at all, prefer **demand-side, campus, heat, or literacy** with measurable local units (kWh avoided, hours cooled, users trained) — not “MW by 2030” claims.  
- Flagship remains **waste → methane + flood co-benefit (Gargar lane)**; energy context is **narrative evidence** for why national mitigation is slow, not the product.  
- Optional mentor-ready one-liner: *“DOE got RE to 25% of generation; coal still 57%. Students don’t auction gigawatts — we own the last mile where agency is real.”*
