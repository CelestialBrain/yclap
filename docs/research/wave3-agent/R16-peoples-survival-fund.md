# R16 — People's Survival Fund (RA 10174): Funding, Access, Disbursement, Critiques (2015–2026)

**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Scope:** Republic Act 10174 (People's Survival Fund Act); funding levels; approved projects; LGU access barriers; disbursement/utilization; critiques 2015–2026  
**Method:** Official DOF/CCC/NICCDIES pages, GAA framing, civil-society case study (Klima/Manila Observatory 2025), academic analyses (Yanquiling/TDRI), press (Rappler, PNA), DOF board releases through Sep 2025–Apr 2026

## Key findings

### 1. Legal design and funding levels

- **RA 10174 (2012)** amends the Climate Change Act of 2009 (RA 9729) and creates the **People's Survival Fund (PSF)** as a dedicated, long-term finance stream for **climate change adaptation** projects of **local government units (LGUs)** and **CCC-accredited local/community organizations**.
- **Floor capitalization:** opening balance of **PHP 1 billion**, with a **running balance that must not fall below PHP 1 billion**. Annual replenishment is programmed from the **General Appropriations Act (GAA)**; the fund may be augmented by donations, endowments, grants, counterpart LGU funds, and private contributions.
- The PSF is **suppletory** — it fills gaps beyond regular agency and LGU climate/DRR appropriations, not a substitute for them.
- **Eligible uses (statutory):** water and land management; agriculture and fisheries; health; infrastructure; ecosystem protection; early warning systems; regional centers/information networks; institutional capacity (planning, preparedness, contingency for drought/flood); and **guarantee for risk-transfer / insurance** needs of farmers and other stakeholders.
- **Governance:** multi-sectoral **PSF Board** chaired by the **Secretary of Finance**; members include CCC vice chair, DBM, NEDA/DEPDev, DILG, Philippine Commission on Women, plus sectoral seats (academe/science, business, NGO). **Bureau of the Treasury (BTr)** has served as Board Secretariat; funds historically channeled via **Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP)** under government audit (COA) rules.
- **Scale relative to need:** ~PHP 1B/year (~USD 18–23M depending on FX) is small against national climate adaptation gaps and against LGU demand (proposals have run into **tens of billions of pesos** in aggregate asks). Fixed annual floor creates a structural **financing gap** if proposal quality and approval velocity improve without parallel budget growth.

### 2. Timeline of operationalization and utilization

| Period | Status |
|--------|--------|
| **2012** | RA 10174 enacted |
| **2015** | First GAA capitalization (~PHP 1B); fund “opened”; first call for proposals |
| **2016–2018** | First wave of full project approvals (6 LGU projects: Del Carmen, Lanuza, San Francisco, Gerona, Kitcharao, Sarangani) |
| **2016** | **Project Development Grant (PDG)** window created to fund proposal preparation (cap **PHP 2M** per proponent; total PDG envelope ~**PHP 60M**; municipalities 2nd class and below) |
| **2019–2022** | **Approval drought** — essentially **no new full projects** approved while proposals accumulated; COVID-19, disasters, elections cited for implementation/disbursement delays |
| **2020 (DOF/BTr)** | ~**172 proposals** from **129 proponents**, total ask ~**PHP 14.57B**; only **PHP 310.34M** approved across 6 projects; **~PHP 147.46M disbursed** (~47% of approved tranche then) |
| **Mar 2023** | Process streamlining; record **~180 proposals** received in a call window |
| **Oct 2023** | Board approves **~PHP 539–540M** batch (Mountain Province, Maramag, Borongan, Cabagan, Catanauan); cumulative allocation then **~PHP 887.63M** of the PHP 1B to **11 projects + 6 PDGs** (~**89% utilization** of the initial billion *as committed*) |
| **Mid-2024** | Klima/MO case study: **13 full projects + 6 PDGs = 19 awards** |
| **Aug 2024 (Rappler)** | **Only 6 of 176** LGU proposals (2015–2022 window framing) had been approved in early reporting; spotlighted chronic under-approval |
| **Sep 2025 (DOF caravan)** | **24** adaptation projects + PDGs totaling **~PHP 1.421B** approved nationwide |
| **~Jul 2025 (DOF social)** | **21** awards cited as reaching only **~1.2% of all LGUs** |
| **Apr 2026 (secondary)** | **~28 projects** supported; **~PHP 1.5B** financing commitments |
| **2025–2026** | Nationwide **PSF Caravan** (DOF + UNDP SHIELD + Australia); 2026 concept-note call (deadline cited **29 May 2026**) |

**Disbursement vs commitment:** Official communications often report **board approval / allocation** (commitment rate). **Actual cash disbursement** has lagged approvals because of financing-agreement templates, ECC/CNC prerequisites, revised work-and-financial-plan approvals, LGU document readiness, disasters, COVID-19, and elections. In Nov 2020, only ~half of the then-approved PHP 310M had been released; some projects (e.g., Kitcharao at that time) had **zero** disbursement pending documents. By late 2023, “utilization” rhetoric (~89%) referred largely to **committing the PHP 1B envelope**, not full project completion.

**Pattern:** Projects cluster in highly climate-exposed rural/provincial LGUs (Mindanao, Eastern Visayas, Cordillera, Northern Luzon). **No accredited community organization has received direct PSF project funding** as of mid-2024 (Klima/MO) — the community-org modality remains largely non-operationalized despite statutory eligibility.

### 4. Access process and prioritization criteria

**Documentary requirements (post-2016 streamlining; down from ~14–15 documents):**

*For LGUs:* (1) Letter of Intent; (2) Project proposal + Work and Financial Plan; (3) Adaptation reference (any of CRVA, CCA-DRR-enhanced CLUP/CDP, LCCAP); (4) Annual Investment Plan (AIP).

*For accredited community orgs:* accreditation certificate; LOI; proposal; board resolution; proof of communication with beneficiary LGU.

**Flow:** Call for proposals (not continuous rolling window historically) → Secretariat screening → technical review / field appraisal (Secretariat, DBP, CCC, interagency) → CCC concurrence/endorsement → **PSF Board final approval** → financing agreement → disbursement conduit (DBP/BTr) under COA rules.

**Selection priorities:** multi-hazard climate risk/vulnerability; poverty incidence and poverty-reduction potential; key biodiversity areas; community participation in design; cost-effectiveness; co-benefits / multi-sectoral spillover; gender-responsive design; existence of local climate plans; (recent calls) scalability, transformational adaptation (traditional knowledge + science, NbS, new tech).

**Support instruments:** Proponent’s Handbook; brochures (multiple languages); PSF eLearning (~12-week course); clinics; mentoring/scoping missions; enhancement writeshops; PDG; 2025–2026 Caravan.

### 5. Access barriers for LGUs (and communities)

Evidence from Yanquiling (TDRI ~2019–2020), CCC case synthesis, DOF reports, Klima/Manila Observatory (Apr 2025), and press:

1. **Technical capacity gap.** Many LGUs lack staff skilled in climate rationale, CRVA, proposal writing, cost-benefit analysis, and MRV. Baseline data incomplete, outdated, or lost. Quality proposals often depend on CSO/consultant “heavy lifting.”
2. **Planning document debt.** PSF assumes ready LCCAP / enhanced CLUP-CDP / risk assessments. Historically weak CLUP/CDP currency (one study: only ~5% of sample LGUs had correct-year CLUP coverage). LCCAP compliance improved to **~89% by Aug 2024** (CCC) — necessary but not sufficient for bankable proposals.
3. **Climate-rationale filter.** Proposals rejected when activities lack clear adaptation linkage (same problem as GCF-style “climate rationale”). Incomplete documents are a top rejection cause.
4. **Batch/call architecture.** Proposals accepted mainly during opened calls (e.g., 2023; 2026 concept notes). Long gaps between approvals (notably **2019–2022**) freeze the pipeline.
5. **Post-approval friction.** ECC/CNC fees and timing; financing agreement delays; WFP revisions for delayed projects; disasters/elections/COVID interrupting sites and staff continuity.
6. **Political/administrative discontinuity.** LGU administration turnover reshuffles personnel and priorities; climate programs not mainstreamed; insecure tenure for technical staff.
7. **Information inequity.** Materials exist online, but remote/low-connectivity communities and under-resourced LGUs still struggle to know the fund exists or navigate process (PSF long described as “largely unknown”).
8. **Community-org modality dormant.** Accreditation path exists on paper; **zero direct awards** to community orgs through mid-2024 — so “People’s” access often means **through LGUs**, not direct grassroots finance.
9. **PDG limits.** Only lower-income **municipalities** eligible for PDG; provinces/cities/barangays excluded from that window; inadequate evaluation of whether PDG converts to successful full awards.
10. **Scale mismatch.** PHP 1B floor vs national vulnerability and vs proposal demand (PHP 14B+ historical asks) means even perfect process leaves most LGUs unfunded.

### 6. Critiques, 2015–2026 (synthesized)

| Theme | Critique | Who / when |
|-------|----------|------------|
| **Chronic underutilization** | Decade+ after enactment, few dozen awards; ~1 project/year average for long stretches; hundreds of proposals rejected or stuck | Rappler 2024; Klima/MO 2025; Yanquiling; DOF self-reports |
| **Approval ≠ cash** | Commitment rates overstated relative to disbursement and completion | BTr/DOF 2020; implementation delay narratives |
| **Access for the vulnerable is inverted** | LGUs most in need often least able to write “winning” proposals; consultants and connected LGUs advantaged | CSO interviews (Klima 2025); public call comments 2026 |
| **Law lacks accessibility mandate** | RA 10174 creates fund, board, uses, priorities — but **no explicit duty to ensure ease of access** for LGUs/CSOs/communities; broad discretion | Klima/MO legal reading 2025 |
| **Community modality empty** | Statutory eligibility of accredited local orgs never translated into awards | Klima/MO 2025 |
| **Bureaucratic needle’s eye** | “Perang bayan” hard to use; reactive vs proactive; process too heavy for climate emergency | Civil society / public discourse |
| **Fixed PHP 1B inadequate** | Flagship fund too small for WorldRisk #1 country; future financing gap if absorption improves | Yanquiling; climate-finance commentary |
| **Coordination bottlenecks** | Multi-agency board + secretariat + CCC review + DBP conduit creates handoff delay | DOF reform messaging 2023–2025 |
| **Reform partial success** | Requirement cut (14→4), PDG, eLearning, financing-agreement template, caravan, 2023–26 approval rebound — but structural capacity and community access remain | DOF/CCC + civil society |

**Counter-narrative (official):** Streamlining since DOF Secretariat role (~2017), 2023 process reform, ~89% commitment of initial billion by late 2023, multi-hundred-million approvals 2023–24, caravan capacity-building 2025–26, and rising commitment stock (~PHP 1.4–1.5B by 2025–26) show intentional acceleration under recent administrations.

### 7. What “good” looks like (reform direction already in literature)

- Institutionalized **capacity-building and technical assistance** (not ad-hoc caravans only).
- Further **simplification / flexible evidence** (accept alternative baseline sources when formal plans lag).
- **Operationalize community-org access** (accreditation + dedicated windows + small grants).
- **Faster, predictable disbursement** after approval (template FA + ECC/CNC path clarity).
- **Rolling or more frequent calls** vs multi-year droughts.
- **Larger envelope or multi-year commitment** beyond the PHP 1B floor if absorption holds.
- Stronger **LGU–CSO co-design** incentives without making LGUs permanently consultant-dependent.
- Transparency dashboards: proposals received / approved / disbursed / completed by LGU class and region.

## YCLAP implication

1. **PSF is not a student-scale product** — it is a national special fund for **LGUs and accredited orgs**. Youth CLAP teams will not “apply as YCLAP” for PHP tens-of-millions packages. Treat PSF as **policy infrastructure**, not a grant product to ship in 5 weeks.

2. **High-leverage YCLAP wedge = last-mile access tooling for LGUs**, not reinventing the fund:
   - **LCCAP / CRVA / AIP packaging helpers** (checklists, data templates, climate-rationale scaffolds) that reduce the #1 rejection drivers.
   - **Concept-note / proposal drafting aids** aligned to official templates and 2026 prioritization criteria (risk relevance, impact, scalability, inclusion, transformational adaptation).
   - **Barangay-to-municipal evidence packs** (hazard stories, beneficiary counts, simple baselines) that under-resourced planning offices can attach.
   - **Open explainers** in Filipino for SK / youth / CSOs on *what* PDG vs full PSF is, and *why* community-org direct access is still broken.

3. **Metro Manila / Ateneo angle:** PSF awards historically favor **highly exposed provincial LGUs** (field schools, watersheds, coastal rehab). NCR cities are not the core historic beneficiary set. For Metro Manila pilots (waste, flood, heat), PSF is a **secondary** finance path; LGU **local funds**, CCC programs, and NGO partners are more realistic near-term. Partner with a **climate-vulnerable municipality** outside NCR only if a real LGU champion exists.

4. **Narrative for YCLAP storytelling:** PSF is the country’s **flagship local adaptation fund** — and a cautionary case that **law + PHP 1B ≠ resilience**. WorldRisk #1 status coexists with ~**1% of LGUs** reached and **zero** direct community-org awards for years. That frames student work on **delivery systems, transparency, and community-ready evidence** as climate justice, not just “apps.”

5. **Do not overclaim:** Avoid pitching “we will unlock the PSF” as a deliverable. Prefer: *“We reduce the documentation/capacity friction that keeps vulnerable LGUs out of the PSF pipeline,”* with a concrete pilot LGU or CSO partnership.

6. **Policy literacy win:** Fellows should know RA 10174, the PHP 1B floor, PDG (PHP 2M prep grants), Board composition, and the approval–disbursement gap — useful for advocacy, NAP/LCCAP conversations, and critiques of adaptation finance theater.
