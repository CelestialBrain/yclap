# R31 — CLIMA Act Philippines (Climate Accountability): Bill Status 2025–2026

**Research date:** 2026-08-07  
**Scope:** Legislative status (House/Senate), sponsors, operative design (what it would change), and why YCLAP student products must not depend on passage  
**Geography:** Philippines (national); relevance to youth climate work, loss-and-damage narrative, corporate accountability education  
**Method:** Official bill texts (HB 9609 19th Congress; HB 4420 / related 20th Congress filings), Greenpeace/LRC/PIA/Inquirer coverage, LSE Grantham analysis, Climate Action Tracker (Dec 2025 update), ESG practitioner reviews (SyCip via Slaughter and May), civil-society campaign materials through mid-2026  

---

## Key findings

### 1. What the CLIMA Act is (and is not)

- The **Climate Accountability (CLIMA) Act** is **proposed** Philippine legislation—not enacted law. It seeks a domestic **polluter-pays** framework that holds large businesses and especially **carbon majors** (oil, gas, coal, cement producers/traders/refineries with significant global GHG contribution) accountable for climate-related loss and damage suffered in the Philippines.
- It is widely described as among the **first Global South / Southeast Asian** attempts to put **corporate climate due diligence + reparations** into primary statute, distinct from the Climate Change Act (RA 9729), People’s Survival Fund (RA 10174), Clean Air Act, and EPR (RA 11898)—which focus on adaptation finance, pollution control, or plastic packaging recovery, **not** carbon-major liability for climate harm.
- **Status as of research date (early August 2026):** **Pending.** Not law. Not a Republic Act. No implementing rules. No operating reparations board or fund under this statute.

**Bottom line:** House is the active chamber of record for CLIMA in 2025–2026 (**refiled, committee/TWG work**). **Senate has not delivered a finished, signed law.** Full Philippine bicameral path (committee → plenary → counterpart chamber → bicam → President) is **incomplete**.

### 4. What the bill would change if enacted (operative design)

Drawn primarily from **HB 9609** text (public PDF) and consistent 2024–2026 summaries (LSE Grantham, LRC, Slaughter and May/SyCip, Greenpeace, PIA). 20th Congress text (HB 4420) is a refiled/evolved version of the same architecture; TWG may still amend numbers and institutional names.

#### A. Who is covered (“Business”)

- Entities with **gross income > ₱100 million** in the previous tax year, **and**
- **Carbon majors** (multinational or state-owned producers/traders/refineries of **crude oil, natural gas, coal, and cement** that significantly contribute to global GHG emissions) **regardless of gross income**.

#### B. Business accountability framework (new duties)

- **Due diligence standard of care / “do no harm”** across the **value chain**, explicitly referencing **UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights**.
- Required measures include: (i) proactive fact-finding on climate harms; (ii) shift away from business-as-usual that exacerbates climate impacts and **strategically reduce GHG emissions**; (iii) risk management given existing/prospective vulnerability.
- Concrete responsibilities typically cited: **climate-related financial disclosure to the SEC**; **measurement of GHG emissions** (value-chain, IPCC-aligned science); **prevention of climate-related human rights abuses** (life, property, participation, ICC/IP rights, etc.).
- **Doctrine of Last Clear Chance:** carbon majors’ knowledge of their contribution creates a duty to **substantially reduce emissions**; failure deepens liability.
- **Attribution science** recognized as a framework for linking extreme/slow-onset events to climate change and for claims (complements traditional proof of damage).
- **Tax-credit** recognition for businesses that meet due-diligence standards and contribute to clean-energy transition consistent with the NDC (details left to IRR).
- **Prohibited / sanctionable conduct** includes **greenwashing** (false or misleading environmental claims) and **climate denialism**, with fines framed in HB 9609 as on the order of **15% of gross income** (penalties accruing to the reparations fund). Non-compliance with due-diligence and related duties can trigger severe monetary penalties in the same ballpark in expert summaries.

#### C. Loss-and-damage / reparations machinery

- Creates a **Climate Change Reparations Fund (CCRF)** as a **special fund in the Bureau of the Treasury**, initially seeded (HB 9609) with **₱50 million** opening balance from the General Appropriations Act, with ongoing financing expected from **penalties** on covered polluters (sponsors emphasize oil/gas entities exceeding emissions thresholds; messaging stresses costs should not fall on Filipino consumers).
- LRC campaign materials also brand a survivor-facing fund concept as **CLAWS** (Climate Change Actions Welfare and Support)—naming may vary across bill drafts/advocacy briefs; the **function** is direct support for climate loss and damage.
- Eligible claim forms (illustrative from HB 9609): compensation for economic and non-economic harms; technology transfer; capacity building; financial support; **relocation**; recovery and rehabilitation.
- A **Climate Change Reparations Board** (quasi-judicial / administrative claim-approval body) would administer the fund, approve claims with finality, set rules of procedure, and can deputize agencies. (HB 4420 text reportedly also contemplates a **Climate Accountability Board / CLIMA Board** construct—confirm exact institutional name against the latest TWG draft before any product language.)

#### D. Access to justice

- Standing for **real parties in interest** and **climate change victims/survivors**; explicit openness to **minors, generations yet unborn, and biodiversity** as litigants via legal guardians under the Rules of Procedure for Environmental Cases.
- Complements (does not replace) ongoing **transnational climate litigation** strategies (e.g. 2025 announcements of Odette survivors exploring UK action against a major oil company)—domestic statute vs. foreign-court torts are different tracks.

#### E. What it does **not** automatically do

- Does **not** by itself phase out coal, rewrite the Philippine Energy Plan, or replace the NDC.
- Does **not** fund day-to-day LGU drainage or youth entrepreneurship.
- Does **not** create immediate cash for student projects, school programs, or barangay MRFs.
- Enforcement, IRR, board appointment, carbon-major jurisdiction (including foreign majors), and collection of penalties would all face **years of institutional and litigation risk** even after Presidential signature.

### 5. Political economy: why passage is uncertain

- **Lapsed once already** (19th Congress)—standard Philippine risk for ambitious private-member / committee bills without LEDAC/priority packaging.
- **Industry opposition risk** high: oil, gas, coal, cement, and large conglomerates face disclosure, emissions reduction, greenwashing liability, and fund contributions.
- **Legal complexity:** extraterritorial carbon majors, attribution science in local courts, 15%-of-income fines, and quasi-judicial boards invite constitutional and commercial challenges.
- **Crowded legislative agenda:** 2025–2026 dominated by flood-control scandal, budget, and LEDAC priorities; CLIMA is a **civil-society priority**, not clearly a **Palace must-pass** item in public SONA priority lists reviewed for this note.
- **International tailwinds** (ICJ AO 2025, ITLOS AO, loss-and-damage fund hosted narratives, COP framing) improve rhetorical odds but do **not** substitute for votes, bicameral concurrence, and signature.

### 6. Existing law vs. CLIMA (do not conflate)

| Instrument | Status | Role vs CLIMA |
|------------|--------|----------------|
| **RA 9729** Climate Change Act (+ amendments) | Law | Policy/CCC architecture; **not** carbon-major liability |
| **RA 10174** People’s Survival Fund | Law | LGU/community **adaptation** finance; chronically small vs need |
| **RA 9003 / RA 11898** waste + EPR | Law | Solid waste & plastic packaging EPR—not climate reparations |
| **CLIMA Act (HB 4420 et al.)** | **Bill only** | Corporate due diligence + reparations for climate loss/damage |

YCLAP education and product design can **teach** CLIMA as proposed policy; they must **ground operational claims** in enacted instruments (PSF access, RA 9003/EPR, LGU ordinances, existing ECC/EIS tools, etc.).

## YCLAP implication

**Do not make any student product, curriculum module, pitch, prototype, funding pathway, or impact metric depend on CLIMA passage.**

### Why (product-design rules)

1. **It is not law.** As of August 2026 the Act is a **pending House bill** (refiled after dying with the 19th Congress). Building a feature that “disburses CLAWS/CCRF claims,” “scores carbon-major compliance under CLIMA,” or “unlocks reparations for barangay X under the Act” would be **false functionality**.
2. **Even if passed, implementation lag is multi-year.** IRR, board constitution, fund capitalization beyond seed money, and first successful claims against well-lawyered carbon majors would trail enactment. Student timelines (semesters, hackathons, 3-minute pitches) cannot wait on that.
3. **Jurisdiction and collectability are hard.** Many carbon majors are foreign or complex multi-entity structures. A classroom app cannot assume penalties will flow to Filipino survivors on a schedule students can demo.
4. **Political risk is two-sided.** Passage could be diluted in TWG/bicam; non-passage leaves advocacy intact. Products that **bet the narrative** on “once CLIMA passes, our solution works” collapse under either outcome if the core value prop was the statute.
5. **YCLAP’s durable lanes already exist without CLIMA:** waste justice and RA 9003/EPR transparency; flood and heat risk communication; informal-worker dignity; local adaptation integrity (including flood-control scandal literacy); green jobs and RE literacy; youth civic action under **existing** environmental rules of procedure. CLIMA is a **policy literacy and advocacy case study**, not a product dependency.

### Safe uses of CLIMA in YCLAP products

- **Explain** polluter-pays, attribution science, and loss-and-damage in accessible Filipino/English.
- **Map** bill status honestly: “proposed / pending in House committee as of [date]—not yet law.”
- **Compare** proposed CCRF/CLAWS design with the **existing** People’s Survival Fund (what exists vs what is still only proposed).
- **Support** youth advocacy (petitions, committee watch, letter-writing) as **civic skill**, without promising reparations payouts.
- **Link** community stories (flood, heat, livelihood loss) to **science and rights**, not to an unpassed fund as a cash source.

### Unsafe / ban-list product claims

- “Apply for CLIMA reparations in-app.”
- “Automatic carbon-major fine calculator that disburses to users.”
- “Compliance certified under the Climate Accountability Act” (no Act in force).
- Any KPI that requires “CLIMA enacted by 2026/2027” for project success.

**Design principle:** Treat CLIMA as **context and campaign literacy**, never as **runtime infrastructure**.
