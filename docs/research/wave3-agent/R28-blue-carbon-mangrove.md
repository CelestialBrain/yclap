# R28 — Philippines blue carbon & mangrove restoration (science and policy, 2020–2026)

**Scope:** Success rates, failed mass planting, community tenure, youth-scale realistic interventions  
**Window:** Evidence base through mid-2026 (classic PH science + 2020–2026 policy/practice updates)  
**Audience:** YCLAP research / program design — not a planting manual  

---

## Key findings

### 1. Extent, loss drivers, and where restoration can still work

- **Historic loss is severe.** Roughly **~64% of Philippine mangroves have been lost since ~1918**, driven especially by conversion to brackishwater aquaculture and coastal development. Contemporary extent estimates cluster around **~285,000–311,000 ha** depending on dataset year and method (Global Mangrove Watch / national figures diverge; treat as order-of-magnitude, not a single official hectare).
- **Protection is incomplete.** About **one-third** of remaining mangroves sit in protected areas in recent NGO summaries; other sources put formal protection lower (~19% in some project briefs). Either way, **most mangroves are not under strong protected-area governance**.
- **“Restorable” area is smaller and more constrained than mass-planting rhetoric implies.** Global Mangrove Watch–linked estimates used by Wetlands International (2025) put **~24,000 ha** as biophysically restorable in the usual sense. Critically: **most easy low-intertidal planting sites have already been used or misused**. The larger, scientifically preferred opportunity is **mid- to high-intertidal zones occupied by abandoned, undeveloped, or underutilized (AUU) aquaculture ponds** — on the order of **~30,000 ha potentially fully restorable via Ecological Mangrove Restoration (EMR)**, plus another **~30,000 ha** of active ponds where **Associated Mangrove Aquaculture (AMA)** / mangrove-friendly practices may be feasible (Wetlands International Philippines, 2025).
- **Blue carbon value is real but not a youth product.** Mangrove soils store carbon at rates often cited as **~4–5× tropical terrestrial forests per unit area**. SE Asia landscape analyses place the Philippines among the region’s larger mangrove carbon stocks (**roughly ~100–580 MgC/ha** depending on stand type and study). Natural stands typically outperform young mono-plantings for carbon and biodiversity. **Credits require tenure clarity, baselines, additionality, permanence, and community benefit-sharing** — national architecture is only now being built (see §5).

### 2. Success rates: what the science actually says

| Pattern | Reported outcome | Implication |
|--------|------------------|-------------|
| Classic PH long-term planting reviews (Primavera / SEAFDEC line of work) | **~10–20%** long-term survival common for many rehabilitation efforts | “Trees planted” ≠ forest restored |
| Country-level averages cited in synthesis journalism | **&lt;20%** of planted saplings survive on average in the Philippines | Mass numbers overstate impact |
| Mudflat / wrong-elevation plantings (PH–Thailand project surveys) | **~1.4%** average survival on mudflats; **~20–50%** when sited correctly | Site selection dominates outcome |
| Global NGO communication on mass planting | **~80% of mass-planting efforts fail**, especially on heavily altered coasts | Default CSR model is broken |
| Typhoon-exposed *Rhizophora* monocultures (e.g. Bantayan Island post-Haiyan science) | **&gt;95% die-off** in oldest seaward plantations; *Avicennia marina* / *Sonneratia alba* far more resilient on exposed fronts | Wrong species + wrong zone = dead greenbelt marketing |
| EMR / hydrology-first approaches | Higher long-term ecological recovery when tidal connection, elevation, and sediment dynamics are restored; planting is secondary or optional | Process over propagules |

**Mechanisms of failure (repeated across 2000s–2020s evidence):**

1. **Wrong elevation / wrong ecosystem** — planting on **mudflats, seagrass beds, river channels, or dry beaches** that are not degraded mangrove habitat (and are valuable ecosystems in their own right).
2. **Wrong species** — default **“planting by convenience”** with *Rhizophora* propagules (easy to stick in the ground) in **seaward** zones where *Avicennia* / *Sonneratia* belong; nurseries for hard species are skipped.
3. **Monocultures and density theater** — uniform *Rhizophora* blocks for photo ops and hectare claims; poor structural diversity and storm performance.
4. **No hydrology repair** — diked AUU ponds and blocked tidal creeks will not become forest by seedling count alone.
5. **Misaligned incentives** — one-day mass plantings, Guinness-style records, CSR quotas, and short project cycles reward **numbers planted**, not **survival at year 3–5** or **community tenure**.

**Bottom line:** In the Philippines, **mass planting without ecological site design has a long, well-documented failure record**. Survival figures in the teens (or lower on mudflats) are the norm for that model — not rare bad luck.

### 3. Failed mass planting: still happening in the 2020s

Evidence of continued malpractice (not only historical):

- **2023 — DENR regional activities in Bohol and Negros Occidental** drew public criticism for **wrong species / wrong zonation** (e.g. *Rhizophora* where *Sonneratia*/*Avicennia* already indicated the correct zone). Advocates (e.g. Mangrove Matters PH) labeled this **“planting by convenience.”** Consequences flagged: wasted public money, low survival, and **damage to seagrass** when mangroves are forced onto nursery grounds for fish.
- **2024–2025 — Cebu / corporate CSR incidents** (including high-profile airline-linked planting reported on seagrass) drew expert condemnation; Jurgenne Primavera and others framed mangrove-on-seagrass as an **“ecological scandal.”** Narrative analyses (2025) document predictable barnacle stress and seaward *Rhizophora* misuse.
- **Ongoing NGO critique (Wetlands International, Mangrove Action Project, Global Mangrove Alliance PH chapter messaging 2023–2025):** traditional mass planting and inappropriate CSR plantings on **existing mudflats and seagrass** must be **rectified or stopped**; science-based **CBEMR / EMR** should become the standard.
- **IUCN mangrove specialist community “Pause before you plant” position** (widely cited post-2020): **protect first; enable natural regeneration; only plant when diagnostics justify it.**

Mass planting is not only ineffective when mis-sited — it can be **actively harmful** (smothering mudflats/seagrass, wasting community labor, and creating a false sense of coastal “protection”).

### 4. What works instead: EMR, AUU reversion, AMA, community stewardship

**Ecological / Community-Based Ecological Mangrove Restoration (EMR / CBEMR):**

- Restore **tidal hydrology and elevation** first (breach dikes, re-open creeks, fix drainage).
- Rely on **natural recruitment** when parent mangroves and propagule supply exist.
- Use **enrichment planting only** when recruitment is limited — and then with **correct species × zone**.
- Aim for biodiverse, resilient stands, not hectare theater.

**AUU fishpond reversion (largest scalable biophysical opportunity):**

- Philippine law has long required cancellation of **abandoned, undeveloped, and underutilized** Fishpond Lease Agreement (FLA) areas and **reversion to DENR for mangrove rehabilitation** (core anchors: **DAO 15-1990**, related DA–DENR orders, Fisheries Code lineage, BFAR F.A.O. definitions of AUU).
- **Implementation lag is chronic.** Historical Region VI analyses showed **very low cancellation rates** despite widespread lease violations; political economy of lessees, weak monitoring, and bureaucratic handoff DENR↔BFAR remain barriers into the 2020s.
- Civil society (ZSL, Wetlands International, Oceana, local POs) continues to press **immediate AUU inventory + cancellation + biophysical reversion** as the path to a healthier **mangrove:pond ratio** (ideal often cited ~**4:1** mangrove:pond; national ratio has long been far worse).

**Associated Mangrove Aquaculture (AMA):**

- 2020s pilots (e.g. **Bataan / Manila Bay** and **Macajalar Bay, Misamis Oriental** with Wetlands International and partners) integrate mangroves into **active** pond systems: water quality, dike stability, nursery habitat, partial greenbelt restoration without total production loss.
- BFAR interest exists as a possible national sustainable-aquaculture pathway; still early for “scaled” claims.

**Green–gray coastal protection:**

- Hybrid designs (e.g. **breakwater + mangrove greenbelt** examples such as Ajuy, Iloilo in ZSL / NBCAP messaging) are promoted as typhoon-era resilience tools — still site-specific, capital-intensive, and dependent on community ownership.

### 5. Policy and blue carbon architecture (2020–2026)

| Instrument / initiative | What it does | Status / caution |
|------------------------|--------------|------------------|
| **DAO 15-1990** (+ related FLA / AUU rules) | Foundational mangrove protection; AUU cancellation & reversion logic | Law exists; **enforcement and reversion pace remain the gap** |
| **CBFMA + DAO 1998-10 (mangrove CBFM)** | Community tenure (typically **25 + 25 years**) for People’s Organizations | Enables stewardship; **does not authorize mangrove cutting**; capacity and finance vary widely |
| **Global Mangrove Alliance – Philippine Chapter (2023)** | CI, FFP, Rare, WWF, ZSL, Wetlands International et al. align science-based restoration | Useful coalition; not a substitute for tenure reform |
| **PhilSA–DENR National Mangrove Map validation** | Spatial basis for priority conservation / restoration | Planning asset if used to **block mudflat/seagrass planting** |
| **National Coastal Greenbelt Action Plan / ICM Bill / wetland bills** | Would reduce tenurial and bureaucratic friction for greenbelts | **Partly pending / incomplete** as of mid-2020s advocacy |
| **Manila Bay mangrove restoration goals** (provincial plans; multi-thousand-ha targets toward 2040) | Landscape-scale ambition | Ambition ≠ method; risk of repeating mass planting |
| **National Blue Carbon Action Partnership (NBCAP)** | Multi-stakeholder platform (DENR + WEF Blue Carbon Action Partnership; **ZSL secretariat**) kicked off **Feb 2025** | Roadmap / investment coordination; **early-stage** |
| **Mangrove Blue Carbon Roadmap to 2030** (science–policy forums incl. DOST-PCAARRD support, 2025) | Science inputs to institutionalize blue carbon action | Watch for **high-quality principles** vs. volume credits |
| **Mangrove Conference 2026** | National convening to boost blue carbon drive | Signal of political attention, not delivery proof |

**Tenure truth for project design:**

- **Public FLA / forestland ponds** vs **private titled ponds** follow different legal paths; students and short programs **cannot invent reversion**.
- **CBFMA / PO tenure** is the legitimate community handle for long-term care, monitoring, and livelihood rules — when it exists and is functional.
- **Carbon or PES money** without secure tenure and community consent risks elite capture and non-permanence. Philippine PES/blue-carbon literature has flagged **governance and implementation challenges** for years; NBCAP is explicitly trying to fix coordination and quality, not open a free-for-all.

### 6. Youth-scale realistic interventions (what a 5-week / campus / SK-adjacent effort can actually do)

**Do not treat as primary interventions:**

- One-day **mass planting** “challenges” or Guinness-style counts  
- **Mudflat / seagrass / beach** planting  
- Default **all-*Rhizophora*** campaigns  
- **Carbon-credit startups** or “offset our flight” products  
- Claims to **restore hundreds of hectares** without DENR/BFAR/PO tenure and multi-year hydrology works  
- Earthballing / transplant theater as “restoration”

**Realistic, high-integrity youth roles:**

1. **Stop-the-harm advocacy & CSR hygiene**  
   - Campaigns and LGU/DENR engagement against **planting on mudflats and seagrass**.  
   - Public scorecards for corporate and agency plantings (species, zone, survival monitoring).  
   - Amplify “pause before you plant” / CBEMR messaging with local fisher knowledge.

2. **Partner, don’t freestyle biophysical works**  
   - Attach to **existing PO/CBFMA holders**, ZSL, Wetlands International, Oceanus-type field orgs, academe (e.g. SUCs with mangrove programs).  
   - Youth value: labor for **nurseries of correct species**, **plot monitoring**, **community mapping**, **story + data**, not solo “founder restores mangrove.”

3. **Monitoring & evidence products (accelerator-friendly)**  
   - Simple **survival/mortality plots**, photo-point time series, GPS of plantings vs. habitat type.  
   - Document **wrong-site plantings** for local accountability.  
   - Support partners testing **bioacoustics / biodiversity co-benefits** (emerging practice) — as research support, not gadget cosplay.

4. **Tenure- and policy-literate local work**  
   - Community-facing explainers on **AUU FLA cancellation**, CBFMA rights/limits, greenbelt ordinances.  
   - Help POs prepare **monitoring sheets, maps, and LCCAP/coastal plan inputs** — not fake land claims.  
   - SK / youth council resolutions that **prefer EMR and protection** over planting quotas.

5. **Livelihood-adjacent, small-N pilots**  
   - Awareness and co-design support for **mangrove-friendly aquaculture** concepts (AMA) with technical partners.  
   - Nursery enterprises for **Avicennia/Sonneratia** where experts and POs request them.  
   - Eco-education with fishers: storm protection narrative grounded in **species × zone science**, not myths.

6. **If any planting is done at all (strict filters)**  
   - Mid-intertidal **degraded mangrove** or **confirmed post-reversion pond** only.  
   - Expert/PO sign-off on **species, spacing, season, seedling height**.  
   - Pre-committed **2–3 year monitoring** and replacement protocol.  
   - Success metric = **survival and recruitment**, not opening-day headcount.

**Scale honesty:** A youth cohort can change **norms, data, and local policy pressure** faster than it can change **hectares**. National blue-carbon finance and AUU reversion are **state + coalition** problems; YCLAP projects should **enable and discipline** those systems, not simulate them.

### 7. Co-benefits that justify care (without hectare theater)

World Bank–linked valuation work has estimated that Philippine mangroves prevent large annual flood damages (order of **hundreds of thousands of people** and **~US$1B+ property damage** avoided per year in the mid-2010s analysis still widely cited). Post-Haiyan and 2024 multi-typhoon seasons keep coastal protection politically salient. **Protection of standing forests and reversion of AUU ponds** deliver more reliable risk reduction than decorative plantings that die before the next super-typhoon.

## YCLAP caution

1. **Mass mangrove planting is the most common well-intentioned climate action — and one of the most evidence-backed failure modes in PH coastal work.** Default student energy toward “plant mangroves” without zonation, tenure, and hydrology is more likely to **waste effort or harm seagrass/mudflats** than to create blue carbon.

2. **Blue carbon ≠ youth carbon product.** NBCAP (2025–) and the Mangrove Blue Carbon Roadmap are **national coordination and quality** efforts. Do not build YCLAP ventures on selling mangrove credits, airline offsets, or hectare claims. Use blue carbon as **narrative science and policy literacy**, not revenue.

3. **Tenure is the bottleneck.** AUU reversion, CBFMA, FLA politics, and private pond ownership decide what is restorable. A 5-week program **cannot** cancel FLAs or create forest tenure. Work **with** POs and agencies; measure success by **partner-validated outcomes**, not Instagram counts.

4. **Protect & enable regeneration &gt; plant.** Prefer projects that **defend remaining mangroves**, support **EMR partners**, improve **monitoring/accountability**, and **block bad CSR**. Any planting must pass **species × site × tenure × monitoring** filters.

5. **Fit to YCLAP flagship logic (Metro Manila waste / flood / justice):** Mangroves are a **coastal/LGU/partner track**, not a default Ateneo scrap-to-value substitute. If a cohort team chooses mangroves, force **small, partnered, non-credit, non-mass-plant** scopes — or redirect coastal energy into **greenbelt policy campaigns and science communication**.

6. **Avoid greenwashing adjacency.** Corporate one-day plantings, Guinness records, and “neutralize emissions with bakawan” messaging are **red flags**. Critique them; do not replicate them for demo day.

## Confidence

| Claim cluster | Confidence | Notes |
|---------------|------------|-------|
| Mass planting frequently fails; PH survival often ~10–20% or worse on bad sites | **High** | Multiple independent scientific and practitioner lines; consistent 2008–2025 |
| Mudflat/seagrass planting is ecologically wrong and often near-total failure | **High** | Strong ecological consensus + recent PH public cases |
| EMR/hydrology-first and AUU reversion are the right large-scale pathways | **High** on direction; **Medium** on how fast national institutions will deliver |
| Exact national mangrove hectare totals and “restorable ha” figures | **Medium** | Sources range (~285k–311k ha extent; restorable ~24k vs AUU ~30k frames differ by method) |
| NBCAP / Blue Carbon Roadmap will unlock high-integrity finance by 2027 | **Low–Medium** | Kickoff and forums real; delivery, carbon-market integrity, and AUU enforcement unproven |
| Youth can lead hectare-scale restoration in 5 weeks | **Very low (reject)** | Contradicted by tenure, hydrology, and multi-year ecology |
| Youth can add value via monitoring, stop-harm advocacy, PO partnership, policy literacy | **High** | Matches documented gaps (CSR malpractice, weak monitoring, low public zonation literacy) |

**Overall brief confidence:** **Medium–High** for science of failure and intervention hierarchy; **Medium** for 2025–2026 policy delivery trajectories; **High** for YCLAP “do not mass-plant / do not sell credits” caution.
