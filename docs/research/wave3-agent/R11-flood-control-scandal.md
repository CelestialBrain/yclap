# R11 — Philippines flood control corruption scandal (2025–2026)

**Scope:** DPWH flood-control budget, ghost projects, contractor concentration, public reaction, and careful use of the “adaptation integrity” narrative for youth climate work.  
**Window:** Peak public controversy July 2025–mid-2026 (still unfolding as of research date).  
**Method:** Multi-source web research (investigative media, wire services, government statements, scholarly commentary). Not legal findings of guilt for every named party.

---

## Key findings

### 1. Scale: climate-adaptation money at national budget scale

- From **July 2022 to May 2025**, the Marcos administration funded on the order of **~9,855 flood-control projects** totaling about **₱545–546 billion** (roughly **US$11 billion** at then-prevailing rates).
- Flood-control outlays had **more than doubled since ~2015**, turning a core climate-adaptation line into one of the largest recurring public-works pots.
- President Marcos used the **July 28, 2025 SONA** to flag anomalies after typhoon-driven flooding, ordered a full project list, regional validation, public publication, audit, and indictment of those responsible. The government later published an interactive inventory (often cited as ~9,855 completed projects) via **Sumbong sa Pangulo** for citizen reporting.
- **2026 flood-control budget** was cancelled / realigned to other priorities (announced mid-Sept 2025); large tranches of DPWH flood funds were ordered transferred to other agencies. That is a policy shock to the adaptation pipeline, not only a corruption story.

### 2. Ghost projects and quality failures (what “ghost” actually means)

- Joint field validation (DPWH with AFP, DND, and planning agencies) of roughly **8,000** projects reported **at least 421** tagged as **“ghost”** works (Oct 9, 2025, Public Works Sec. Vince Dizon).
- The Independent Commission for Infrastructure (**ICI**, EO 94, Sept 2025) later prioritized about **80** of those for deeper probe—especially projects tied to the **top 15 contractors** and sites hit hard by later storms.
- **Caveat (integrity of the number itself):** Acting DOJ leadership later stated that of the 421 initially tagged, **only a small subset** (reports cited **14**) had so far been **confirmed truly non-existent**; many “ghost” tags may mean unfinished, mislocated, substandard, or documentation-failed rather than pure fiction. Youth narratives should say **“alleged / tagged / under validation,”** not treat 421 as final court truth.
- Documented case types include:
  - **Paid but unbuilt** structures (e.g. ICI flags of multi-tens-of-millions Bulacan projects).
  - **Substandard collapse** (e.g. ~₱100M Lucena riverside works reported crumbling with weak reinforcement).
  - **Ocular confirmation** of non-existence at claimed sites (e.g. Sandiganbayan-related inspection of a Pandi, Bulacan project linked to a malversation case involving former Sen. Bong Revilla and DPWH personnel—prosecutors reported no significant structure where one was claimed).
  - District-level suspensions (e.g. Ombudsman preventive suspension of DPWH Bulacan 1st DEO personnel over hundreds of millions in alleged ghost works).

### 3. Contractor concentration (monopoly pattern, not just “a few bad apples”)

- Marcos publicly flagged that **only 15 of ~2,409 accredited contractors** cornered about **₱100 billion**—roughly **18–20%** of the multi-year flood-mitigation budget—with some firms operating across nearly all regions.
- Investigative data (PCIJ and others) highlight family/corporate clusters:
  - **Discaya-linked firms** (e.g. St. Timothy, Alpha & Omega and related entities): widely reported **~345 projects, ~₱25.2 billion** (2022–2025); luxury-lifestyle coverage amplified public anger.
  - **Sunwest** (associated with then-Rep. Zaldy Co’s business orbit): among top recipients by peso volume (reports of ~₱10B+ on dozens of flood projects).
  - Other recurring names in Senate/House/ICI discourse: Wawao Builders (Bulacan concentration), Topnotch, SYMS, L.R. Tiqui, and others.
- Parallel irregularities alleged in probes: **license renting** (AAA credentials leased to weaker builders), simultaneous multi-company bidding by related firms, and **kickbacks** (Discaya Senate testimony: lawmakers/officials demanding on the order of **~25%** of project cost; senators and ex-officials alleged total “cuts” leaving only **~30–40%** for actual works).
- **Geographic skew:** PCIJ analysis of the open dataset found **uneven allocation**—high flood-risk provinces in **Mindanao** often got fewer projects/pesos than some Luzon areas, undercutting a pure “put money where the hazard is” adaptation logic.

### 4. Political and institutional entanglement

- **Senate Blue Ribbon** (“Philippines Under Water”) and House mega-panels held nationally televised hearings from Aug 2025 onward; witnesses named lawmakers, DPWH engineers, and bagmen. Many named parties **deny** allegations; cases are at various stages (Ombudsman, Sandiganbayan, NBI).
- High-visibility political fallout: resignation of DPWH Sec. **Manuel Bonoan** (Sept 1, 2025); House Speaker **Martin Romualdez** stepped down (Sept 17, 2025) amid kickback allegations he disputes; creation of **ICI**; DOJ immigration lookouts; asset freezes (Marcos cited on the order of **~₱12 billion** in frozen assets of suspects by late Nov 2025); recommended and filed graft/malversation/plunder-related complaints against officials, contractors, and some legislators.
- Structural enablers repeatedly cited by analysts (East Asia Forum, PCIJ, civil society): weakened procurement blacklisting after 2010-era reforms were rolled back; **congressional insertions / “allocables” / unprogrammed appropriations** as pork-like channels; contractor–campaign finance links; restricted public access to SALNs (later eased under a new Ombudsman); weak whistleblower protection.
- **Do not over-collapse:** This is a **cross-faction, multi-year system** story (contractors, district engineers, budget insertions, oversight gaps)—not a clean single-villain morality play. Selective prosecution claims remain part of the political fight into 2026.

### 5. Public reaction: mass anger, church, youth, online “nepo” discourse

- **Street politics:** Waves of anti-corruption mobilizations from Sept 2025 into 2026—**Trillion Peso March**, **Baha sa Luneta / Baha sa EDSA**, church-linked White Ribbon actions, Bonifacio Day and EDSA anniversary rallies. Peak Manila events drew **tens to hundreds of thousands** (wire reports of ~320k+ at some Iglesia ni Cristo–linked / multi-group days; multi-day sequences). Demands: jail corrupt officials and contractors, return stolen funds, transparent investigation (not closed-door only).
- **Moral framing:** CBCP voices (e.g. Cardinal Pablo Virgilio David pre-SONA pastoral critique) and bishops urged prosecutions, restitution, and—explicitly—**young people using social media to expose injustice**.
- **Online culture:** Outrage at luxury displays by contractor families and political “nepo” figures (Discaya car garage, influencer children of contractors) fused corruption with class anger; some accounts went private after backlash.
- **Risk to protest space:** Progressive coverage also documents police violence, arrests, and contested death reports around some Sept 2025 Manila actions—relevant if youth organize, not only if they post.

### 6. Climate / adaptation integrity (why this is a climate story)

- Flood control is **primary climate adaptation infrastructure** in a WorldRisk-top-tier archipelago. When structures are missing, misplaced, or substandard, **extreme rainfall becomes more lethal and costly**—corruption multiplies climate risk rather than offsetting it.
- Analysts explicitly link ghost/substandard works to **waist-high flash floods even outside classic flood plains**, and to post-typhoon blame cycles (e.g. Cebu after severe 2025 storms prompting fresh project lists to ICI).
- Estimated fiscal drain from flood-control corruption: **DOF-linked / media figures of ~₱42–118.5 billion per year** since ~2023 (wide range; treat as order-of-magnitude, not a single audited total). Greenpeace and others float still higher multi-year “lost to corruption” claims—use with attribution and caution.
- Integrity failure also **crowds out** school, health, and community-scale resilience spending (raised in Senate debate comparing flood vs education infra envelopes).
- **Adaptation finance credibility** (domestic and international) is at stake: large adaptation lines that cannot be verified on the ground undermine trust in future climate budgets, NAP implementation, and LGU proposals.

## YCLAP implication (do not overclaim)

### What youth *can* responsibly say

1. **Adaptation without integrity is not resilience.** A fair, youth-safe line: *“Flood budgets only protect people if projects exist, work, and match real hazard maps—not contractor maps.”* Ground that in published project lists, validation counts, and ongoing court/Ombudsman processes—not rumor.
2. **Integrity is a climate co-benefit of local action.** Projects that cut **waste clogging drainage**, improve **barangay early action**, map **actual inundation**, or help LGUs file **auditable** LCCAP/SWM evidence strengthen adaptation *without* claiming to reform DPWH procurement.
3. **Citizen verification culture.** Sumbong-style reporting, photo geotags, open contract data literacy, and “does this structure match the bid?” habits are teachable skills. Position YCLAP as **literacy + local proof**, not as a shadow prosecutor.
4. **Justice framing without partisan branding.** Use systemic language: concentration of contracts, ghost/substandard delivery, budget insertions, weak oversight. Avoid making a YCLAP pitch a **hit list of surnames**—many cases are unadjudicated; defamation and safety risks are real.
5. **Narrative fit with Gargar / waste–flood story.** Public already connects **basura → baha**. Connecting **organic waste diversion** and informal-worker justice to **drainage performance** sits *beside* the scandal without claiming the scandal is “solved” by a student pilot.

### What youth *should not* claim

- That a 5-week student product will **end DPWH corruption**, recover billions, or replace ICI/Ombudsman/Sandiganbayan.
- That **all** of the ₱545B was stolen, or that **all** 421 “ghost” tags are court-proven non-existent works.
- That climate adaptation spending is **always** a scam (that cynicism freezes good LGU and community projects).
- That naming a political faction is a substitute for **measurable local impact** (kg diverted, households reached, flood-day playbooks, verified structures).

### Careful one-liner for decks

> “In 2025–26 Filipinos learned that flood-control pesos can vanish into ghost projects and concentrated contracts—so climate solutions we ship must be **verifiable, community-scale, and honest about what they cannot fix**.”
