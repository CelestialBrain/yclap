# R03 — RA 11898 Extended Producer Responsibility Act: Implementation Status (2023–2026)

**Research date:** 2026-08-07  
**Scope:** Implementation timeline, producer compliance, PRO schemes, plastic packaging recovery rates, informal-collector gaps, latest DENR rules  
**Geography:** Philippines (national; Metro Manila / Pasig relevance for YCLAP)

---

## Key findings

### 1. Legal architecture and diversion targets

- **Republic Act No. 11898** (Extended Producer Responsibility Act of 2022) amended **RA 9003** (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000). It institutionalizes mandatory EPR for **plastic packaging waste** and took effect in 2022 (widely dated July–August 2022).
- **Obliged Enterprises (OEs):** large product producers / brand owners / importers whose total assets (excluding land) **exceed PHP 100 million**. MSMEs are generally not mandatory but may be covered if related entities under the same brand, label, or trademark collectively cross the threshold; voluntary participation is encouraged.
- **Covered packaging:** flexible plastics (sachets, labels, laminates, multi-layer), rigid plastics (containers, caps, lids), plastic bags, and polystyrene.
- **Statutory recovery / diversion targets** (of the plastic packaging footprint generated in the immediately preceding year), per RA 11898 Sec. 44-F:

  | Deadline | Minimum recovery / diversion target |
  |----------|-------------------------------------|
  | 31 Dec 2023 | 20% |
  | 31 Dec 2024 | 40% |
  | 31 Dec 2025 | 50% |
  | 31 Dec 2026 | 60% |
  | 31 Dec 2027 | 70% |
  | 31 Dec 2028 and every year after | 80% |

- Late registrants do **not** restart at 20%: IRR clarifies that programs registered in 2024 start at **40%**, and those registered in 2025 at **50%**.

### 2. Implementation timeline (2023–2026)

| Period | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| **Jan 2023** | **DAO 2023-02** — Implementing Rules and Regulations (EPR IRR) issued by DENR. |
| **Aug 2023** | EPR Registry / **Loop Forward** campaign launch to track compliance. |
| **2023 compliance year** | First mandatory recovery year (20%); phase-in / self-declaration of footprint under IRR. |
| **Apr 2024** | **DAO 2024-04** — Compliance reporting and audit guidelines (ECAR, Independent Third-Party Auditor). |
| **2024–2025** | Registration growth; DENR-EMB outreach and Show Cause Orders to SEC Top 1000 plastic-using firms. |
| **Jan 30, 2026** | **2026 EPR Recognition Program** (DENR-EMB + PARMS): public scoreboard of diversion performance and registered entities. |
| **Apr 2026 (reported)** | **PAB Resolutions No. 01 and 02, Series of 2025** — formalized multi-million-peso fines and procedures for non-registration and program non-compliance. |
| **Mid-2026** | DENR-EMB + **UNDP / NPAP Philippines** drafting guidance to credit **upstream interventions** (reuse/refill, redesign, lightweighting, recycled content) under EPR—beyond pure recovery. Fiscal-incentive **Joint Administrative Order (JAO)** with DOF still under technical work. |

### 3. Producer compliance and recovery performance

- **Registration (late 2025 / Jan 2026 DENR figures):** about **1,017 entities** (OEs, collectives, PROs) across **201 EPR programs** with the National Ecology Center. PCX (June 2026) cites **>1,000** registered businesses (~717 obliged + ~300 voluntary). Earlier PCX/Eco-Business (2024) reported ~947 and first-year recovery of **~163,000 MT**.
- **National diversion vs statutory floors (reported):**
  - **Compliance year 2023:** ~**33.16%** national diversion vs **20%** target (PCX citing DENR-EMB status reporting).
  - **Compliance year 2024 / DENR Recognition 2026 narrative:** ~**55.98%** national diversion vs **40%** mandated floor for the referenced period; **~246 million kg** plastic waste diverted; flexible **56.33%**, rigid **55.60%** via recycling, upcycling, and co-processing.
- **Caveat on “national diversion rate”:** DENR’s public scoreboard reflects **audited recovery/diversion relative to registered OE footprints**, not total Philippine plastic generation or leakage. Country-level mismanagement remains high (e.g. Earth Action / Inquirer 2026: ~1.51 Mt plastic waste projected for 2025 with large mismanaged share). Headline “exceeded target” does **not** mean the country has solved plastic leakage.
- **ECAR cycle:** annual **EPR Compliance Audit Report** within six months after year-end, footprint + diversion verified by an independent third-party auditor (DAO 2024-04).
- **Penalties (law + PAB 2025 resolutions):** failure to register or meet program obligations — progressive fines **PHP 5–10M** (1st), **10–15M** (2nd), **15–20M** (3rd) plus **business-permit suspension** on third offense; shortfall fines can be **twice the cost of recovering the shortfall** if higher. Enforcement is tightening via Show Cause Orders and PAB process.

### 4. PRO schemes and compliance pathways

- OEs may comply **individually**, as a **collective** (lead OE), or through a **Producer Responsibility Organization (PRO)** authorized under the IRR.
- **PRO functions (market practice, e.g. PCX Solutions model):** set internal recovery/audit standards; monitor member reduction/reuse/recycling; consolidate footprints and ECARs; ensure traceable diversion evidence; issue **plastic credit / offset certificates** for member compliance.
- **Downstream compliance tools** recognized in law/IRR include redemption/buy-back, recycling and value-adding diversion, transport to proper facilities, coastal/road clean-ups, investment in recycling/thermal facilities, and **partnership with LGUs, communities, and the informal waste sector**.
- **Offsets / plastic credits** are an explicit compliance route when OEs cannot recover enough volume themselves. Credits must map to verified recovery (recyclers, co-processors, accredited collectors, etc.). This has channeled private money into collection and processing but remains controversial among zero-waste and justice advocates (co-processing, additionality, and who captures credit value).
- Other platforms (e.g. **PARMS** waste-diversion credit marketplace, community networks such as Aling Tindera / HOPE-type models, Plastic Bank–style collection) sit alongside PRO compliance services. Standards and additionality rules are still maturing relative to the statutory climb to 80% by 2028.

### 5. Gaps for informal collectors

- **Structural reliance:** ~100,000+ informal waste workers do much of day-to-day recovery; only ~**39%** of barangays have materials recovery facilities (COA). EPR law **names** partnership with the informal sector as a valid program element, but implementation has been uneven.
- **Exclusion from rule-making:** Waste-worker leaders (e.g. Philippine National Waste Workers Alliance / Mother Earth Foundation advocates) report being **left out of EPR rule discussions** and lacking visibility into the **value of materials sold into plastic-credit markets**.
- **Livelihood and rights gaps:** low/irregular pay, hazardous conditions, limited PPE, local rules that can treat informal picking as unauthorized removal of recyclables, weak formalization of junkshops/aggregators into auditable EPR supply chains.
- **Policy response still pending:** **Magna Carta for Waste Workers** bills filed (e.g. Senate bill after 2024 PNWWA consultation with Sen. Legarda’s office; House counterpart proposals) seeking minimum wage floor, hazard pay, health insurance, training, and policy voice—**not enacted as of latest 2025–2026 public reporting**.
- **Just-transition risk as targets rise (50%→60%→80%):** without fair pricing, formal contracts, and social safeguards, PRO-driven recovery can **extract volume for corporate ECAR scores** while informal workers remain price-takers. Conversely, high-integrity collection partnerships and transparent rates are the main justice pathway inside the current statute.

### 6. Latest DENR / related rules (practical checklist)

1. **DAO 2023-02** — full EPR IRR (definitions of OE/PRO/collective, diversion targets, program content, NEC role, semi-annual rate/cost reporting hooks).
2. **DAO 2024-04** — ECAR format, audit evidence, I3PA requirements for OEs, collectives, and PROs.
3. **PAB Resolutions 01 & 02 (Series of 2025)** — operationalized fines for non-registration and program non-compliance (public reporting April 2026).
4. **Upstream measurement guidance (2026, draft)** — DENR-EMB + UNDP/NPAP methodologies so reuse, redesign, recycled content, and refill can count toward footprint reduction with integrity (anti–double-counting).
5. **Fiscal incentives JAO (DENR–DOF, in process)** — tax incentives under NIRC/SIPP, deductibility of EPR expenses, donation exemptions; industry pushing for clearer treatment of offsets and transition rules.

## YCLAP-Gargar implication

**Gargar’s lane stays justice + transparency, not OE compliance or plastic “neutrality.”**

1. **Do not claim EPR compliance, plastic credits, or plastic neutrality** for the pilot. Campaign canvas already excludes carbon credits and plastic-neutrality offsets; keep that hard line. Gargar metrics = **kg diverted, ₱ rate transparency, verified collectors**—not ECAR tons for a brand.
2. **Informal-collector gap is the product opportunity.** EPR is generating corporate demand for recoverable plastic and PRO-mediated offsets, but workers report opaque prices and weak voice. Gargar’s **reference rates + Pasig collector directory + diversion log** directly address the opacity informal workers and households face—aligned with RA 11898’s “partnership with informal waste sectors” language without becoming a credit broker.
3. **Optional research-only brand conversation (post-pilot):** large OEs under rising 50–60–80% targets need auditable local recovery partners. After the Sept 2026 showcase, a *conversation* with EcoWaste / a junkshop–MRF network about how campus/barangay diversion might *later* feed ethical, worker-fair supply chains is useful—still **not** a credit product.
4. **Watch co-processing / offset narratives.** National diversion headlines (55.98%) mix recycling, upcycling, and co-processing. Mentor-safe pitch: Gargar proves **household ↔ collector** diversion and fair rates; it does not greenwash residual plastic burned in cement kilns.
5. **Local proof > national law story.** RA 11898 is the macro “why producers should care”; Gargar’s demo is micro **basura → value / less residual → less methane and clogged drains** at Ateneo–Pasig scale. Cite EPR only as policy context and justice gap, not as YCLAP deliverable.
