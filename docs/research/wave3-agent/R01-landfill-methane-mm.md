## Title

Metro Manila / Greater Manila landfill methane: Payatas, Rodriguez, San Mateo, Navotas — waste volumes, methane risk, RA 9003 gaps, and 2024–2026 updates

**Context:** YCLAP research wave (Aug 2026). Flagship framing: Gargar scrap pilot + EcoWaste — waste diversion as methane and landfill-load mitigation, **not** carbon-credit products.

---

## Key findings

1. **Metro Manila generates on the order of ~9,500–10,500 tons/day of municipal solid waste (MSW).** NSWMC-linked assessments put NCR at ~9,500 tpd around 2020, rising toward ~10,400 tpd by 2025; secondary market/industry estimates cite ~10,500 tpd for urban Metro Manila. EMB/NSWMC dashboard figures put national annual generation near **~21–22.9 million MT** (early-mid 2020s → 2025 projection), with NCR historically ~22–26% of national load and per-capita rates ~**0.61–0.69 kg/capita/day** in highly urbanized NCR (vs national averages nearer 0.4–0.53 kg).

2. **For years, almost all formal Metro Manila disposal funnelled into three private sanitary landfills (SLFs):** (i) **Rizal Provincial SLF (RPSL / ISWIMS–Green Leap)** in Barangay San Isidro, **Rodriguez, Rizal**; (ii) **New San Mateo SLF**, Rizal; (iii) **Navotas Sanitary Landfill (NSLF)**, ~**40–41 ha** on reclaimed coastal/island land, operated by Philippine Ecology Systems Corporation (Phileco). EMB-era mapping also noted ~**53–55%** of Metro Manila waste reaching those major SLFs, with a large residual fraction poorly disposed or diverted outside official chains.

3. **Navotas closed as an active tip site (franchise/operations halted ~August 2025)** under airport-related land pressure (expropriation interest for New Manila International Airport / SMAI) and franchise expiry. **On 10 April 2026 a major fire ignited at the closed NSLF**, attributed by authorities to **methane buildup + extreme heat**; Philippine Space Agency imagery showed ~**28.6 ha (~71.5%)** burned by mid-April. The blaze lasted ~**one month** (declared extinguished **10 May 2026**), forced evacuations (~500 nearby), caused at least one related death, and drove unhealthy AQI across parts of Metro Manila and Bulacan. DENR-EMB cited incomplete **Safe Closure and Rehabilitation Plan (SCRP)** / soil cover and gas-vent gaps — a direct post-closure methane safety failure.

4. **Rodriguez (RPSL) is the primary northern mega-sink and a repeated disaster site.** Reporting after the **20 February 2026 trash slide** describes a ~**50 ha** private facility receiving **more than 3,000 metric tons/day** from Metro Manila and other LGUs; ~**420,000 m³** of waste collapsed, burying equipment, with at least one confirmed fatality and contested missing-person counts. History includes **six prior collapses**, an **April 2025 fire**, and seasonal leachate floods. DENR suspended Phase 5 (~6 ha) and issued a CDO against the operator (ISWIMS / Green Leap). Methane, slope instability, and overloading are tightly coupled risks at this site.

5. **San Mateo became the political and capacity pressure valve after Navotas shut.** MMDA Chair Artes stated the New San Mateo SLF **ECC allows up to 5,000 tons/day** of Metro Manila waste and that only ~**25%** of Metro Manila garbage was going there (still “below” the ECC cap) when Manila was ordered to divert from Navotas (Aug 2025). San Mateo Mayor Rivera opposed the unconsulted surge, warning of premature fill-out, traffic, and public health risk — classic host-LGU conflict under RA 9003’s devolved but underfunded disposal model.

6. **Payatas (Quezon City) is closed but remains the region’s methane-history case study.** Opened as a dump in the 1970s; **July 2000 landslide** killed ~**200–250+** people (~60,000 m³ slide). Converted to controlled facility; permanent closure **2017** after DENR-EMB ECC review. Historic **landfill-gas (LFG) recovery** (PNOC EC / later private operators; pilot ~100 kW then scale-up toward ~1 MW designs; community power narratives of thousands of households) made QC the first PH city to recover methane for energy — proof that **organics → CH₄ is real and technologically addressable**, but also that energy recovery alone does not fix upstream generation and diversion failures.

7. **Methane estimates: directionally huge, quantification uneven.** A 2024 LEAP-IBC peer-reviewed scenario study (Jao et al., *Sustainability*) models Metro Manila waste-sector methane under BAU as very large and rising (authors report multi-tens-of-Mt-scale figures through mid-century and large % cuts under recovery scenarios; **unit scale is contested relative to global CH₄ budgets and should be treated as model-relative, not inventory-grade**). Older project literature put Metro Manila MSW methane near **~173 Gg CH₄/year** in one C40/competition write-up; national IGES/SLCP work put whole-country MSW CH₄ on the order of **several MtCO₂e**. **Consensus point:** biodegradable MSW in poorly managed landfills is a **high-GWP, near-term climate lever**; fires at Navotas and Rodriguez prove uncollected LFG is also a **public-safety** issue, not only a GHG line item.

8. **RA 9003 (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000) remains strong on paper, weak in delivery ~25 years on.** Mandates segregation at source, barangay/cluster **MRFs**, phase-out of open/controlled dumps, and sanitary landfilling of residuals. Persistent gaps (COA, DENR, GAIA, GIZ/DILG policy briefs, 2025–2026 news):  
   - **MRF coverage:** ~**11,637 MRFs** serving only ~**16,418 of ~42,046 barangays (~39%)** as of early-2020s COA figures;  
   - **SLF access:** national SLF count rose (e.g. **~245 operational / ~478 LGUs (2021)** → **~343–366 SLFs / ~677–748 LGUs by 2024–2025**), still far from universal;  
   - **Financing:** SWM Fund and related incentives poorly operationalized;  
   - **Enforcement:** penalties almost never applied; DENR leadership (Lotilla, RA 9003@25 messaging) and GAIA’s Froilan Grate both emphasize **implementation, not legal text**;  
   - **National SLF share of waste** often cited around **~54%** reaching sanitary landfills, remainder leaking to environment, informal dumps, or waterways — feeding both methane and flood-clog pathways.

9. **2024–2026 crisis sequence (Greater Manila disposal system under stress):** capacity concentration in Rizal north corridor → **Navotas shutdown (2025)** → **Manila/Malabon diversion fights (San Mateo vs Rodriguez)** → **Rodriguez collapse (Feb 2026)** → **Navotas methane fire (Apr–May 2026)** → EMB flags **5 landfills nationwide in “critical condition” / fire risk (Jul 2026)** → continued DENR probes (e.g. San Mateo location vs protected-area concerns, Jun 2026). System is **brittle, landfill-centric, and methane-unsafe at end-of-life**.

10. **Organic / recyclable diversion is the only student-scale lever that simultaneously cuts (a) tons to overloaded SLFs, (b) future CH₄ generation, (c) fire/slide risk drivers, and (d) flood-clog waste — without requiring carbon-market architecture.** End-of-pipe LFG-to-energy helps closed cells but does not replace **source reduction, segregation, MRF function, and scrap value chains**.

## YCLAP-Gargar implication

- **Gargar + EcoWaste should own the narrative “scrap diversion = methane and landfill-load mitigation,”** grounded in MM’s ~10 kt/day disposal crisis and 2025–2026 SLF failures — **not** as a carbon-credit product, Article 6 play, or plastic-neutrality scheme.
- **Design metrics that matter for Ateneo/Pasig pilot:** kg diverted from residual stream (especially organics + high-value scrap), collector income/price transparency, and avoided tip tons — with methane framed as **co-benefit story + climate justice for informal workers**, not as a sellable tonne CO₂e.
- **Policy hook for cohort demos:** RA 9003 MRF/segregation gap (~60% of barangays underserved) and host-LGU conflict (San Mateo/Rodriguez) make **local diversion infrastructure and price-visible junkshop networks** more legitimate than new mega-landfill advocacy.
- **Risk messaging for LEARN/campaign:** Navotas fire (methane) + Rodriguez slide (overloading) = visceral, recent Metro Manila evidence that “basura sa landfills” is climate *and* safety; use official DENR/EMB/MMDA/news facts, avoid inventing site-level CH₄ inventories.
- **Out of scope for flagship:** LFG plant development, carbon finance origination, or WtE incineration politics — those are state/operator scale; YCLAP wins on **upstream diversion + EcoWaste intel + 7-day shippable pilot**.

## Confidence + caveats

| Claim cluster | Confidence | Caveat |
|---------------|------------|--------|
| MM waste ~9.5–10.5 kt/day; national ~21–23 Mt/yr | **High** | Figures span NSWMC/EMB years; always cite year and whether generation vs collected vs disposed. |
| Three-SLF system + Navotas Aug 2025 stop + San Mateo ECC 5,000 tpd | **High** | ECC ceiling ≠ continuous actual throughput; “25% of MM garbage” is MMDA chair statement (Aug 2025). |
| Navotas fire methane cause, ~71% burn, Apr–May 2026 timeline | **High** | BFP/PhilSA/Marcos declaration well covered; criminal liability vs Phileco still evolving. |
| Rodriguez Feb 2026 collapse ~420k m³, >3,000 tpd, ~50 ha | **Medium–high** | Fatality/missing counts conflicted across outlets; operator vs NGO narratives differ. |
| RA 9003 MRF ~39% barangays; SLF access minority of LGUs; weak enforcement | **High** | COA + GIZ + DENR messaging align; exact SLF counts move year-to-year (343–366 range). |
| Absolute Mt CH₄ from LEAP-IBC (Jao 2024) | **Low for inventory use** | Peer-reviewed scenario tool; absolute units appear inconsistent with global CH₄ budgets — use **direction, % reduction scenarios, and co-benefit narrative**, not for credit baselines. Prefer site LFG project docs + national inventory when quantifying. |
| Payatas LFG technical performance today | **Medium–low** | Historic success documented; post-2017 closure operations need fresh LGU verification for any current kW claim. |

**Method note (Aug 2026):** Synthesis from DENR/NSWMC/MMDA/World Bank/COA, peer-reviewed LEAP work, and major PH/international news on 2025–2026 landfill crises. No primary site sampling. For pilot claims, prefer **kg diverted** and documented EcoWaste rates over speculative CH₄ tonnes.

**Bottom line for YCLAP:** Greater Manila’s landfill system is capacity-stressed, methane-hazardous, and RA 9003-under-implemented. **Gargar’s job is diversion and worker-centered scrap economics that shrink residual mass before it becomes landfill CH₄ — not to sell carbon.**
