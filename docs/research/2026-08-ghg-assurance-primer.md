## 2. What assurance work actually is

Verification is the paid act of checking someone else's climate numbers against a published rulebook, then signing your name to the result: does the inventory follow GHG Protocol scope rules, are the activity data real (meter readings, fuel receipts), do the conversion factors match, and is anything material missing? Assurance is graded like exam results (limited vs reasonable) and ends in an opinion letter that investors, regulators, and carbon-market bodies rely on. Consulting is the other side of the same coin — building the inventory, cutting the emissions — and standards bodies forbid one firm from doing both for the same client, which is exactly why the verifier role cannot be faked from the inside.

Auditors are scarce because the job stacks three rare profiles: accounting-grade skepticism, engineering-grade measurement literacy (kWh, tCO₂e, methane yields), and accreditation under schemes that require supervised experience. In-repo evidence of the shortage: at Roadmap writing the Philippines had **no accredited third-party auditors operating in-country** for forest-carbon verification (`docs/research/2026-08-ph-carbon-markets.md:78`), and EPR compliance already requires independent third-party auditors for annual ECARs (`docs/research/wave3-agent/R03-epr-ra11898-status.md:36`,`49`). When 2028 lands, demand for verifiers jumps while local supply stays thin — that gap is the career opening Speaker 3 pointed at.
